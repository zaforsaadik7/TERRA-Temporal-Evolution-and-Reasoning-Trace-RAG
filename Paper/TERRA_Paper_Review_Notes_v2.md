# **Review Notes v2: TERRA — Judicial GraphRAG Paper** 

Revised manuscript (20,000-node corpus) — follow-up review before Q1 submission 

This is a follow-up review of the revised manuscript. The good news: most of the contradictions flagged in the first review round are fixed. The bad news: this pass surfaced a new, more serious data-integrity issue (Section 2.1 below), plus a few reporting gaps that a Q1 reviewer will still catch. 

## **1. What Was Fixed (real progress)** 

- **Corpus size is now consistent.** Abstract, Introduction, Section III, Tables I & II, and the Conclusion all agree on 20,000 nodes / 145,363 edges. The previous draft's split between a 400-node and a 10,009-node corpus is resolved. 

- **Table I sums correctly.** The seven doctrinal categories (5,707 + 4,594 + 4,501 + 1,765 + 1,614 + 1,153 + 666) add up to exactly 20,000. 

- **Derived graph statistics check out.** Density (0.000363) and mean degree (14.54) are correctly computed from N = 20,000, E = 145,363. 

- **Faithfulness, ROUGE-L, latency, and effect-size numbers now agree across the abstract and Tables IV, V, VI, VII, VIII, and IX.** This is a meaningful improvement — the three-way faithfulness disagreement and the stray p-value from the previous draft are both gone. 

- **The 10k-vs-20k comparison in Section VII.B is intentional, not a leftover contradiction.** It's clearly framed as a before/after finding across two corpus scales (vector gate interception rising from 0% to 28%), and is a genuinely interesting empirical observation. 

- **Real methodological additions.** A same-architecture component ablation (Table III), ROUGE-L precision/recall disentanglement (Table V), and an honest discussion of refusal-volume masking a faithfulness metric (Section VII.C.1) all strengthen the paper's rigor. 

## **2. New / Remaining Issues** 

### **2.1 Section IV.C and Fig. 2 appear to be un-recomputed leftovers from the smaller corpus** 

This is the most serious finding in this round. The corpus grew from 10,009 nodes / 37,918 edges (previous draft) to 20,000 nodes / 145,363 edges (this draft) — essentially double the nodes and nearly quadruple the edges. Several topology metrics in Table II correctly changed to reflect this (density: 0.000379 → 0.000363; mean degree: 7.58 → 14.54; clustering coefficient: 0.1634 → 0.2047; WCC and SCC also changed). That's expected and good. 

But the following figures are **identical, to the decimal, to the previous 10,009-node draft** : 

- Graph diameter: 15 hops (both drafts, exactly). 

- Average shortest path length: 4.713 hops (both drafts, to three decimal places). 

- The entire top-5 betweenness-centrality ranking and values in Fig. 2 — Crowell v. Benson (0.0034), Yakus v. United States (0.0029), Simpson v. Shepard (0.0028), U.S. v. Socony-Vacuum Oil Co. (0.0027), and U.S. v. Appalachian Electric Power Co. (0.0026) — are the same cases with the same values to four decimal places. 

- The named high-degree hub examples — “Boyd v. United States (degree 121)” and “Screws v. United States (degree 93)” — are word-for-word and number-for-number identical to the previous draft. 

It is not plausible that doubling the node count and nearly quadrupling the edge count of a real citation graph would leave the diameter, average shortest path length, and every top-5 centrality figure completely unchanged, while density, mean degree, clustering, and WCC/SCC all shifted appropriately in the same table. This strongly suggests Section IV.C, Fig. 2, and the diameter/average-shortest-path rows of Table II were carried over from the earlier draft without being recomputed on the actual 20,000-node graph. 

**Fix:** rerun the full topology/centrality analysis (graph_analytics module) on the current 20,000-node EEG and replace Fig. 2, the diameter, and the average-shortest-path figures with the real output. This needs to be resolved before submission — a reviewer who notices this (and centrality figures are easy to spot-check) will reasonably question whether other “updated” results in the paper were actually rerun. 

### **2.2 Fig. 1's generator model contradicts the methodology text** 

Fig. 1's architecture diagram still labels the “Grounded Generator” as **gemma-4-26b-a4b-it** , but Section III.B.6 states the final answer is generated by **databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct** . This is a direct contradiction between the figure and the text — the figure was not updated when the generator model was changed. 

### **2.3 HippoRAG's headline refusal count (165/350) isn't traceable to any table** 

The abstract, Results, and Discussion all repeat: HippoRAG's higher raw faithfulness comes from “high refusal volume (165/350 vs. 115/350 refusals).” TERRA's 115 checks out exactly (Cat C 75/75 + Cat D 40/75 = 115, per Table VI). But HippoRAG's Category C + D refusals in the same table only sum to 101 (74/75 + 27/75), not 165. The remaining 64 refusals must be coming from Categories A and/or B, but no table anywhere breaks out refusal counts for those categories. 

**Fix:** add a full per-category refusal breakdown for all five pipelines so this headline number (used three times as supporting evidence for the paper's central “dual-metric faithfulness” argument) is actually verifiable from the data shown. 

### **2.4 Table IX claims N = 20 hypothesis tests but only shows 9** 

Table IX's title states “Benjamini-Hochberg FDR Control (N = 20)”, implying the correction was applied across a family of 20 hypothesis tests. Only 9 comparisons are actually listed in the table. The remaining 11 tests that the FDR correction was supposedly computed against are never shown, so the correction can't be independently verified. 

### **2.5 Minor wording / sourcing precision issues** 

- “TERRA's fast path executes in a responsive 3.07s (closely matching Direct LLM and Flat RAG)” — 3.07s vs. Flat RAG's 2.95s is indeed close, but vs. Direct LLM's 4.86s median that's actually 37% faster, not “closely matching.” Reword to avoid overstating the comparison to Direct LLM specifically. 

- The “4.89× operational speedup” and “1.38s fast-path” figures cited in Section VI.C (around the main 350-query Table VI) actually come from the 35-query ablation study (Table III), not from the main benchmark's own measured fast-path latency (Table VIII: 3.19s mean). Make clear which benchmark each latency figure is drawn from — as written it reads as if it's describing the main benchmark. 

- “Zero false refusals on factual lookups” (abstract) is demonstrated at n = 10 in the ablation subset (Table III). The full 100-query Category A of the main benchmark doesn't report an explicit false-refusal count, so the claim's generality to the full-scale benchmark isn't directly evidenced in the data shown. 

- Category B faithfulness for “Full TERRA” differs substantially between the ablation study (0.815, 35-query pilot) and the main benchmark (0.695, Tables IV/VI, 100-query set). This is plausible given the different sample sizes/query sets, but isn't flagged anywhere in the text — a reader skimming both numbers could easily read this as a contradiction. Add a one-line note explaining these are different evaluation sets. 

- “Zero fatal constraint violations” (abstract) is used without a formal definition anywhere in the methodology — define what counts as a “fatal” violation and how it's measured. 

## **3. Is It Suitable for Q1 Now?** 

Closer, but not yet. The statistical apparatus (Wilcoxon tests, Cohen's dz, bootstrap CIs, FDR correction, judge-calibration correlation, an honest limitations section) is now genuinely at the level Q1 reviewers expect, and the internal numeric consistency across the abstract/tables/discussion is dramatically better than the previous draft. 

The blocking issue is Section 2.1: unrecomputed topology figures presented as results from the new, larger corpus are a data-integrity problem, not a cosmetic one. Combined with the two reporting gaps in 2.3 and 2.4 (numbers that are cited as evidence but aren't traceable to any table), a careful Q1 reviewer will ask whether the full results section was actually regenerated end-to-end on the current corpus and evaluation run, or whether other figures were also carried over without being recomputed. That question needs a clean answer before submission.
