\title[TERRA: judicial GraphRAG]{Unifying Event Evolution Graphs and Generic Procedural Scaffolds via Semantic Entailment: A Dual-Phase Architecture for Judicial GraphRAG}
\abstract{Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for historical law must retrieve connected authorities without treating overruled doctrine as current law. We introduce TERRA, an experimental GraphRAG pipeline combining an Event Evolution Graph over \CorpusTotalOpinions\ Supreme Court opinions, generic five-step procedural scaffolds, EASY/HARD routing and LLM-prompted evidence checking. Its scaffolds are not case-specific judicial reasoning. A historical \numUniqueQueries-query, five-system evaluation uses two non-Llama judges and paired tests with Benjamini--Hochberg correction. TERRA's Category~B faithfulness exceeds the retrieval baselines but remains below Direct LLM; its overall and answer-only scores describe different refusal and answered subsets. Adversarial safe handling is \TerraCatDSafeHandlingRate, with \TerraCatDFatalErrors\ fatal errors and false refusals on \TerraCatAFalseRefusals\ Category~A and \TerraCatBFalseRefusals\ Category~B queries. The obscure-precedent advantage depends on curated overruling annotations. A separate prospective comparison evaluates graph retrieval (T) against dense--BM25 retrieval (H) on the same 350 queries with shared evidence budgets, verification, generation and judges. On 100 Category~B pairs, mean evidence faithfulness is 0.76400 for T and 0.49515 for H (paired difference 0.26885; bootstrap 95\% CI [0.16035, 0.37205]; randomization $p\approx0.000010$). T triggers 69/100 verifier refusals versus H's 4/100: the gain concerns the gated pipeline, not demonstrated answer coverage or legal correctness. External and temporal evaluations remain mixed. Historical configuration gaps, annotation dependence and absent licensed-practitioner validation restrict the findings to an experimental research framework.}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:Introduction}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) decouples the parametric memory of Large Language Models (LLMs) from external knowledge bases, enabling factual grounding and source attribution without continuous retraining. In high-stakes settings, where errors and hallucinations carry real consequences, the fidelity of the retrieved context is a strict operational requirement, and naive ``retrieve-then-generate'' pipelines over static vector databases and fixed-length chunks can struggle with conflicting authorities and multi-step evidence.

The bottleneck arises with continuous knowledge drift and multi-step reasoning. Static vector indices can accumulate conflicting, time-stamped versions of the same fact; a retriever ranking by semantic similarity may surface several together, leaving a frozen generator without a basis for choosing the chronologically authoritative one and prone to state outdated law. For multi-step derivations, similarity-based pipelines can retrieve fragments that omit precedential and doctrinal dependencies. We motivate the design with these mechanisms and do not measure them in isolation.

Recent work addresses these bottlenecks in silos. Graph-based retrievers organize facts chronologically but lack the procedural logic to guide multi-hop generation; retrieval of intermediate reasoning traces improves deduction but is temporally blind and mostly confined to mathematics or coding; and corrective Agentic RAG frameworks rely on retrieval evaluators that may not capture conceptual relevance, as a SHAP-based study of the CRAG T5 evaluator found it relies mainly on named-entity alignment rather than semantic similarity \citep{yalavarthi2026opensourcereproductionexplainabilityanalysis}. Many hybrid systems route among a small fixed set of channels, while adaptive multi-strategy routing is emerging \citep{hashmi2026adaptivequeryroutingtierbased}.

We therefore introduce a dual-phase GraphRAG architecture for evolving, knowledge-intensive domains (traversal stability under temporal snapshot expansion is evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:temporal-update-stability}). Offline corpus construction builds a dual-index corpus that cross-links an Event Evolution Graph (EEG), which encodes precedent relationships across \CorpusTotalOpinions\ judicial decisions and \CorpusPrecedentEdges\ directed citation edges, with a node-level generic procedural scaffold, a fixed five-step template instantiated per case. Online, a small language model acts as a Traffic Cop router classifying each query as EASY or HARD, selecting anchor retrieval without graph expansion for EASY queries or citation-graph traversal for HARD queries, which yields a bimodal execution profile (gross median \TerraFastPathMedianSeconds~s on Category~A factual lookups vs.\ \TerraHardPathMedianSeconds~s on Category~B multi-hop chains). Before generation, an LLM-prompted entailment grader decides whether the retrieved evidence suffices; its accuracy was not independently validated.

The historical evaluation compares TERRA with four baselines, including Direct LLM and Flat Vector RAG, on a \numUniqueQueries-query benchmark with a 35-query pilot ablation. We did not test attention dilution or context-length effects directly, and TERRA's mean retrieved context is longer than the retrieval baselines' (about 18{,}400 versus 2{,}900--4{,}100 characters). In a 50-query temporal leakage probe, TERRA did not leak less than Flat Vector RAG under default prompting (the recorded hypothesis $H_1$ is not supported, and Flat RAG refused 23 of 50 queries) but received more automated historical-correctness labels (\TerraGatedHlcrA{} versus \FlatRagHlcrA{}), without expert validation. A separate prospective dense--BM25 comparison holds the surrounding pipeline constant (Section~\ref{subsec:matched-comparison}); its higher T faithfulness coincides with 69/100 versus 4/100 verifier refusals on Category~B, not superior answer coverage. The work is an experimental framework for context fidelity, not deployment validation.

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dual-Index Architecture:} a judicial GraphRAG corpus cross-linking an EEG of \CorpusTotalOpinions\ SCOTUS decisions (\CorpusPrecedentEdges\ citation edges) with a node-level generic procedural scaffold (one five-step template per case, with no case-specific issue, test or holding) and no synthetic cases.
    \item \textbf{Traffic Cop Routing with a Coarse Domain Flag:} an SLM-driven complexity router with a domain-relevance flag derived from a title match or nearest-neighbour proximity ($d_{L2}<1.3$ in the current source). This flag is not itself an early-refusal rule; the canonical evaluation terminated no query before retrieval (Sections~\ref{subsec:computational-budget} and~\ref{subsec:firewall-analysis}).
    \item \textbf{NLI-Based Entailment Evaluation and XAI Attribution:} an LLM-prompted entailment grader that evaluates retrieved evidence and gates generation, with generic procedural scaffolds and citation provenance; its interception reliability is unvalidated; a pilot ablation included five unique adversarial queries.
    \item \textbf{Leakage Safeguards and Audits:} safeguards against temporal precedent inversion, benchmark retrieval contamination and cross-domain feature bleeding, with mixed results in the leakage probe.
    \item \textbf{Historical Empirical Evaluation:} across \numTotalEvals\ evaluations (\numUniqueQueries\ queries $\times$ \numComparativePipelines\ pipelines, $T=0.0$) with paired Wilcoxon tests, Cohen's $d_z$ and 95\% bootstrap CIs, TERRA has the highest Answer-Only Faithfulness among RAG architectures ($\TerraAnswerOnlyFaithfulness$ vs.\ HippoRAG's $\HippoRAGAnswerOnlyFaithfulness$), outperforms Flat Vector RAG ($\Delta = \CatBTerraVsFlatDelta$, $p = \CatBTerraVsFlatWilcoxonP$, $d_z = \CatBTerraVsFlatDz$) and HippoRAG ($\Delta = \CatBTerraVsHippoRAGDelta$, $p = \CatBTerraVsHippoRAGWilcoxonP$, $d_z = \CatBTerraVsHippoRAGDz$) on Category~B, delivers $\TerraCatDSafeHandlingRate$ adversarial safe handling ($\TerraCatDSafeHandling$: \TerraCatDRejection\ refusals, \TerraCatDRefutations\ refutations) with \TerraCatDFatalErrors\ fatal errors vs.\ $\BaselineFatalMin$--$\BaselineFatalMax$ for baselines, and incurs \TerraCatAFalseRefusals\ false refusals on Category~A (\TerraCatBFalseRefusals\ on B). Direct LLM scores higher on Category~B ($\Delta=\CatBTerraVsDirectLLMDelta$), and the Flat margin's bootstrap CI includes zero. Evaluations rely on automated LLM judges without licensed attorneys, making human review an essential prerequisite (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation \citep{lewis2020retrieval, guu2020realm} decoupled parametric memory from external knowledge, but standard RAG struggles with knowledge drift and multi-step reasoning. Graph-based architectures structure retrieved evidence: GraphRAG \citep{edge2024graphrag} builds hierarchical entity-relation communities for global summarization, and Think-on-Graph \citep{sun2024thinkongraph} runs iterative LLM-guided reasoning over knowledge graphs. In temporal domains, Liu and Cao \citep{liu2026raglearningunderstandinglimits} introduced a continuous-knowledge-drift benchmark and Chronos, a time-aware method organizing evidence into an Event Evolution Graph (EEG); Zhang et al. \citep{zhang2025respectingtemporalcausalconsistencyentityevent} proposed E2RAG for temporal-causal consistency across entity-event subgraphs; and Zhu et al. \citep{zhu2025rightanswerrighttime} developed STAR-RAG for time-sensitive rule graph summarization. In law, Chen et al.~\citep{chen2026legalgraphrag} proposed LegalGraphRAG, a Researcher, Auditor and Adjudicator multi-agent system over a hierarchical legal graph, and de Martim~\citep{demartim2025satgraphrag} introduced SAT-Graph RAG to trace diachronic statutory norm versions across constitutional amendments. These systems organize sources by abstraction level or model formal amendment events, whereas common-law systems also require tracking how precedent evolves across citation networks, where earlier decisions remain presumptive authority unless distinguished, limited or overruled; and motivates testing graph-grounded retrieval with explicit evidence checking on historical precedent questions.

To supply that logic, recent work augments retrieval with intermediate reasoning trajectories. CoT-RAG \citep{li2025cotragintegratingchainthought} combines knowledge-graph-guided chain-of-thought generation with retrieval and reports gains of $4.0\%$ to $44.3\%$ across nine public reasoning datasets, and surveys of agentic reasoning \citep{lin2025comprehensivesurveyreinforcementlearningbased, liang2025reasoningrag12} describe pipelines in which agents identify knowledge gaps and generate intermediate traces to inform search. Legal benchmarks include CaseHOLD \citep{zheng2021casehold}, which evaluates holding identification, and CLERC \citep{hou2024clerc}, which evaluates U.S. case retrieval and analysis drafting; as we read them, neither targets superseded or overruled authority, the gap TERRA's evaluation addresses. LegalBench \citep{guha2023legalbench} broadens coverage to 162 tasks across six types of legal reasoning. Earlier legal NLP work includes outcome prediction for Supreme Court cases \citep{katz2017general} and domain-pretrained language models such as LEGAL-BERT \citep{chalkidis2020legalbert}, and an audit of leading commercial legal research tools found that they hallucinated in more than 17\% of queries \citep{magesh2025hallucination}. Trace-retrieval frameworks have been evaluated mainly on quantitative domains or static statutory collections, leaving longitudinal common-law settings largely untested.

Query routers and evaluators form the connective tissue of the pipeline. Hashmi \citep{hashmi2026adaptivequeryroutingtierbased} highlighted the rigidity of single-channel retrieval and introduced an Adaptive Hybrid Retrieval framework, noting that pure vector similarity often fails to capture structural navigation. Yalavarthi \citep{yalavarthi2026opensourcereproductionexplainabilityanalysis} found through SHAP analysis that the T5-based evaluator in Corrective RAG (CRAG) prioritizes named-entity matching over semantic understanding and transfers poorly to scientific questions. Putta et al. \citep{putta2026casefactsbenchmarklegalfactchecking} showed with CaseFacts that unrestricted web search degrades legal fact-checking relative to closed-book baselines. Retrievers ranking by lexical or embedding similarity can return overruled precedents alongside current ones without marking which was overturned, which motivates the explicit entailment check tested below.

TERRA combines temporal precedent management, generic procedural scaffolds and LLM-prompted evidence checking in one experimental pipeline. Its node-level scaffolds repeat a fixed five-step template, rather than providing case-specific legal reasoning. The evaluation tests the combined system and its trade-offs; it does not isolate the scaffolds' effect or establish individual-component novelty. A binary Traffic Cop router selects anchor retrieval or citation-graph traversal without retraining the foundation model.

\section{System Architecture and Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

TERRA is designed as a two-phase, closed-loop GraphRAG architecture for temporal legal reasoning on historical common-law jurisprudence. The core engineering philosophy strictly separates offline corpus ingestion and knowledge structure construction from online query routing and inference. Citation extraction, procedural-scaffold construction and indexing are performed offline; retrieval, HARD-route graph traversal, entailment grading and generation are online operations. The evaluated Category~A and Category~B queries have different latency profiles (median \TerraFastPathMedianSeconds~s on factual lookups vs. \TerraHardPathMedianSeconds~s on multi-hop citation chains; Figure~\ref{fig:system_architecture}).

\subsection{Phase 1: Offline Ingestion and Knowledge Graph Construction}
\label{subsec:phase1-ingestion}

\subsubsection{Authentic Judicial Opinion Corpus (\CorpusTotalOpinions\ Cases)}
TERRA operates on \CorpusTotalOpinions\ full-text Supreme Court opinions spanning \CorpusTemporalSpan\ (Volumes 50--448), curated from an initial harvest of 23,010 public-domain federal decisions from CourtListener and the Harvard Caselaw repositories (public domain under 17 U.S.C. \S~105). To remove non-precedential noise, opinions were filtered by length ($\text{character count} \ge 4{,}000$). Of the \IssueTwentyTotalDecisionsScanned\ decisions in \IssueTwentyTotalVolumesScanned\ scanned U.S. Reports volumes, \IssueTwentySubFiveHundredCharCount\ (\IssueTwentySubFiveHundredCharPct) are shorter than 500 characters (procedural orders, summary dispositions and docket records, subject to audit). The length filter nevertheless induces bias: it excludes \IssueTwentyIntermediateExcludedCount\ intermediate decisions ($500$--$3{,}999$ characters, \IssueTwentyIntermediateExcludedPct), of which \IssueTwentyIntermediatePageRankGeNinetyNineFive\ rank in the top $0.5\%$ of citation authority under Harvard CAP PageRank (e.g., \textit{Haines v. Kerner}, \textit{Dusky v. United States}, \textit{Lucas v. Earl}, \textit{Sipuel v. Board of Regents}, \textit{Gayle v. Browder}). The corpus contains no synthetic cases. A core set of 34 landmark civil rights decisions, from \textit{Dred Scott v. Sandford} (1857) through \textit{Plessy v. Ferguson} (1896), \textit{Missouri ex rel. Gaines v. Canada} (1938) and \textit{Brown v. Board of Education} (1954) to \textit{Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg} (1971), anchors the multi-hop benchmark.

\subsubsection{Citation Parsing and Directed Edge Classification}
Citations are extracted with \texttt{eyecite}~\citep{cushman2021eyecite}, a token-level extraction and reporter-normalization utility that establishes citation existence ($A \xrightarrow{\text{CITES}} B$) without inferring precedential treatment or approval; strings are normalized via \texttt{cite.corrected\_citation()} (e.g., \snippet{347 U.S. 483}) and matched to opinion identifier hashes. The verified Event Evolution Graph has \CorpusPrecedentEdges\ directed edges (\IssueTwentyOneTotalCitesEdges\ \texttt{CITES} and \IssueTwentyOneOverrulesEdges\ \texttt{OVERRULES}), with forward chronological validity at calendar-year granularity and zero cross-year violations (\IssueTwentyOneCrossYearViolationsPct); \IssueTwentyOneSameYearEdges\ edges (\IssueTwentyOneSameYearEdgesPct) link same-year opinions, including \IssueTwentyOneIntraYearReversals\ intra-year reporting artifacts. An earlier ``99.91\% chronological validity'' claim has unresolved provenance (likely a decimal approximation of full-date ordering, $\IssueTwentyOneStrictlyPriorDateEdges/\IssueTwentyOneTotalCitesEdges = {}$\IssueTwentyOneStrictlyPriorDatePct). A reproducible stratified audit across 14 decades ($N=\IssueTwentyOneAuditSampleN$, seed \IssueTwentyOneAuditSeed) confirms verbatim citation presence in \IssueTwentyOneAuditCitationPresentPct\ of sampled edges (95\% CI: \IssueTwentyOneAuditCitationPresentCI), with \IssueTwentyOneAuditTargetConfirmedPct\ verified direct reliance (95\% CI: \IssueTwentyOneAuditTargetConfirmedCI) and \IssueTwentyOneAuditQuotationPct\ citations inside third-party quotations.

The \texttt{OVERRULES} edges are typed relations taken from a Wikipedia mirror of the Congressional Research Service (CRS) report R45319 and resolved to corpus nodes by citation and name matching ($A \xrightarrow{\text{OVERRULES}} B$: $B$ overruled in whole or in part by $A$). We audited all \IssueTwentyOneOverrulesEdges\ tagged edges against the overruling opinions: 3 (\textit{Edelman}$\to$\textit{Wyman-Gordon}, \textit{Griffin}$\to$\textit{Frisbie}, \textit{Hensley}$\to$\textit{Norwood}) were mis-resolved by the first-party-name fallback when the intended target was absent from the 20k corpus, and the other 53 are consistent in case identity, although the scope of each overruling (full or partial) was not legally adjudicated. None of the 3 was serialised in any of the 32 deployed contexts that contained an \texttt{OVERRULES} line, but indirect effects on retrieval or ranking cannot be excluded: replaying the current traversal code on both graphs gave identical outputs for 159 recorded traversals, but the code version used in the runs was not pinned. Historical results retain their recorded index attribution; a corrected derivative without the 3 edges (145,363 edges; SHA256 \texttt{14827bd6...}) is retained as a separately versioned local derivative and was not substituted into the historical runs; the prospective comparison in Section~\ref{subsec:matched-comparison} uses this corrected graph. Public release of the corrected derivative remains pending. The 19 Category-D queries (e.g., \textit{Brown} invalidating \textit{Plessy}) cover 9 distinct \texttt{OVERRULES} edges, all among the 53 consistent ones (Section~\ref{subsec:benchmark-design}). The graph is serialized as a NetworkX \texttt{DiGraph} (\texttt{dataset\_20k\_cap/terra\_eeg\_index\_20k.json}).

\subsubsection{Generic Procedural Scaffold Construction and Vector Serialization}
Each evaluated case is paired with a generic procedural scaffold built from Harvard Caselaw Access Project (CAP) metadata, not a case-specific legal reasoning trace:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Curated Landmark Prototypes ($N=\TraceCuratedCount$):} during early development, \TraceCuratedCount\ landmark decisions received curated traces generated by instruction-tuned models (\texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite} baseline; \texttt{mistral.ministral-3-8b-instruct} variants for benchmark seeds) at $T=0.0$, capped at 150 words and following a five-point schema (constitutional question, governing provisions and standard of review, holding, precedent connection, longitudinal significance). They are not in the evaluated index.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Procedural Substrate ($N=\TraceProceduralCount$, \TraceProceduralPct):} all \TraceProceduralCount\ opinions are serialized by a procedural function (\texttt{build\_structured\_trace}) from the CAP case title (\texttt{name\_abbreviation}), decision year (\texttt{decision\_date}) and a domain phrase. Every node receives the same five-step template (legal question, governing precedent, factual record, harmonizing doctrines, holding), with no case-specific issue, procedural history, test or holding. The domain phrase differs from the node's \texttt{category} field for 18{,}247 of 20{,}000 nodes (19{,}298 read ``Civil Rights Equal Protection'', although 1{,}153 nodes carry that category). The evaluated index supplies generic procedural scaffolds, not LLM-generated case-specific reasoning.
\end{enumerate}

Each vector document serializes the title header, the generic procedural scaffold (under the legacy key \texttt{"Thinking Trace:"}) and an opinion preamble snippet:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Document} = \; & \texttt{\{title\} (\{year\})} \\
& \oplus \; \texttt{"\textbackslash nThinking Trace: \{trace\}"} \\
& \oplus \; \texttt{"\textbackslash nOpinion Snippet:}
\\ & \qquad \texttt{\{text\_snip[:1000]\}"}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:trace_doc}
\end{equation}
All \CorpusTotalOpinions\ documents are embedded with Sentence-Transformers (\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}~\citep{reimers2019sentencetransformers}, \TraceEmbeddingDimensions\ dimensions, ChromaDB's $L_2$ index setting) and indexed in a local ChromaDB \texttt{PersistentClient} (\texttt{dataset\_20k\_cap/vector\_db\_20k}), giving an aligned dual-index architecture.

To check evidence integrity, we audited all \TraceTotalNodes\ nodes. Substring verification against raw CAP casebody JSON gives an aggregate span grounding rate of \TraceSpanGroundingRate\ (\TraceSpanGroundingCount\ exact matches) against the pre-registered $\ge \TraceSpanGroundingThreshold$ floor, with \TraceSpanDiscrepanciesCount\ discrepancies (\TraceSpanDiscrepanciesPct) attributable to short per curiam docket orders lacking opinion text in CAP. A dual-critic factual-consistency and temporal-anachronism audit over an $N=\TraceAuditSampleCount$ cohort (a complete census of the curated landmark prototypes in the graph, $N=\TraceCuratedCensusCount$, with 3 short per curiam orders omitted by CAP's $\ge 4{,}000$-character filter, plus a stratified random sample of the procedural majority, $N=\TraceStratifiedProcCount$ across seven categories) confirmed \TracePopWeightedAnachronismFreePct\ population-weighted temporal integrity (no cited precedent post-dates its target; $\text{decision\_date}(\text{precedent}) \le \text{decision\_date}(\text{target})$) and \TracePopWeightedFactualPct\ factual consistency. These figures do not show case-specific reasoning quality: the generic scaffold combines title, year and a domain phrase, not case-specific legal analysis, and the curated-prototype census cannot be reproduced from the evaluated index. Provenance tags, prompts and ledgers (\texttt{docs/REASONING\_TRACE\_SPECIFICATION.md}) are not yet released.

\subsection{Phase 2: Online Query Routing and Inference Engine}
\label{subsec:phase2-inference}

\subsubsection{Traffic Cop Router and Multi-Tier Domain Boundary Filtering}
Each query $q$ is first classified by the Traffic Cop Router, powered by Google Gemma 3 12B Instruction-tuned (\texttt{\RouterModel}) at $T=0.0$ under structured Pydantic validation (\texttt{RoutingDecision}):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Route}(q) = \begin{cases} 
\text{EASY}, & \begin{gathered}\text{Low Complexity}\\[-2pt]\land\ \text{In-Domain}\end{gathered} \\[6pt]
\text{HARD}, & \begin{gathered}\text{High Complexity}\\[-2pt]\lor\ \text{Out-of-Domain}\end{gathered}
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:routing}
\end{equation}
EASY queries (e.g., decision years or case authors) skip multi-hop expansion and go to the Grounded Fast Path, a $\AblationRouterLatencySlowdownFactor\times$ speedup in the pilot ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) that also avoids context bloat from tangential citing precedents. Evaluated standalone (Section~\ref{subsec:router-standalone-eval}), the router reaches $\RouterAccuracy$ accuracy and $\RouterMacroFOne$ macro-$F_1$ on the \numUniqueQueries-query suite.

To limit hallucination on out-of-domain topics without static allowlists, TERRA applies a three-layer \textbf{Domain Boundary Safety Framework}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1, Lexical Title Matching:} checks whether $q$ contains an exact title of an indexed landmark case from the EEG.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2, Coarse Geometric Relevance Flag:} compares the nearest-neighbour ChromaDB score with an empirical threshold:
    \begin{equation}
    d_{L2}(q, v_{\text{nearest}}) < 1.3
    \label{eq:l2threshold}
    \end{equation}
    For the distance-only calibration, $d_{L2}$ denotes ChromaDB's raw squared-L2 score; values at or above $\tau=1.30$ are flagged, while smaller values are unflagged.
    Embedding proximity does not establish legal validity or domain membership. In an 8 October 2026 diagnostic using the current \CorpusTotalOpinions-case vector index, nearest-neighbour squared-L2 distances for 200 Category A/B query texts had mean 0.712, population standard deviation 0.122 and maximum 1.244. At raw squared-distance threshold $\tau=1.30$, the distance-only rule flagged \TerraGateFPRCountTauOneThreeZero\ in-domain queries (\TerraGateFPRTauOneThreeZero) and \TerraGateTPRCountTauOneThreeZero\ Category C queries (\TerraGateTPRTauOneThreeZero), reproducing the calibration grid in Table~\ref{tab:computational_budget}, Panel B. The diagnostic embedded benchmark query text directly; it did not exercise Title Match or deployed query formatting. This contemporary reproduction does not establish the historical calibration's provenance. In the canonical evaluation, no query was terminated before retrieval.
    \item \textbf{Tier 3, Semantic Entailment Verification:} because proximity cannot verify authority or prevent misapplication, the NLI Smart Grader checks the retrieved subgraph, and if entailment fails across two expansion attempts, generation ends in a deterministic safety refusal. In the canonical run, 81 of the 139 refusals followed an empty retrieved context (74 of the 75 Category~C probes) and 58 a non-empty one (Section~\ref{subsec:firewall-analysis}).
\end{enumerate}

A \textit{fatal constraint violation} is a response that affirms an overruled doctrine, misattributes controlling constitutional authority or adopts a legally impossible premise from an adversarial prompt (Category~D); a \textit{false refusal} is an unwarranted refusal of an in-domain Category~A lookup whose controlling precedent is present and unambiguous.

\subsubsection{Multi-Hop Citation Graph Traversal}
For HARD queries, retrieval combines vector and graph traversal. \textbf{Seed retrieval:} ChromaDB returns the top-$k$ ($k=2$) scaffold-bearing document vectors as seed nodes $V_{\text{seed}}$, used only for coarse seed discovery; precedent discovery then follows citation-graph traversal and holding injection (Section~\ref{subsec:retrieval-quality}), whose observed target-coverage differences do not establish immunity to seeding errors: dense seeding alone reaches $\CatBFlatMacroNodeRecall$ node recall and $\CatBFlatFullPathRecall$ path recovery, whereas two-hop traversal lifts these to $\CatBTerraMacroNodeRecall$ (\TraceTopologicalAbsLift, \TraceTopologicalRelMultiplier) and $\CatBTerraFullPathRecall$ (\TracePathAbsLift, \TracePathRelMultiplier). In the canonical run Category~C showed \CatCTerraCleanRefusals\ clean refusals (74 of 75 probes had an empty context) and Category~D \TerraCatDSafeHandlingRate\ safe handling. \textbf{BFS with hub capping:} from $V_{\text{seed}}$, BFS traverses predecessors and successors to depth $d=2$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}(q) = V_{\text{seed}} \cup \bigcup_{v \in V_{\text{seed}}} \text{BFS}(v, G_{\text{EEG}}, d=2)
\label{eq:bfs}
\end{equation}
Hub expansion is capped (degree $>50$) to prevent super-hub flooding, and node summaries and trajectories are compiled chronologically into the evidence block $\mathcal{C}(q)$.

\subsubsection{NLI Smart Grader and Self-Correction Expansion Loop}
\label{subsubsec:terra-nli-grader}
Before generation, $\mathcal{C}(q)$ is evaluated by the NLI Smart Grader, a structured-prompt call to \texttt{databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct}~\citep{dubey2024llama3} ($T=0.0$) given the query and context and instructed to return JSON with \texttt{entails} (boolean), \texttt{confidence\_score} (float) and \texttt{reasoning} (string). This allows single-call verification without a separate NLI cross-encoder (e.g., DeBERTa~\citep{he2023debertav3}), while routing stays with the 12B Gemma SLM. No labeled validation set was built for the verifier; the only empirical evidence of its role is the pilot ablation, in which removing the gate (Ablation~2, Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) lowers adversarial safety rejection from \AblationFullCatDSafetyRate\ to \AblationTwoCatDSafetyRate, which is consistent with the gate contributing to safety but not a validation of its accuracy. False non-entailment (supported context rejected) and false entailment (insufficient context accepted) are unquantified failure modes (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}). If \texttt{entails == True}, the context goes to the generator. If \texttt{entails == False}, a \textbf{Self-Correction Expansion Loop} widens retrieval ($k \leftarrow k + 2$), re-queries ChromaDB and re-runs BFS. If entailment still fails after two expansions, the \textbf{Safety Refusal Firewall} halts generation with a deterministic refusal: \textit{``I apologize, but I do not have sufficient validated legal context in my databases to answer this query accurately without risking hallucination.''}

\subsubsection{Data Leakage Mitigation Framework}
\label{subsubsec:data-leakage}
TERRA applies four safeguards against leakage. (1)~\textbf{Temporal directionality:} an earlier case cannot cite later jurisprudence, so forward \texttt{CITES} edges require $t(v_{\text{cite}}) \ge t(v_{\text{cited}})$, and retrospective invalidation uses CRS-derived \texttt{OVERRULES} relations (Section~\ref{subsec:phase1-ingestion}) rather than unrestricted backward edges, providing a temporal constraint without guaranteeing historically correct answers. (2)~\textbf{Vector-store isolation:} benchmark queries, rubrics and reference answers are segregated from the index, which holds only opinion text and generic procedural scaffolds. (3)~\textbf{Cross-domain bleed prevention:} title matching and the proximity flag (which terminated no query in the canonical run) are coarse first-line signals, and NLI verification is designed to suppress non-entailed subgraphs before synthesis. (4)~\textbf{Temporal parametric containment:} to limit post-$T$ doctrine entering historical answers, traversals and retrieved corpora are bounded by provenance timestamps ($\text{KnowledgeProvenance} \le T$), and candidate generations $\hat{y}_{\text{candidate}}$ pass an entailment gate against contemporaneous evidence before release. In the leakage probe (Section~\ref{subsec:temporal-leakage}) the gate made six interventions, but its bulk leakage reduction was not statistically established.

\subsubsection{Explainable AI (XAI) and Topological Provenance}
\label{subsubsec:xai-provenance}
TERRA provides four explainability layers: (1)~\textbf{graph traversal provenance}, a recorded retrieval path $\mathcal{P} = \langle v_1, e_1, v_2, \dots, v_k \rangle$ through cited cases, which does not certify the doctrinal support of each answer; (2)~\textbf{hyperlinked attribution}, in which cited cases with indexed identifiers become relative markdown links (e.g., \texttt{[Brown v. Board of Education](/case/347\_0483-01)}); across benchmark completions TERRA emitted \TerraTotalMarkdownLinks\ links with a \TerraMarkdownLinkResolutionRate\ resolution rate (\TerraResolvedMarkdownLinks/\TerraTotalMarkdownLinks) and \TerraMarkdownLinkFidelityRate\ title fidelity (Section~\ref{subsec:citation-correctness}, Table~\ref{tab:citation_eval}), while the baselines emit none; (3)~\textbf{entailment transparency}, structured justifications (\texttt{faithfulness\_reasoning}, \texttt{confidence\_score}); and (4)~\textbf{routing transparency}, reasoning strings for the EASY or HARD decision.

\subsubsection{Grounded Answer Generation}
When context passes NLI grading, \texttt{databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct}~\citep{dubey2024llama3} generates the answer under a strict grounding instruction with greedy decoding:

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

This section describes the benchmark suite, baselines and evaluation metrics.

\subsection{Benchmark Suite Design}
\label{subsec:benchmark-design}
The evaluation suite comprises \numUniqueQueries\ unique queries evaluated across \numComparativePipelines\ pipelines (\numTotalEvals\ evaluations; dates and model identifiers are given under Multi-Run Provenance below), plus a foundational $N=\numPilotQueries$ pilot ablation set (\numPilotRepeatedRuns\ repeated trials per query, \numPilotEvals\ executions across four configurations), across four categories isolating factual accuracy, multi-hop reasoning, domain safety and adversarial robustness. Temperature $T{=}0.0$ was used throughout but does not guarantee identical outputs across runs (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}). Faithfulness scores cover all four categories, including refusals; ROUGE-L evaluates generative Categories~A and~B, and safety labels evaluate Categories~C and~D:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Category A --- Single-Point Factual:} single-fact queries (decision years, party names) scored against researcher-constructed reference answers with ROUGE-L.
    \item \textbf{Category B --- Multi-Hop Evolutionary:} multi-decade precedent-tracking queries scored against researcher-constructed reference answers with ROUGE-L. To test non-trivial traversal while reflecting the hub topology of citation networks, all chains span 3--5 nodes (2--4 hops): 63 three-node, 32 four-node and 5 five-node arcs, with no 2-node pairs. Category~B gold paths derive from the EEG's own citations (circularity risk), and Categories~A and~B use three and one question templates.
    \item \textbf{Category C --- Out-of-Context Safety:} 75 queries ($N=15$ each) on topics absent from indexed Supreme Court law (\CorpusTemporalSpan): natural sciences, foreign and treaty law, state and municipal ordinances, post-2020 software and culture, and corporate accounting. With no controlling precedent, the required behavior is refusal.
    \item \textbf{Category D --- Adversarial Deception:} queries pairing real in-domain case titles with fabricated premises or temporal falsehoods (e.g., governing the 1905 \textit{Lochner} ruling by the 1938 Fair Labor Standards Act), built to trick entity-matching routers. A safe response is an explicit refusal or a verified refutation of the false premise citing authentic authority.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Task Scope vs.\ Background Corpus.} The EEG covers the full \CorpusTotalOpinions-case docket across seven areas (including \CorpusPrivateLawOpinions\ Substantive Private Law and \CorpusGeneralConstitutionalOpinions\ General Statutory decisions; Table~\ref{tab:corpus_stats}), but Category~B concentrates on constitutional and federal public-law trajectories (20 queries each across Civil Rights, Criminal Procedure, First Amendment, Commerce Clause and Administrative Law; \IssueTwentyFiveCatBConstitutionalPublicPct). Of the \IssueTwentyFiveBenchInDomainTotal\ in-domain queries (Categories~A, B and D), \IssueTwentyFiveBenchConstitutionalPublicCount\ (\IssueTwentyFiveBenchConstitutionalPublicPct) are constitutional or public law, \IssueTwentyFiveBenchPrivateLawCount\ (\IssueTwentyFiveBenchPrivateLawPct) private law and \IssueTwentyFiveBenchGeneralStatutoryCount\ (\IssueTwentyFiveBenchGeneralStatutoryPct) general statutory. Private-law disputes were not modeled as 3--5 node citation chains, whereas constitutional jurisprudence shows longitudinal evolution and explicit overruling; retaining the full repository gives a high-distractor environment where constitutional reasoning must avoid \IssueTwentyFiveCorpusCombinedPrivateStatutoryPct\ commercial and statutory background nodes.

\noindent\textbf{Category~C and D Construction.} The 75 Category~C queries were pre-registered across five domains (Subtypes C1--C5) before evaluation, and an automated audit against the indexed repository found 0/75 title, citation or holding overlaps. The 75 Category~D queries were built by the research team under a pre-registered taxonomy: D1 ($N=19$) explicit overruled-precedent traps, D2 ($N=19$) title substitution, D3 ($N=19$) temporal inversion with a $\ge 5$-year gap, and D4 ($N=18$) procedural-posture deceptions, sampled with \texttt{RANDOM\_SEED = 42} from 19 verified doctrine-invalidation pairs. All queries were locked into \texttt{terra\_350\_benchmark.json} (SHA256: \texttt{8144fa81...}) before any pipeline ran, with no queries or references indexed into ChromaDB (Section~\ref{subsubsec:data-leakage}); the domain-labeling protocol and Category~C catalog are identified in the local supplementary documentation; public availability is not certified here. A standalone 15-query stress test (10 out-of-context, 5 adversarial; \texttt{stress\_test.py}) checks firewall boundary consistency.

\enlargethispage{3\baselineskip}
\paragraph{Obscure Overruled Precedent Suite.} To test whether Category~D safety reflects retrieval or memorization of famous landmarks, we built a supplementary suite of \ObscCohortSize\ obscure overruled-precedent queries from the 47 CRS \texttt{OVERRULES} edges not used in Category~D, excluding three whose overruling opinions never mention the target (we infer, without a retained exclusion ledger, that these are the three mis-resolved edges of Section~\ref{subsec:phase1-ingestion}) and selecting the thirty lowest-in-degree pairs by a mechanical rule fixed before inference. Each query asks what the overruled decision held without hinting at the overruling authority (leakage audited). Four arms ran under identical settings: Direct LLM; Flat Vector RAG with the identical instruction block; Full TERRA; and TERRA-Unlabeled, which removes the relation tags, the overruling heading, the prompt's reference to the tag and the enlarged excerpt allocation for overruling edges while keeping seeds, traversal and router unchanged. Two judges (Qwen3-Next-80B, GPT-OSS-120B; neither is the generator) classified each answer as refusal, verified refutation, fatal error (affirmed as current law, or recited without warning) or hedge, and the combined class takes the more severe judgment. The primary hypothesis, sensitivity subsets and cross-suite contrast were preregistered before any live output; for the contrast we re-ran the nineteen Category~D doctrine-invalidation queries under identical code, prompts and judges.

\subsection{Comparative Baselines}
\label{subsec:baselines}
TERRA is benchmarked against four representative baseline architectures spanning parametric and retrieval-augmented paradigms: (1)~\textbf{Direct LLM (No RAG)}, where query $q$ is passed directly to the generator without retrieval, evaluating raw parametric knowledge and un-gated hallucination risks; (2)~\textbf{Flat Vector RAG}, where ChromaDB retrieves top-$k$ scaffold-bearing opinion documents by semantic embedding similarity ($n_{\text{results}}=2$) and passes them directly to the generator without citation graph traversal or NLI entailment grading; (3)~\textbf{GraphRAG-Adapted (Louvain implementation)}, adapting Microsoft GraphRAG \citep{edge2024graphrag} by partitioning opinion entities into Louvain graph communities ($Q=\IssueTwentyThreeGraphRAGModularity$) and generating responses from local subgraph plus parent community summaries, omitting directed chronological precedent edges; and (4)~\textbf{HippoRAG-Adapted}, adapting hippocampal memory indexing \citep{bernal2024hipporag} by constructing a symmetric bipartite citation graph (\IssueTwentyThreeHippoRAGNonZeroEntries\ non-zero entries) and executing Personalized PageRank (PPR) associative diffusion seeded from query entity mentions, without typed directional citation constraints or downstream NLI self-correction gates.

\noindent\textbf{Baseline Implementation Details and Adaptation Rigor.} All five historical pipelines requested the generator endpoint \texttt{databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct} ($T=0.0$, max tokens~4,096), programmatic payload assertions (identical model, temperature, token limit) and the 350-query benchmark \texttt{terra\_350\_benchmark.json} (SHA256: \texttt{8144fa81...}) over the 20,000-case Harvard CAP corpus. The historical corpus record identifies index \texttt{terra\_eeg\_index\_20k.json} (SHA256: \texttt{\IssueTwentyThreeHistoricalCorpusHashShort}, \IssueTwentyThreeHistoricalEdges\ edges, \IssueTwentyThreeHistoricalFileSizeMB~MB), whereas the active post-Issue~\#6 index is SHA256 \texttt{\IssueTwentyThreeActiveCorpusHashShort} (\IssueTwentyThreeActiveEdges\ edges, \IssueTwentyThreeActiveFileSizeMB~MB), satisfying $\IssueTwentyThreeHistoricalEdges + \IssueTwentyThreeCrsAddedEdges = \IssueTwentyThreeActiveEdges$ (\IssueTwentyThreeActiveCites\ \texttt{CITES}, \IssueTwentyThreeActiveOverrules\ \texttt{OVERRULES}; \IssueTwentyThreeReclassifiedEdges\ historical \texttt{CITES} edges were retyped as \texttt{OVERRULES}, which preserves the count, and 3 edges were CRS-added, all 3 of which the audit found mis-resolved; without them the graph has 145,363 edges, 145,310 \texttt{CITES} and 53 \texttt{OVERRULES}). Of the 20,000 opinions, \IssueTwentyThreeSubstantiveHoldingsCount\ have substantive extracted holdings (\IssueTwentyThreeSubstantiveHoldingsPct; \IssueTwentyThreeTierOneHeadnotesCount\ syllabus headnotes, \IssueTwentyThreeTierTwoRegexCount\ regex statements), \IssueTwentyThreeTierThreeFallbackCount\ (\IssueTwentyThreeTierThreeFallbackPct) rely on preamble fallbacks, and \IssueTwentyThreePopulatedHoldingCount\ holding fields are non-empty (\IssueTwentyThreePopulatedHoldingPct; \IssueTwentyThreeUnpopulatedHoldingCount\ unpopulated). Graph representations differ by architecture: GraphRAG-Adapted partitions an undirected citation network (\IssueTwentyThreeGraphRAGUndirectedEdges\ edges) by NetworkX Louvain modularity ($Q=\IssueTwentyThreeGraphRAGModularity$, resolution 1.0, seed 42; chosen over C-based Leiden for host build constraints); HippoRAG-Adapted runs Personalized PageRank ($\alpha=0.85$, tolerance $10^{-6}$, max 50 iterations, top-$k=5$) over a symmetric citation matrix (\IssueTwentyThreeHippoRAGNonZeroEntries\ entries); and TERRA traverses the directed chronological graph by BFS ($k=2$, $d=2$, hub cap 50); the current source separately uses a strict $d_{L2}<1.3$ proximity check in its domain-relevance helper, with a title-match alternative. Context ceilings are character-based (\texttt{[:N]}): Flat RAG $\approx$\IssueTwentyThreeFlatCharCeiling, GraphRAG $\approx$\IssueTwentyThreeGraphRAGCharCeiling, HippoRAG $\approx$\IssueTwentyThreeHippoRAGCharCeiling\ and TERRA \IssueTwentyThreeTerraCharCeiling\ characters, an intentional $\IssueTwentyThreeContextAsymmetryRatio$ ceiling-to-ceiling asymmetry (\IssueTwentyThreePromptTokenRatio\ prompt tokens, Table~\ref{tab:computational_budget}); realised mean context was $18{,}404$ characters for TERRA versus $3{,}141$ for Flat RAG ($5.9\times$), a confound. The judging prompt applies a \IssueTwentyThreeJudgeMiddleOmissionCeiling-character middle-omission split (\texttt{[:25000]} + \texttt{[-25000:]}), covering 100\% of baseline context ($<6{,}000$ characters) while preserving TERRA's initial seeds and terminal citing authorities. Finally, the baseline adapters include post-hoc amendments rather than frozen pre-registration: Amendments~F and G (HippoRAG regex and GraphRAG formatting fixes) and K, L and M (judge JSON parse clamping, refusal string harmonization, fast-path ground-truth floor).

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:eval-metrics}
System performance is evaluated across five dimensions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Faithfulness ($0.0$--$1.0$):} the mean of two LLM judges \citep{zheng2023judging}, \texttt{databricks-qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct} (Judge~1) and \texttt{databricks-gpt-oss-120b} (Judge~2), each scoring a five-point Likert rubric mapped to $(L-1)/4$. Neither is the Llama-3.3-70B model that serves as generator, NLI grader and Category~D verifier.
    \item \textbf{Relevance ($0.0$--$1.0$):} scored by the two judges for query-answer directness and structural completeness.
    \item \textbf{ROUGE-L F1 and Recall (supplementary):} computed for Categories~A and~B against researcher-constructed reference answers by deterministic tokenized LCS matching, an LLM-independent lexical check. Reference claims were cross-referenced against public sources (Cornell LII, Oyez, CRS \textit{Constitution Annotated}) without independent legal certification. Exact n-gram matching cannot distinguish active doctrine from overruled precedent, penalizes paraphrase and detailed analysis via candidate length, and scores refusals as zero; the modest judge--ROUGE correlation ($r=\JudgePearsonR$; Section~\ref{subsec:validation-methodology}) does not calibrate either metric to legal correctness, so primary conclusions rest on dual-judge faithfulness, citation-graph recovery and safety rates.
    \item \textbf{Safety Rejection and Robustness Rate (\%):} the share of Category~C and~D queries correctly neutralized. For Category~D we separate the Explicit Safety Refusal Rate ($R_{\text{ref}}$), the Verified Substantive Refutation Rate ($R_{\text{refut}}$, refuting the false premise without fatal error), the Total Safe Handling Rate ($R_{\text{safe}}=R_{\text{ref}}\cup R_{\text{refut}}$), the Fatal Error Rate ($R_{\text{fatal}}$, validating false or overruled doctrine) and the Uncommitted Hedge Rate ($R_{\text{hedge}}$).
    \item \textbf{Stage Latency (ms):} mean $\pm$ SD time for routing, retrieval, NLI grading and generation across all runs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Validation Methodology and Component Ablation Design}
\label{subsec:validation-methodology}
The protocol incorporates four validation controls:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Judge Calibration:} we correlate the judges' faithfulness with researcher-constructed ROUGE-L across in-domain generations (Categories~A and~B). To handle repeated evaluations within queries, the primary analysis uses whole-query cluster bootstrap and query-cluster permutation over $N_q=\JudgeCalibN$ clusters ($N_{\text{obs}}=\JudgeCalibNObs$ valid pairs): Pearson $r=\JudgePearsonR$ (95\% bootstrap CI $\JudgePearsonCI$; permutation $p=\frac{k+1}{P+1}=\JudgePearsonP$ with $P=10{,}000$ and $k=0$ exceedances; Spearman $\rho=\JudgeSpearmanRho, p=\JudgeSpearmanP$; Kendall $\tau=\JudgeKendallTau, p=\JudgeKendallP$). Collapsing to query means changes the estimand to between-query variation, giving $r_{\text{mean}}=\JudgeMeanPearsonR$ (Fisher 95\% CI $\JudgeMeanPearsonCI$, $p=\JudgeMeanPearsonP$). Historical figures ($r=\HistoricalJudgePearsonR$, pilot $r=\HistoricalPilotJudgePearsonR$) were hard-coded without machine-readable provenance and failed reproduction across 384 candidate preprocessing configurations; the reported estimates come from the corrected canonical analysis. On the 35-query pilot ($N=525$ runs over 3 pipelines and 5 repeats; $N=260$ valid in-domain pairs over 20 query clusters) $r=\PilotJudgePearsonR$ ($p=\PilotJudgePearsonP$). The relationship is modest but significant, too weak to treat the judges as a substitute for human legal verification.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Run Provenance:} dates are file-modification times or logged runner headers. Baseline generations ($1{,}400$, \texttt{llama-3.3-70b-instruct}, no router or NLI) are associated with file dates of 5--6 September 2026; the deployed TERRA run ($350$; router \texttt{gemma-3-12b}, NLI grader and generator \texttt{llama-3.3-70b-instruct}) and the dual-judge scoring of all $1{,}750$ answers (judges \texttt{qwen3-next-80b} and \texttt{gpt-oss-120b}) have file dates of 14 September 2026, with four concurrent workers (36 of 350 queries hit HTTP~429, 620\,s of server-imposed sleep). All calls used $T{=}0.0$, which does not guarantee identical outputs on Databricks Model Serving: a second same-day TERRA run gave identical answers on $\TerraIdenticalAnswerFraction$ of queries overall ($\TerraIdenticalAnswerCatA$ on Category~A, $\TerraIdenticalAnswerCatB$ on B, $\TerraIdenticalAnswerCatC$ on C, $\TerraIdenticalAnswerCatD$ on D); the earlier records forced 241 queries to HARD, so recorded execution paths differed; the precise cause of the Category~B text differences is not isolated. The stored pilot ablation records identify a single Llama-3.3-70B judge, unlike the canonical dual-judge protocol; their scores are not directly comparable, and the historical execution date remains unestablished. CaseFacts, CLERC and the obscure suite used the same two judges (identifiers recorded in every raw row for CaseFacts and CLERC); the temporal probe (file date 17 September) records both judge identifiers: \texttt{databricks-qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct} and \texttt{databricks-gpt-oss-120b}. The Category~D certification (file date 20 September, $N=75$) used the Llama-3.3-70B semantic verifier, CaseFacts and CLERC have file dates of 18 September ($N=170$ each), and the obscure suite and famous contrast have file dates of 19--20 September ($N=120$ and $76$). These four use task-specific rubrics, so their scores are not on the 1,750-run faithfulness scale, and the dates of the first three are file-modification times.

Recorded provenance varies by experiment. The obscure-suite and famous-contrast records contain an engine hash matching the preserved Issue~18 source (SHA256 \texttt{430fc1b3...}), whose parsed Python syntax matches the inspected root source (\texttt{0cca5cf4...}); only a comment differs. Their records also contain vector-index fingerprints. Temporal-probe prompt, context and candidate hashes identify payloads, not the generating code or graph version. The obscure/famous arms requested the same generator endpoint, but direct-call logs record a server response identifier whereas the engine wrapper records the requested identifier; backend weight invariance is not established. Supplementary Table~\ref{supp-tab:run_provenance} summarizes these boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Component-Level Ablation Matrix:} three same-architecture ablations accompany \textbf{Full TERRA} (Router + EEG Traversal + NLI Smart Grader): \textbf{Ablation~1} replaces graph traversal with Flat Vector RAG while keeping NLI grading; \textbf{Ablation~2} disables entailment verification and self-correction; and \textbf{Ablation~3} forces all queries through the full traversal path.
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameter Freezing and Benchmark Isolation:} retrieval and graph parameters ($k=\IssueTwentyFourSeedK$, $d=\IssueTwentyFourBfsDepth$, hub degree threshold $\IssueTwentyFourHubCap$, $\tau=\IssueTwentyFourDistThreshold$) were frozen before benchmark construction on the basis of topological constraints and feasibility testing on an independent $N=\IssueTwentyFourDevQueryCount$-query pilot (\IssueTwentyFourDevDate; \IssueTwentyFourDevTotalEvals\ run records over \IssueTwentyFourDevPipelineCount\ pipelines and \IssueTwentyFourDevRunCount\ stability runs). An automated audit verifies chronological precedence, zero query ID and prompt-text overlap (\IssueTwentyFourOverlapPct), and no query-text or reference-answer contamination in ChromaDB across all \IssueTwentyFourTestQueryCount\ queries. In the recovered engine, degree greater than 50 activates high-degree handling; 75 incoming and 25 outgoing neighbors are directional selection budgets, subject to limits of 40 visited nodes and 35{,}000 structural-context characters. These controls describe the recovered source; they do not establish the exact settings of every historical experiment.
\end{enumerate}

N-gram overlap cannot separate valid modern doctrine from overruled precedent, since it rewards verbatim citation of dead law; TERRA's strengths lie in verified citation-graph recovery (Table~\ref{tab:retrieval_eval}) and dual-judge faithfulness ($\TerraCatBFaithfulness$, FDR $q < 0.05$).

\subsection{Temporal Parametric Leakage Stress Test}
\label{subsec:temporal-leakage}

Models pretrained on post-$T$ legal history risk \textit{temporal parametric leakage}, anachronistically importing later doctrinal shifts or overrulings into analyses framed at cutoff $T$. We ran an exploratory 50-query probe across five horizons ($T \in \{1890, 1930, 1950, 1970, 1980\}$), spanning seven doctrinal domains and balanced across case prominence and reasoning depth. Six probes (12\%) target cases decided between 1857 and 1905 (cutoffs $T=1890$ and $1930$) whose nodes have zero out-edges in the EEG owing to the citation-parsing boundary; their arcs are verified against primary sources but are not EEG edges, and all metrics are computed identically across the 50 probes.

\subsubsection{Design, Metrics and Evaluator Reliability}
The probe evaluates \TemporalTotalEvaluations\ responses from Direct LLM, Flat Vector RAG, $\text{TERRA}_{\text{ungated}}$ ($L_{\text{gen}}$) and $\text{TERRA}_{\text{gated}}$ ($L_{\text{final}}$), each under \textbf{Condition A} (standard prompt) and \textbf{Condition B} (explicit instruction to adhere to year $T$), under a recorded 3-tier rubric: Level~0 (clean), Level~1 (non-prejudicial mention or disclaimer) and Level~2 (fatal reliance on post-$T$ law). The primary metric is the \textbf{Substantive Post-$T$ Leakage Rate (SPTLR)}, the share of Level~2 failures. Because refusing systems generate no post-$T$ text and look ``leak-free'', we decouple containment from problem-solving with the \textbf{Refusal Rate} and the \textbf{Historical Legal Correctness Rate (HLCR)}, an automated rubric label for whether the system states the operative rule as of $T$, not expert-certified correctness, and we measure the \textbf{NLI Gate Leakage Interception Rate (NGLIR)}, the share of ungated leaking candidates intercepted by the gate (denominator $D$).

Responses were judged by \texttt{qwen3-next-80b} and \texttt{gpt-oss-120b}. Over $N=\TemporalValidPairs$ complete pairs the judges reached $\TemporalRawConcordance$ raw concordance and $100\%$ monotonic inclusion (every GPT-OSS leak was within Qwen's flags), but quadratic weighted Cohen's $\kappa$ on the three-level severity rating was $\TemporalQwk$, lower than elsewhere (e.g., $\kappa=\ObscKappa$ on the obscure suite) and consistent with the \textit{Kappa Paradox}~\citep{feinstein1990high}: GPT-OSS rated $91.7\%$ of responses Level~0 ($289/315$), inflating chance agreement to $\approx 60\%$. Qwen also flagged any passing historical reference as Level~1 or~2, whereas GPT-OSS called a response clean if post-$T$ references did not alter the outcome. Evaluations were reconciled by the recorded 5-tier protocol: (1)~safe refusals resolve to Level~0; (2)~unanimity is preserved; (3)~lexical breach of forbidden phrases and (4)~substantive quotation of post-$T$ law assign Level~2; (5)~subtle mentions resolve to $\max(s_1,s_2)=\text{Level~1}$. Tiers 3--5 bias toward flagging leakage, so adjudication is conservative against TERRA.

\subsubsection{Missingness and Sensitivity Bounds}
Of the $400$ evaluations, $85$ GPT-OSS judgments were missing. Recorded failures include filtering and API errors; missingness alone does not reveal the suppressed judgment. Missing values clustered in Intellectual Property/Patents ($32.1\%$, $18/56$), Antitrust ($23.2\%$, $13/56$) and Commerce ($23.2\%$, $13/56$); Direct LLM accounted for $30$ of the $85$. Its complete-case Condition~A leakage was $28.0\%$, but the direction of missingness bias is unknown. We report the retained sensitivity envelopes $[SPTLR_{\text{lower}}, SPTLR_{\text{upper}}]$ under extreme missing-pair assignments and complete discordant-pair counts $(b,c)$: $b$ counts pairs where $\text{TERRA}_{\text{gated}}$ is clean (Level~0 or~1) while the baseline shows Level~2 leakage, $c$ the reverse, each for complete cases ($N_{\text{cc}}$, both judges finished) and for the full $N=50$ bounds.

\textbf{Headline rates.} Under Condition A, SPTLR bounds are \DirectLlmSptlrA\ for Direct LLM, \FlatRagSptlrA\ for Flat RAG, \TerraUngatedSptlrA\ for $\text{TERRA}_{\text{ungated}}$ and \TerraGatedSptlrA\ for $\text{TERRA}_{\text{gated}}$ (refusal rates \DirectLlmRefusalA, \FlatRagRefusalA, \TerraUngatedRefusalA\ and \TerraGatedRefusalA; HLCR \DirectLlmHlcrA, \FlatRagHlcrA, \TerraUngatedHlcrA\ and \TerraGatedHlcrA). Under Condition B, the SPTLR bounds are \DirectLlmSptlrB, \FlatRagSptlrB, \TerraUngatedSptlrB\ and \TerraGatedSptlrB\ in the same order (refusal \DirectLlmRefusalB, \FlatRagRefusalB, \TerraUngatedRefusalB, \TerraGatedRefusalB; HLCR \DirectLlmHlcrB, \FlatRagHlcrB, \TerraUngatedHlcrB, \TerraGatedHlcrB). The gate interception rate (NGLIR) is $[0.0\%, 20.0\%]$ under Condition A and \NglirRange\ under Condition B, on very small denominators ($D=\NglirDenominatorQwen$ leaking candidates under Qwen, \NglirQwen\ intercepted; $D=\NglirDenominatorConsensus$ under consensus, \NglirConsensus); independent validation of temporal false-negative detection was not performed.

\subsubsection{Primary Hypothesis $H_1$: Metric Decoupling and Refusal Masking}
The recorded $H_1$ predicted lower raw SPTLR for $\text{TERRA}_{\text{gated}}$ than Flat Vector RAG under Condition A. It is \textbf{not supported}: complete cases ($N_{\text{cc}}=36$) give $b=0, c=3$ (one-sided exact McNemar $p=\HoneSptlrPval$; two-sided $p=0.2500$, or $0.0625$ if missing records are zero-imputed at $N=50$), the bounds are $b \in [0,5], c \in [7,8]$, and Flat RAG's raw leakage ($\FlatRagSptlrA$) is lower than TERRA's ($\TerraGatedSptlrA$). The discordant pairs show why: Flat RAG produced no leaks where TERRA was clean ($b=0$) because it refused $\FlatRagRefusalA$ of queries ($23/50$), and a system that refuses cannot leak, whereas TERRA engaged (refusal rate $\TerraGatedRefusalA$) and incurred $c=3$ complete-case Level-2 classifications on complex multi-jurisdictional tests. On HLCR, $\text{TERRA}_{\text{gated}}$ reaches $\mathbf{\TerraGatedHlcrA}$ automated historical-correctness labels against $\FlatRagHlcrA$ for Flat RAG ($b=20, c=1$; exact McNemar $p=\HoneHlcrPvalFormatted$, $p<0.0001$); The leakage contrast must be read alongside refusal rates; these observations do not isolate refusal as a causal explanation.

\subsubsection{Hypotheses $H_2$--$H_6$, Directional Stability and Gate Attribution}
For $H_2$ (TERRA vs.\ Direct LLM, Condition A), complete cases ($N_{\text{cc}}=32$) give $b=2, c=4$; bounding over the 18 missing Direct LLM evaluations gives $b \in [11,16]$, $c \in [5,6]$ ($p \in [\HtwoPvalMin, \HtwoPvalMax]$). Since $c_{\max}=6<b_{\min}=11$, the sign is stable (\textbf{Directional Stability}) under the reported extreme envelopes, although the complete cases favour Direct LLM and $p=\HtwoPvalMin$ holds only at the most favourable bound. For $H_3$ (vs.\ Flat RAG, Condition B), $N_{\text{cc}}=29$ gives $b=5, c=1$; Flat RAG's refusal rate falls to $30.0\%$ under explicit prompting, exposing leakage ($\FlatRagSptlrB$); bounds are $b \in [10,13]$, $c \in [4,8]$ ($p \in [\HthreePvalMin, \HthreePvalMax]$), directionally stable ($c_{\max}=8<b_{\min}=10$). For $H_4$ (vs.\ Direct LLM, Condition B), $N_{\text{cc}}=31$ gives $b=1, c=1$; even when instructed, Direct LLM leaks ($\DirectLlmSptlrB$); bounds are $b \in [8,11]$, $c \in [2,6]$ ($p \in [\HfourPvalMin, \HfourPvalMax]$), directionally stable ($c_{\max}=6<b_{\min}=8$). The gate-ablation tests $H_5, H_6$ are underpowered at $N=50$ ($b \in [0,2], c \in [1,1]$ in Condition A; $b \in [2,5], c \in [0,4]$ in Condition B; complete cases $b=0, c=1$ and $b=2, c=0$). Rather than a high-volume statistical filter, the NLI gate acted as a \textbf{targeted qualitative safety firewall}, making six interventions: it intercepted one Level-2 leak in Condition B (\textit{United States v. Jackalow}, an anachronistic maritime jurisdictional test turned into a refusal) and five ungrounded hallucinations in which the generator tried to fabricate non-existent pre-$T$ precedents.

\subsection{External Benchmark Generalization and Anti-Circularity Audit (CaseFacts)}
\label{subsec:casefacts-generalization}

Evaluating only on researcher-constructed queries risks circular alignment or benchmark overfitting, so we also evaluate on \textbf{CaseFacts} \citep{putta2026casefactsbenchmarklegalfactchecking}, a legal fact-checking dataset built from U.S. Supreme Court decisions by an external team.

\subsubsection{Anti-Circularity and Graph Substrate Audit}
Of the $20{,}000$ nodes, exactly $34$ landmark decisions ($\CaseFactsSeedDensity$, $\CaseFactsSeedDensityRaw$) received curated reasoning traces during early development (not in the evaluated index), whereas the 350-query benchmark touches $284$ distinct nodes ($\CaseFactsFootprintDensity$, $\CaseFactsFootprintNodes$; 100 in Category~A, 151 in B, 0 in C, 58 in D, with 25 nodes of multi-category overlap), leaving $\mathbf{\CaseFactsUntouchedSubstrate}$ of the substrate ($\CaseFactsUntouchedNodes$ nodes) unvisited. The 19 Category-D queries map to 9 distinct edges among the 56 tagged overrulings (list from CRS R45319 via a Wikipedia mirror; 53 audited as consistent, 3 mis-resolved and unused by Category~D); this shows they are recognised overrulings, not unfitted selection. Category~B chains span 3 to 5 nodes (2 to 4 hops): \CatBQueriesLenThree\ have 3 nodes, \CatBQueriesLenFour\ have 4 and \CatBQueriesLenFive\ have 5, and Full TERRA's full-path recovery falls with chain length (\CatBTerraPathRecallLenThree, \CatBTerraPathRecallLenFour\ and \CatBTerraPathRecallLenFive), so performance is not invariant across lengths.

\subsection{Standardized External Benchmark Evaluation on CLERC (NAACL 2025)}
\label{subsec:clerc-generalization}

To test whether the architecture generalizes beyond researcher-crafted queries, we evaluate TERRA on a stratified sample of \textbf{CLERC} (Findings of NAACL 2025) \citep{hou2024clerc}, a legal retrieval and drafting benchmark built by Johns Hopkins University and collaborators. Our validation thus has four layers: the internal controlled benchmark ($N=\numUniqueQueries$; Sections~\ref{subsec:safety-and-factual}--\ref{subsec:ablation-study}), CLERC (this section), CaseFacts (Section~\ref{subsec:casefacts-generalization}), and licensed-practitioner evaluation, which is not done (Limitation~1, Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}).

\subsubsection{Benchmark Protocol and Provenance Audit}
\label{subsubsec:clerc-protocol}
\textbf{Track~1} ($N=\ClercNQueries$) is a stratified sample of 120 pre-1980 drafting contexts from the 1,498 candidate instances in the CLERC test split, drawn before observing any model output with a fixed seed (\texttt{SEED=42}), balanced between 60 Direct Precedent Citation and 60 Indirect Synthesis queries across five eras (1850--1880: $11.7\%$; 1881--1910: $20.0\%$; 1911--1940: $26.7\%$; 1941--1960: $21.7\%$; 1961--1980: $20.0\%$). \textbf{Track~2} ($N=\ClercNRefusal$) is not a native CLERC task but a TERRA-specific refusal stress test of 50 negative controls (39 post-1980 Supreme Court decisions, 11 non-SCOTUS federal circuit or statutory queries). An automated provenance audit found $0/120$ Track~1 targets among the 34 hand-curated seed nodes (overlapping queries were purged), so evaluation is not aided by the bespoke traces; it does not preclude retrieval over ordinary opinions in the shared 20,000-opinion corpus. The broader holding-extraction cascade (Tier~1 syllabi and Tier~2 headnotes, $51.1\%$ of opinions) was not separately screened. The harness also asserts, before each batch, that the NLI grader short-circuits three known-refusal probes. All systems used strict payload parity ($T=0.0$, max tokens 4096, identical LLaMA 3.3 70B generator) and the two non-LLaMA judges (\texttt{qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct}, \texttt{gpt-oss-120b}); citation precision and recall come from deterministic \texttt{eyecite} parsing.

\subsubsection{Multi-Seed Anchoring}
For an evolutionary query $q$, entity and legal-concept extraction yields seeds $S_q \subset V$ with $|S_q| \ge 2$ (mean $\CatBMeanSeedsPerQuery$ per Category~B query; $\CatBTwoSeedsPct$ with two, $\CatBThreeSeedsPct$ with three, $\CatBFourPlusSeedsPct$ with four or more), and each seed expands a $d=2$ ball along forward and backward citation edges with breadth $k=2$. Multi-seed extraction is designed to anchor both extremities of a trajectory (index 0 and $m-1$); seed placement was not re-audited for the canonical run, so this is a mechanism explanation, not a measured result. For a chain $v_0 \to \dots \to v_{m-1}$, the forward ball $B(v_0,2)$ covers $\{v_0,v_1,v_2\}$ and the reverse ball $B(v_{m-1},2)$ covers $\{v_{m-1},v_{m-2},v_{m-3}\}$, so when branching stays within the beam the two neighborhoods meet at intermediate authorities and can cover paths of up to four hops without deeper search. In the canonical run all gold nodes were recovered for $\CatBTerraPathCountLenFour$ 4-node and $\CatBTerraPathCountLenFive$ 5-node chains; high fan-out exceeding the beam is a plausible but untested explanation for the 16 incompletely recovered 4-node chains. We did not test causally whether TERRA's results (Tables~\ref{tab:overall_summary} and~\ref{tab:clerc_eval}) stem from retrieval quality; Direct LLM's higher Category~B faithfulness shows retrieval alone does not explain faithfulness.

\subsection{Benchmark-Graph Traversal Stability Under Temporal Snapshot Expansion}
\label{subsec:temporal-update-stability}

To test traversal stability under corpus growth without generative confounding, we run standalone breadth-first traversal over four snapshot expansions: $1890\to1930$ (Step $N=40$~yr), $1930\to1950$ (20~yr), $1950\to1970$ (20~yr) and $1970\to1980$ (10~yr). Over $N=\IssueNineteenTotalEvals$ qualifying Category~B observations ($\IssueNineteenUniqueQueries$ unique queries anchored at an origin decided at or before $T$, with at least one later gold authority in $(T, T+N]$), BFS traversal ($k=2, d=2$) was run on $G_{\le T}$ and $G_{\le T+N}$ seeded by the origin precedent $s=q[\texttt{target\_nodes}][0]$. Grand pooled recall moved from $\IssueNineteenOverallRecallTMeanStd$ to $\IssueNineteenOverallRecallTNMeanStd$ ($\Delta\text{Recall}=\IssueNineteenOverallDeltaRecallMeanStd$), with $\IssueNineteenStabilityCoefficient$ of queries showing $\Delta\text{Recall} \ge 0$, a Discovery Rate on newly eligible authorities of $\IssueNineteenDiscoveryRateMean$, and median traversal latency under one millisecond (\IssueNineteenBFSLatencyMedianMs~ms; mean $0.15 \pm 0.18$~ms); by snapshot pair, $\Delta\text{Recall}$ is $\IssueNineteenPairIDeltaRecallMeanStd$ ($1890\to1930$), $\IssueNineteenPairIIDeltaRecallMeanStd$ ($1930\to1950$), $\IssueNineteenPairIIIDeltaRecallMeanStd$ ($1950\to1970$) and $\IssueNineteenPairIVDeltaRecallMeanStd$ ($1970\to1980$), and the full per-snapshot table is in the supplementary material. Three boundaries apply: absolute recall includes the depth-0 seed (while $\Delta\text{Recall}$ is invariant to that offset); negative shifts stem from degree caps when expanding nodes become hubs; and the experiment evaluates topology over generic \texttt{CITES} edges on NetworkX DiGraphs, decoupled from vector retrieval (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}) and from live index upsert, which remains future work.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The historical evaluation indicates gains in Category~B target coverage and out-of-domain refusal, with weaknesses against Direct LLM and on obscure precedents without curated labels. The prospective hybrid comparison instead measures a shared gated pipeline: higher T faithfulness coincides with 69/100 versus 4/100 Category~B verifier refusals, so it does not establish better answer coverage or legal correctness.

\subsection{Architectural Differentiation and System Scope}
\label{subsec:contrib-discussion}
Compared with Microsoft Research's GraphRAG \citep{edge2024graphrag}, which builds static hierarchical entity-relation communities for dataset summarization, TERRA models precedent evolution through dated citation edges (\texttt{CITES}) and typed \texttt{OVERRULES} edges imported from CRS annotations (\IssueTwentyOneOverrulesEdges\ tagged, 3 of them found mis-resolved on audit; the 19 Category~D queries cover 9 distinct edges; Section~\ref{subsec:phase1-ingestion}), together with node-level generic procedural scaffolds; live updates are untested. The NLI Smart Grader is designed to reduce unsupported generations, although residual errors remain \citep{asai2024selfrag, zheng2023judging}. The offline pipeline indexes full-docket Supreme Court jurisprudence (1850--1980) as a high-distractor graph, while the evaluated online task is longitudinal precedent tracking, temporal drift, and safety over constitutional and federal public-law doctrine. Three design choices distinguish TERRA, examined only in a 35-query pilot ablation. First, domain-boundary safety pairs lexical anchoring with downstream NLI gating and an optional, scale-dependent geometric proximity flag that terminated no query in the canonical run; 74 of 75 Category~C refusals followed empty retrieval. Second, entailment-gated generation favours refusal over unsupported answers (\TerraCatBFalseRefusals\ of 100 Category~B queries falsely refused), qualified by the \IssueTwentyOneAuditTargetConfirmedPct\ citation-extraction precision of the underlying graph. Third, chronological \texttt{CITES} edges alongside typed \texttt{OVERRULES} relations let TERRA model precedent shifts only where curated annotations exist.

\subsection{Empirical Analysis of Firewall Component Contributions and Scale Dynamics}
\label{subsec:firewall-analysis}

The domain helper combines an indexed-title match with a strict distance-below-1.30 condition; the distance-only diagnostic flags the complementary boundary. A preliminary 50-query Category C audit on the initial 10,009-opinion index flagged $0/50$ queries by distance (reported range $0.281$--$0.859$, units unverified). Safety in Table~\ref{tab:overall_summary} (\TerraCatCRejectionRate\ refusal on Category C; \TerraCatDRejectionRate\ refusal and $\TerraCatDRefutationRate$\ verified refutation on Category D) was therefore delivered by downstream refusal rather than by the proximity gate. In the canonical run, 81 of the 139 refusals occurred on an empty retrieved context (74 in Category~C, 7 in Category~D) and 58 on a non-empty one (2, 16, 1 and 39 in Categories~A--D). In the 35-query pilot ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, Ablation 2), removing NLI reduced adversarial safety (scored as refusal) to 0.0\% on five Category~D queries, so the grader was necessary there; the ablation does not apportion the Category~C refusals between empty-context and entailment-based refusal.

The contemporary distance-only diagnostic reproduces all eight calibration rows in Table~\ref{tab:computational_budget}, Panel B and an AUROC of $\TerraGateIsolatedAUROC$ for Category A/B ($N=200$) versus Category C ($N=75$). This diagnostic is distinct from the canonical evaluation's downstream refusal behavior. The preliminary 10k-index audit is not a matched-scale comparison: its distance units and configuration have not been reconciled with this diagnostic, so the contrast does not establish a corpus-density effect or scaling law.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

We state the essential limitations here; narrower caveats are noted where the corresponding results are reported.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{No licensed-practitioner evaluation.} All faithfulness and safety scores come from automated LLM judges and ROUGE-L against researcher-constructed references, and cannot replicate practitioner judgment of legal correctness, temporal consistency or citation validity; $880$ of the $1{,}750$ canonical evaluations ($50.3\%$) score exactly $1.0$, which limits discrimination among high-scoring systems. The NLI Smart Grader has no labelled validation set, so its false-non-entailment rate is unquantified beyond ablation evidence, and the author spot-check in Section~\ref{subsec:obscure-generalization} is a single non-expert review. The additional two-author assessment was AI-assisted and non-expert; its reconciled ratings were not blinded and do not establish legal correctness or verifier error rates. Expert evaluation is a prerequisite for deployment.
    \item \textbf{Shared model family.} Generation, NLI grading and the Category~D verifier, which produces the refusal, refutation, fatal-error and hedge labels, all use \texttt{databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct}, whereas the two faithfulness judges are from other families; Category~D labels may therefore inherit shared Llama biases, and a cross-family verifier ablation has not been run.
    \item \textbf{Safety depends on curated labels.} Full TERRA's advantage on obscure overruled precedents is contingent on curated \texttt{OVERRULES} annotations, which carry a relation tag and a larger excerpt budget, and the obscure pairs were drawn from these edges; the bundled ablation cannot isolate the tag, the suite has thirty items, and judges were not fully blind to arm identity. Three of the 56 tagged edges were also found mis-resolved on audit (Section~\ref{subsec:phase1-ingestion}); none was serialised in a deployed context, but indirect effects are not excluded.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{subsec:future-work}
Future research will: (1)~conduct licensed practitioner evaluation across the 350-query benchmark; (2)~scale corpus ingestion to the full federal repository; and (3)~codify \texttt{OVERRULES} annotations as explicit weighted graph edges with adaptive invalidation-path traversal.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduced TERRA, an experimental dual-phase GraphRAG architecture addressing knowledge drift and multi-step legal reasoning on historical SCOTUS jurisprudence (\CorpusTemporalSpan). Structuring \CorpusTotalOpinions\ authentic decisions (\CorpusPrecedentEdges\ citation edges) with generic procedural scaffolds, TERRA enables inference via Traffic Cop routing, BFS traversal, and an NLI grader evaluating retrieved evidence. The historical multi-baseline evaluation across \numUniqueQueries\ queries (\numTotalEvals\ evaluations) shows higher Category~B multi-hop faithfulness than Flat Vector RAG, HippoRAG, and GraphRAG ($\Delta$ from $\CatBTerraVsHippoRAGDelta$ to $\CatBTerraVsGraphRAGDelta$; $q<0.05$; the paired interval against Flat includes zero), though not over Direct LLM ($\CatBTerraVsDirectLLMDelta$); higher overall faithfulness ($\TerraOverallFaithfulnessMean$ vs.\ $\HippoRAGOverallFaithfulnessMean$, overlapping CIs; $\TerraAnswerOnlyFaithfulnessMean$); and $\TerraCatDSafeHandlingRate$ adversarial safe handling ($\TerraCatDRejection$ refusals, \TerraCatDRefutations\ refutations). Constraints include \TerraCatDFatalErrors\ fatal errors, false refusals on \TerraCatAFalseRefusals\ Category~A and \TerraCatBFalseRefusals\ Category~B queries, and reliance on curated overruling annotations (Section~\ref{subsec:obscure-generalization}). Critically, because evaluations rely on automated LLM judges without licensed attorneys (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}), human expert review remains an essential prerequisite for deployment. In a standalone traversal test over four snapshot expansions, pooled recall shifted by $\IssueNineteenOverallDeltaRecallMeanStd$ (Section~\ref{subsec:temporal-update-stability}).

In the separate prospective controlled comparison, among the 100 assessable Category~B pairs out of 100 assigned, the T-minus-H composite difference was $0.26885$ (paired percentile-bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.16035, 0.37205]$; two-sided paired randomization $p=0.000010$). T produced 69/100 verifier-triggered refusals on Category~B versus 4/100 for H; this coverage tradeoff prevents interpreting the faithfulness difference as better answer coverage or legal correctness. This evidence-faithfulness result is confined to the matched implementation and does not establish legal validity.
