Unifying Event Evolution Graphs and Generic
Procedural Scaﬀolds via Semantic Entailment: A
Dual-Phase Architecture for Judicial GraphRAG
Abstract
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for historical law must retrieve con-
nected authorities without treating overruled doctrine as current law. We
introduce TERRA, an experimental GraphRAG pipeline combining an Event
Evolution Graph over 20,000 Supreme Court opinions, generic ﬁve-step proce-
dural scaﬀolds, EASY/HARD routing and LLM-prompted evidence checking.
Its scaﬀolds are not case-speciﬁc judicial reasoning. A historical 350-query, ﬁve-
system evaluation uses two non-Llama judges and paired tests with Benjamini–
Hochberg correction. TERRA’s Category B faithfulness exceeds the retrieval
baselines but remains below Direct LLM; its overall and answer-only scores
describe diﬀerent refusal and answered subsets. Adversarial safe handling is
97.3%, with 2/75 (2.7%) fatal errors and false refusals on 2 Category A and 16
Category B queries. The obscure-precedent advantage depends on curated over-
ruling annotations. A separate prospective comparison evaluates graph retrieval
(T) against dense–BM25 retrieval (H) on the same 350 queries with shared evi-
dence budgets, veriﬁcation, generation and judges. On 100 Category B pairs,
mean evidence faithfulness is 0.76400 for T and 0.49515 for H (paired diﬀerence
0.26885; bootstrap 95% CI [0.16035, 0.37205]; randomization p ≈0.000010). T
triggers 69/100 veriﬁer refusals versus H’s 4/100: the gain concerns the gated
pipeline, not demonstrated answer coverage or legal correctness. External and
temporal evaluations remain mixed. Historical conﬁguration gaps, annotation
dependence and absent licensed-practitioner validation restrict the ﬁndings to an
experimental research framework.
Keywords: Retrieval-augmented generation, Temporal knowledge graphs, Judicial
GraphRAG, Procedural scaﬀolds, Semantic entailment, Temporal leakage
1 Introduction
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) decouples the parametric memory of Large
Language Models (LLMs) from external knowledge bases, enabling factual grounding
and source attribution without continuous retraining. In high-stakes settings, where
errors and hallucinations carry real consequences, the ﬁdelity of the retrieved con-
text is a strict operational requirement, and naive “retrieve-then-generate” pipelines
1


over static vector databases and ﬁxed-length chunks can struggle with conﬂicting
authorities and multi-step evidence.
The bottleneck arises with continuous knowledge drift and multi-step reasoning.
Static vector indices can accumulate conﬂicting, time-stamped versions of the same
fact; a retriever ranking by semantic similarity may surface several together, leaving a
frozen generator without a basis for choosing the chronologically authoritative one and
prone to state outdated law. For multi-step derivations, similarity-based pipelines can
retrieve fragments that omit precedential and doctrinal dependencies. We motivate
the design with these mechanisms and do not measure them in isolation.
Recent work addresses these bottlenecks in silos. Graph-based retrievers organize
facts chronologically but lack the procedural logic to guide multi-hop generation;
retrieval of intermediate reasoning traces improves deduction but is temporally blind
and mostly conﬁned to mathematics or coding; and corrective Agentic RAG frame-
works rely on retrieval evaluators that may not capture conceptual relevance, as a
SHAP-based study of the CRAG T5 evaluator found it relies mainly on named-entity
alignment rather than semantic similarity (Yalavarthi 2026). Many hybrid systems
route among a small ﬁxed set of channels, while adaptive multi-strategy routing is
emerging (Hashmi 2026).
We therefore introduce a dual-phase GraphRAG architecture for evolving,
knowledge-intensive domains (traversal stability under temporal snapshot expansion is
evaluated in Section 6.15). Oﬄine corpus construction builds a dual-index corpus that
cross-links an Event Evolution Graph (EEG), which encodes precedent relationships
across 20,000 judicial decisions and 145,366 directed citation edges, with a node-level
generic procedural scaﬀold, a ﬁxed ﬁve-step template instantiated per case. Online,
a small language model acts as a Traﬃc Cop router classifying each query as EASY
or HARD, selecting anchor retrieval without graph expansion for EASY queries or
citation-graph traversal for HARD queries, which yields a bimodal execution proﬁle
(gross median 5.83 s on Category A factual lookups vs. 17.94 s on Category B multi-
hop chains). Before generation, an LLM-prompted entailment grader decides whether
the retrieved evidence suﬃces; its accuracy was not independently validated.
The historical evaluation compares TERRA with four baselines, including Direct
LLM and Flat Vector RAG, on a 350-query benchmark with a 35-query pilot ablation.
We did not test attention dilution or context-length eﬀects directly, and TERRA’s
mean retrieved context is longer than the retrieval baselines’ (about 18,400 versus
2,900–4,100 characters). In a 50-query temporal leakage probe, TERRA did not leak
less than Flat Vector RAG under default prompting (the recorded hypothesis H1 is
not supported, and Flat RAG refused 23 of 50 queries) but received more automated
historical-correctness labels (76.0% versus 38.0%), without expert validation. A sep-
arate prospective dense–BM25 comparison holds the surrounding pipeline constant
(Section 6.3); its higher T faithfulness coincides with 69/100 versus 4/100 veriﬁer
refusals on Category B, not superior answer coverage. The work is an experimental
framework for context ﬁdelity, not deployment validation.

1.1 Summary of Contributions
• Dual-Index Architecture: a judicial GraphRAG corpus cross-linking an EEG
of 20,000 SCOTUS decisions (145,366 citation edges) with a node-level generic
procedural scaﬀold (one ﬁve-step template per case, with no case-speciﬁc issue, test
or holding) and no synthetic cases.
• Traﬃc Cop Routing with a Coarse Domain Flag: an SLM-driven complexity
router with a domain-relevance ﬂag derived from a title match or nearest-neighbour
proximity (dL2 < 1.3 in the current source). This ﬂag is not itself an early-refusal
rule; the canonical evaluation terminated no query before retrieval (Sections 6.14
and 7.2).
• NLI-Based Entailment Evaluation and XAI Attribution: an LLM-prompted
entailment grader that evaluates retrieved evidence and gates generation, with
generic procedural scaﬀolds and citation provenance; its interception reliability is
unvalidated; a pilot ablation included ﬁve unique adversarial queries.
• Leakage Safeguards and Audits: safeguards against temporal precedent inver-
sion, benchmark retrieval contamination and cross-domain feature bleeding, with
mixed results in the leakage probe.
• Historical Empirical Evaluation: across 1,750 evaluations (350 queries × 5
pipelines, T = 0.0) with paired Wilcoxon tests, Cohen’s dz and 95% bootstrap
CIs, TERRA has the highest Answer-Only Faithfulness among RAG architec-
tures (0.586 ± 0.399 vs. HippoRAG’s 0.392 ± 0.370), outperforms Flat Vector RAG
(∆= +0.070, p = 0.0032, dz = 0.166) and HippoRAG (∆= +0.066, p = 0.0068,
dz = 0.207) on Category B, delivers 97.3% adversarial safe handling (73/75(97.3%):
48/75 (64.0%) refusals, 25/75 (33.3%) refutations) with 2/75 (2.7%) fatal errors
vs. 4–8 for baselines, and incurs 2 false refusals on Category A (16 on B). Direct
LLM scores higher on Category B (∆= −0.081), and the Flat margin’s boot-
strap CI includes zero. Evaluations rely on automated LLM judges without licensed
attorneys, making human review an essential prerequisite (Section 7.3).
2 Related Work
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Lewis et al. 2020; Guu et al. 2020) decoupled
parametric memory from external knowledge, but standard RAG struggles with knowl-
edge drift and multi-step reasoning. Graph-based architectures structure retrieved
evidence: GraphRAG (Edge et al. 2024) builds hierarchical entity-relation communi-
ties for global summarization, and Think-on-Graph (Sun et al. 2024) runs iterative
LLM-guided reasoning over knowledge graphs. In temporal domains, Liu and Cao
(Liu et al. 2026) introduced a continuous-knowledge-drift benchmark and Chronos,
a time-aware method organizing evidence into an Event Evolution Graph (EEG);
Zhang et al. (Zhang et al. 2026) proposed E2RAG for temporal-causal consistency
across entity-event subgraphs; and Zhu et al. (Zhu et al. 2025) developed STAR-RAG
for time-sensitive rule graph summarization. In law, Chen et al. (Chen et al. 2026)
proposed LegalGraphRAG, a Researcher, Auditor and Adjudicator multi-agent sys-
tem over a hierarchical legal graph, and de Martim (de Martim 2025) introduced
SAT-Graph RAG to trace diachronic statutory norm versions across constitutional
3


amendments. These systems organize sources by abstraction level or model formal
amendment events, whereas common-law systems also require tracking how precedent
evolves across citation networks, where earlier decisions remain presumptive author-
ity unless distinguished, limited or overruled; and motivates testing graph-grounded
retrieval with explicit evidence checking on historical precedent questions.
To supply that logic, recent work augments retrieval with intermediate reasoning
trajectories. CoT-RAG (Li et al. 2025) combines knowledge-graph-guided chain-of-
thought generation with retrieval and reports gains of 4.0% to 44.3% across nine public
reasoning datasets, and surveys of agentic reasoning (Lin et al. 2025; Liang et al. 2025)
describe pipelines in which agents identify knowledge gaps and generate intermediate
traces to inform search. Legal benchmarks include CaseHOLD (Zheng et al. 2021),
which evaluates holding identiﬁcation, and CLERC (Hou et al. 2025), which evaluates
U.S. case retrieval and analysis drafting; as we read them, neither targets superseded or
overruled authority, the gap TERRA’s evaluation addresses. LegalBench (Guha et al.
2023) broadens coverage to 162 tasks across six types of legal reasoning. Earlier legal
NLP work includes outcome prediction for Supreme Court cases (Katz et al. 2017) and
domain-pretrained language models such as LEGAL-BERT (Chalkidis et al. 2020),
and an audit of leading commercial legal research tools found that they hallucinated
in more than 17% of queries (Magesh et al. 2025). Trace-retrieval frameworks have
been evaluated mainly on quantitative domains or static statutory collections, leaving
longitudinal common-law settings largely untested.
Query routers and evaluators form the connective tissue of the pipeline. Hashmi
(Hashmi 2026) highlighted the rigidity of single-channel retrieval and introduced an
Adaptive Hybrid Retrieval framework, noting that pure vector similarity often fails to
capture structural navigation. Yalavarthi (Yalavarthi 2026) found through SHAP anal-
ysis that the T5-based evaluator in Corrective RAG (CRAG) prioritizes named-entity
matching over semantic understanding and transfers poorly to scientiﬁc questions.
Putta et al. (Putta et al. 2026) showed with CaseFacts that unrestricted web search
degrades legal fact-checking relative to closed-book baselines. Retrievers ranking by
lexical or embedding similarity can return overruled precedents alongside current ones
without marking which was overturned, which motivates the explicit entailment check
tested below.
TERRA combines temporal precedent management, generic procedural scaﬀolds
and LLM-prompted evidence checking in one experimental pipeline. Its node-level
scaﬀolds repeat a ﬁxed ﬁve-step template, rather than providing case-speciﬁc legal
reasoning. The evaluation tests the combined system and its trade-oﬀs; it does not
isolate the scaﬀolds’ eﬀect or establish individual-component novelty. A binary Traﬃc
Cop router selects anchor retrieval or citation-graph traversal without retraining the
foundation model.

3.1 Phase 1: Oﬄine Ingestion and Knowledge Graph
Construction
3.1.1 Authentic Judicial Opinion Corpus (20,000 Cases)
TERRA operates on 20,000 full-text Supreme Court opinions spanning 1850–1980
(Volumes 50–448), curated from an initial harvest of 23,010 public-domain federal deci-
sions from CourtListener and the Harvard Caselaw repositories (public domain under
17 U.S.C. § 105). To remove non-precedential noise, opinions were ﬁltered by length
(character count ≥4,000). Of the 131,315 decisions in 399 scanned U.S. Reports
volumes, 102,235 (77.85%) are shorter than 500 characters (procedural orders, sum-
mary dispositions and docket records, subject to audit). The length ﬁlter nevertheless
induces bias: it excludes 8,324 intermediate decisions (500–3,999 characters, 6.34%),
of which 46 rank in the top 0.5% of citation authority under Harvard CAP PageR-
ank (e.g., Haines v. Kerner, Dusky v. United States, Lucas v. Earl, Sipuel v. Board
of Regents, Gayle v. Browder). The corpus contains no synthetic cases. A core set of
34 landmark civil rights decisions, from Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857) through Plessy
v. Ferguson (1896), Missouri ex rel. Gaines v. Canada (1938) and Brown v. Board of
Education (1954) to Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg (1971), anchors the multi-hop
benchmark.

3.2.3 NLI Smart Grader and Self-Correction Expansion Loop
Before generation, C(q) is evaluated by the NLI Smart Grader, a structured-prompt
call to databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct (Dubey et al. 2024) (T = 0.0)
given the query and context and instructed to return JSON with entails (boolean),
confidence_score (ﬂoat) and reasoning (string). This allows single-call veriﬁcation
without a separate NLI cross-encoder (e.g., DeBERTa (He et al. 2023)), while routing
stays with the 12B Gemma SLM. No labeled validation set was built for the veriﬁer;
the only empirical evidence of its role is the pilot ablation, in which removing the gate
(Ablation 2, Table 2) lowers adversarial safety rejection from 100.0% to 0.0%, which
is consistent with the gate contributing to safety but not a validation of its accuracy.
False non-entailment (supported context rejected) and false entailment (insuﬃcient
context accepted) are unquantiﬁed failure modes (Section 7.3). If entails == True,
the context goes to the generator. If entails == False, a Self-Correction Expan-
sion Loop widens retrieval (k ←k + 2), re-queries ChromaDB and re-runs BFS.
If entailment still fails after two expansions, the Safety Refusal Firewall halts
generation with a deterministic refusal: “I apologize, but I do not have suﬃcient val-
idated legal context in my databases to answer this query accurately without risking
hallucination.”

3.2.4 Data Leakage Mitigation Framework
TERRA applies four safeguards against leakage. (1) Temporal directionality:
an earlier case cannot cite later jurisprudence, so forward CITES edges require
t(vcite) ≥t(vcited), and retrospective invalidation uses CRS-derived OVERRULES rela-
tions (Section 3.1) rather than unrestricted backward edges, providing a temporal
constraint without guaranteeing historically correct answers. (2) Vector-store isola-
tion: benchmark queries, rubrics and reference answers are segregated from the index,
which holds only opinion text and generic procedural scaﬀolds. (3) Cross-domain
bleed prevention: title matching and the proximity ﬂag (which terminated no query
in the canonical run) are coarse ﬁrst-line signals, and NLI veriﬁcation is designed
to suppress non-entailed subgraphs before synthesis. (4) Temporal parametric
containment: to limit post-T doctrine entering historical answers, traversals and
retrieved corpora are bounded by provenance timestamps (KnowledgeProvenance ≤
T), and candidate generations ˆycandidate pass an entailment gate against contempora-
neous evidence before release. In the leakage probe (Section 6.8) the gate made six
interventions, but its bulk leakage reduction was not statistically established.
3.2.5 Explainable AI (XAI) and Topological Provenance
TERRA provides four explainability layers: (1) graph traversal provenance, a
recorded retrieval path P = ⟨v1, e1, v2, . . . , vk⟩through cited cases, which does
not certify the doctrinal support of each answer; (2) hyperlinked attribution,
in which cited cases with indexed identiﬁers become relative markdown links
(e.g., [Brown v. Board of Education](/case/347_0483-01)); across benchmark
completions TERRA emitted 664 links with a 99.40% resolution rate (660/664)

5.1 Benchmark Suite Design
The evaluation suite comprises 350 unique queries evaluated across 5 pipelines (1,750
evaluations; dates and model identiﬁers are given under Multi-Run Provenance below),
plus a foundational N = 35 pilot ablation set (5 repeated trials per query, 700 exe-
cutions across four conﬁgurations), across four categories isolating factual accuracy,
multi-hop reasoning, domain safety and adversarial robustness. Temperature T=0.0
was used throughout but does not guarantee identical outputs across runs (Section 7.3).
Faithfulness scores cover all four categories, including refusals; ROUGE-L evaluates
generative Categories A and B, and safety labels evaluate Categories C and D:
• Category A — Single-Point Factual: single-fact queries (decision years, party
names) scored against researcher-constructed reference answers with ROUGE-L.
• Category B — Multi-Hop Evolutionary: multi-decade precedent-tracking
queries scored against researcher-constructed reference answers with ROUGE-L. To
test non-trivial traversal while reﬂecting the hub topology of citation networks, all
chains span 3–5 nodes (2–4 hops): 63 three-node, 32 four-node and 5 ﬁve-node arcs,
with no 2-node pairs. Category B gold paths derive from the EEG’s own citations
(circularity risk), and Categories A and B use three and one question templates.
• Category C — Out-of-Context Safety: 75 queries (N = 15 each) on top-
ics absent from indexed Supreme Court law (1850–1980): natural sciences, foreign
and treaty law, state and municipal ordinances, post-2020 software and culture,
and corporate accounting. With no controlling precedent, the required behavior is
refusal.
• Category D — Adversarial Deception: queries pairing real in-domain case
titles with fabricated premises or temporal falsehoods (e.g., governing the 1905
Lochner ruling by the 1938 Fair Labor Standards Act), built to trick entity-matching
routers. A safe response is an explicit refusal or a veriﬁed refutation of the false
premise citing authentic authority.
Task Scope vs. Background Corpus. The EEG covers the full 20,000-case docket
across seven areas (including 5,707 Substantive Private Law and 4,594 General Statu-
tory decisions; Table 1), but Category B concentrates on constitutional and federal
public-law trajectories (20 queries each across Civil Rights, Criminal Procedure, First
13


Amendment, Commerce Clause and Administrative Law; 100.0%). Of the 275 in-
domain queries (Categories A, B and D), 232 (84.4%) are constitutional or public
law, 23 (8.4%) private law and 20 (7.3%) general statutory. Private-law disputes were
not modeled as 3–5 node citation chains, whereas constitutional jurisprudence shows
longitudinal evolution and explicit overruling; retaining the full repository gives a high-
distractor environment where constitutional reasoning must avoid 51.5% commercial
and statutory background nodes.
Category C and D Construction. The 75 Category C queries were pre-registered
across ﬁve domains (Subtypes C1–C5) before evaluation, and an automated audit
against the indexed repository found 0/75 title, citation or holding overlaps. The
75 Category D queries were built by the research team under a pre-registered
taxonomy: D1 (N = 19) explicit overruled-precedent traps, D2 (N = 19) title sub-
stitution, D3 (N = 19) temporal inversion with a ≥5-year gap, and D4 (N = 18)
procedural-posture deceptions, sampled with RANDOM_SEED = 42 from 19 veriﬁed
doctrine-invalidation pairs. All queries were locked into terra_350_benchmark.json
(SHA256: 8144fa81...) before any pipeline ran, with no queries or references indexed
into ChromaDB (Section 3.2.4); the domain-labeling protocol and Category C cat-
alog are identiﬁed in the local supplementary documentation; public availability is
not certiﬁed here. A standalone 15-query stress test (10 out-of-context, 5 adversarial;
stress_test.py) checks ﬁrewall boundary consistency.
Obscure Overruled Precedent Suite.
To test whether Category D safety reﬂects retrieval or memorization of famous land-
marks, we built a supplementary suite of 30 obscure overruled-precedent queries from
the 47 CRS OVERRULES edges not used in Category D, excluding three whose overrul-
ing opinions never mention the target (we infer, without a retained exclusion ledger,
that these are the three mis-resolved edges of Section 3.1) and selecting the thirty
lowest-in-degree pairs by a mechanical rule ﬁxed before inference. Each query asks
what the overruled decision held without hinting at the overruling authority (leakage
audited). Four arms ran under identical settings: Direct LLM; Flat Vector RAG with
the identical instruction block; Full TERRA; and TERRA-Unlabeled, which removes
the relation tags, the overruling heading, the prompt’s reference to the tag and the
enlarged excerpt allocation for overruling edges while keeping seeds, traversal and
router unchanged. Two judges (Qwen3-Next-80B, GPT-OSS-120B; neither is the gen-
erator) classiﬁed each answer as refusal, veriﬁed refutation, fatal error (aﬃrmed as
current law, or recited without warning) or hedge, and the combined class takes the
more severe judgment. The primary hypothesis, sensitivity subsets and cross-suite con-
trast were preregistered before any live output; for the contrast we re-ran the nineteen
Category D doctrine-invalidation queries under identical code, prompts and judges.

The ablation (Table 2) shows the system-level value of routing (Ablation 3 in the

Gemma 3 12B Instruction-tuned (databricks-gemma-3-12b) via the Databricks

6.5.1 Intent Classiﬁcation Performance and Category D Misroutes
Overall accuracy is 97.14% (340/350) with macro-F1 96.60% (macro precision
95.45%, macro recall 98.00%). On the factual class (Gold EASY, Category A) F1 is
95.24% with perfect recall (100.00%, 100/100); on complex inquiries (Gold HARD,
Categories B–D) it is 97.96% with 100.00% precision (240/240). The False-HARD rate
on factual queries is 0.00% (0/100), and the False-EASY rate over complex queries is
4.00% (10/250), with none on Category B (0/100) or Category C (0/75). In isolation

All 10 misroutes are Category D queries (13.33%, 10/75): D021, D039, D060, D062,

evidence that downstream mechanisms reliably prevent hallucination.

6.8 Temporal Parametric Leakage Stress Test
Models pretrained on post-T legal history risk temporal parametric leakage, anachro-
nistically importing later doctrinal shifts or overrulings into analyses framed at
cutoﬀT. We ran an exploratory 50-query probe across ﬁve horizons (T
∈
{1890, 1930, 1950, 1970, 1980}), spanning seven doctrinal domains and balanced across
case prominence and reasoning depth. Six probes (12%) target cases decided between
1857 and 1905 (cutoﬀs T = 1890 and 1930) whose nodes have zero out-edges in the
EEG owing to the citation-parsing boundary; their arcs are veriﬁed against primary
sources but are not EEG edges, and all metrics are computed identically across the
50 probes.
6.8.1 Design, Metrics and Evaluator Reliability
The probe evaluates 400 responses from Direct LLM, Flat Vector RAG, TERRAungated
(Lgen) and TERRAgated (Lﬁnal), each under Condition A (standard prompt) and
Condition B (explicit instruction to adhere to year T), under a recorded 3-tier rubric:
Level 0 (clean), Level 1 (non-prejudicial mention or disclaimer) and Level 2 (fatal
reliance on post-T law). The primary metric is the Substantive Post-T Leakage
Rate (SPTLR), the share of Level 2 failures. Because refusing systems generate
no post-T text and look “leak-free”, we decouple containment from problem-solving
with the Refusal Rate and the Historical Legal Correctness Rate (HLCR), an
automated rubric label for whether the system states the operative rule as of T, not
expert-certiﬁed correctness, and we measure the NLI Gate Leakage Interception
Rate (NGLIR), the share of ungated leaking candidates intercepted by the gate
(denominator D).
Responses were judged by qwen3-next-80b and gpt-oss-120b. Over N = 315
complete pairs the judges reached 68.6% raw concordance and 100% monotonic inclu-
sion (every GPT-OSS leak was within Qwen’s ﬂags), but quadratic weighted Cohen’s
κ on the three-level severity rating was 0.260, lower than elsewhere (e.g., κ = 0.5746
on the obscure suite) and consistent with the Kappa Paradox (Feinstein and Cicchetti
1990): GPT-OSS rated 91.7% of responses Level 0 (289/315), inﬂating chance agree-
ment to ≈60%. Qwen also ﬂagged any passing historical reference as Level 1 or 2,
whereas GPT-OSS called a response clean if post-T references did not alter the out-
come. Evaluations were reconciled by the recorded 5-tier protocol: (1) safe refusals
resolve to Level 0; (2) unanimity is preserved; (3) lexical breach of forbidden phrases
and (4) substantive quotation of post-T law assign Level 2; (5) subtle mentions resolve
to max(s1, s2) = Level 1. Tiers 3–5 bias toward ﬂagging leakage, so adjudication is
conservative against TERRA.

6.8.3 Primary Hypothesis H1: Metric Decoupling and Refusal
Masking
The recorded H1 predicted lower raw SPTLR for TERRAgated than Flat Vector RAG
under Condition A. It is not supported: complete cases (Ncc = 36) give b = 0, c = 3
(one-sided exact McNemar p = 1.0000; two-sided p = 0.2500, or 0.0625 if missing
records are zero-imputed at N = 50), the bounds are b ∈[0, 5], c ∈[7, 8], and Flat
RAG’s raw leakage ([2.0%, 14.0%]) is lower than TERRA’s ([18.0%, 18.0%]). The dis-
cordant pairs show why: Flat RAG produced no leaks where TERRA was clean
(b = 0) because it refused 46.0% of queries (23/50), and a system that refuses cannot
leak, whereas TERRA engaged (refusal rate 8.0%) and incurred c = 3 complete-case
Level-2 classiﬁcations on complex multi-jurisdictional tests. On HLCR, TERRAgated
reaches 76.0% automated historical-correctness labels against 38.0% for Flat RAG
(b = 20, c = 1; exact McNemar p = 2.1×10−5, p < 0.0001); The leakage contrast must
be read alongside refusal rates; these observations do not isolate refusal as a causal
explanation.

Audit (CaseFacts)

6.10.1 Anti-Circularity and Graph Substrate Audit
Of the 20,000 nodes, exactly 34 landmark decisions (0.17%, 34/20, 000) received
curated reasoning traces during early development (not in the evaluated index),
whereas the 350-query benchmark touches 284 distinct nodes (1.42%, 284/20, 000; 100
in Category A, 151 in B, 0 in C, 58 in D, with 25 nodes of multi-category overlap),
leaving 98.58% of the substrate (19, 716/20, 000 nodes) unvisited. The 19 Category-
D queries map to 9 distinct edges among the 56 tagged overrulings (list from CRS
R45319 via a Wikipedia mirror; 53 audited as consistent, 3 mis-resolved and unused
by Category D); this shows they are recognised overrulings, not unﬁtted selection.
Category B chains span 3 to 5 nodes (2 to 4 hops): 63 have 3 nodes, 32 have 4 and
5 have 5, and Full TERRA’s full-path recovery falls with chain length (77.8%, 50.0%
and 20.0%), so performance is not invariant across lengths.

6.11.2 Empirical Findings
Track 1 (Table 9, Panel A) is mixed. In retrieval (Task 1A), Full TERRA has
slightly higher Recall@10 (10.8% vs. 10.0%) and nDCG@10 (0.069 vs. 0.066) than Flat
Vector RAG, a one-query diﬀerence in hits@10 (13/120 vs. 12/120; MRR@10 0.056 vs.
0.055), so the margin is modest (+0.8 points). In drafting (Task 1B), TERRA attains
6.50 ROUGE-L and 1.10 BLEU-4 and the highest Citation Precision (CP = 5.5% vs.
2.9% for Flat and 4.5% for Direct), with Citation Recall 2.6% (Flat 1.4%, Direct 4.9%)
and Citation F1 3.5%[5.5%/2.6%]. Direct LLM has the highest Citation F1 (4.7%,
against TERRA’s 3.5% and Flat’s 1.8%; micro-pooled over items; TERRA’s pooled
precision omits its 53 gate-refused items, which carry no citations, while its recall still
counts them), and TERRA’s judged soundness is the lowest of the three (1.98 against
2.34 and 2.84).
We recomputed a citation audit from the raw Track 1 completions. Among case
captions extracted by pattern from judge-ﬂagged completions, the share matching no
corpus title is 15.3% for TERRA (43/281), 15.0% for Flat (88/585) and 41.1% for
Direct LLM (147/358); the remaining TERRA captions resolve to in-corpus decisions
(84.7%), and gate-intercepted TERRA completions carry no citations (0/53). This is a
corpus-membership check, not an existence check: the unmatched captions include real
Supreme Court decisions outside the 20k subset as well as extraction fragments, so we
do not report a fabrication rate, and an earlier recorded value of 0.0% fabrication for
TERRA and Flat, which this recomputation does not reproduce, is withdrawn. Dual
judging nevertheless ﬂagged doctrinal misapplication in 50.0%/37.5% of all 120 com-
pletions (50.0% under OR, 37.5% under AND; Flat 98.3%/86.7%, Direct 90.0%/64.2%,
neither gated). All of TERRA’s ﬂags fall among the 67 completions generated on
incomplete retrieval subgraphs (89.6%(60/67) OR, 67.2%(45/67) AND), where incom-

generated misapplications: authentic evidence does not equate to suﬃcient evidence,
which motivates the path-recovery analysis in Section 6.12.

6.12.2 Multi-Seed Anchoring
For an evolutionary query q, entity and legal-concept extraction yields seeds Sq ⊂V
with |Sq| ≥2 (mean 2.41 per Category B query; 73.0% with two, 19.0% with three,
8.0% with four or more), and each seed expands a d = 2 ball along forward and
backward citation edges with breadth k = 2. Multi-seed extraction is designed to
anchor both extremities of a trajectory (index 0 and m −1); seed placement was not
re-audited for the canonical run, so this is a mechanism explanation, not a measured
result. For a chain v0 →· · · →vm−1, the forward ball B(v0, 2) covers {v0, v1, v2}
and the reverse ball B(vm−1, 2) covers {vm−1, vm−2, vm−3}, so when branching stays
within the beam the two neighborhoods meet at intermediate authorities and can
cover paths of up to four hops without deeper search. In the canonical run all gold
nodes were recovered for 16/32 4-node and 1/5 5-node chains; high fan-out exceeding
the beam is a plausible but untested explanation for the 16 incompletely recovered 4-
node chains. We did not test causally whether TERRA’s results (Tables 4 and 9) stem
from retrieval quality; Direct LLM’s higher Category B faithfulness shows retrieval
alone does not explain faithfulness.

6.13 Generative Citation Correctness, Doctrinal Support, and
Interactive Hyperlink Provenance
Legal validity depends on whether the generator cites and attributes doctrine cor-
rectly in its ﬁnal completions, since language models commonly invent case names,
cite authorities for propositions they never established, or hallucinate citations on
unanswerable inquiries. Table 11 therefore evaluates citation ﬁdelity in the synthe-
sized completions across the 350-query suite (1,750 evaluations over 5 pipelines).
Grounded doctrinal precision and F1 are primary (TERRA 49.4%/53.3%, HippoRAG

(Step N = 40 yr), 1930 →1950 (20 yr), 1950 →1970 (20 yr) and 1970 →1980 (10 yr).
Over N = 106 qualifying Category B observations (82 unique queries anchored at an
origin decided at or before T, with at least one later gold authority in (T, T +N]), BFS

7 Discussion
The historical evaluation indicates gains in Category B target coverage and out-
of-domain refusal, with weaknesses against Direct LLM and on obscure precedents
without curated labels. The prospective hybrid comparison instead measures a shared
gated pipeline: higher T faithfulness coincides with 69/100 versus 4/100 Category B
veriﬁer refusals, so it does not establish better answer coverage or legal correctness.
7.1 Architectural Diﬀerentiation and System Scope
Compared with Microsoft Research’s GraphRAG (Edge et al. 2024), which builds
static hierarchical entity-relation communities for dataset summarization, TERRA
models precedent evolution through dated citation edges (CITES) and typed
OVERRULES edges imported from CRS annotations (56 tagged, 3 of them found mis-
resolved on audit; the 19 Category D queries cover 9 distinct edges; Section 3.1),
together with node-level generic procedural scaﬀolds; live updates are untested. The
NLI Smart Grader is designed to reduce unsupported generations, although residual
errors remain (Asai et al. 2024; Zheng et al. 2023). The oﬄine pipeline indexes full-
docket Supreme Court jurisprudence (1850–1980) as a high-distractor graph, while
the evaluated online task is longitudinal precedent tracking, temporal drift, and safety
over constitutional and federal public-law doctrine. Three design choices distinguish
TERRA, examined only in a 35-query pilot ablation. First, domain-boundary safety
pairs lexical anchoring with downstream NLI gating and an optional, scale-dependent
geometric proximity ﬂag that terminated no query in the canonical run; 74 of 75 Cate-
gory C refusals followed empty retrieval. Second, entailment-gated generation favours
refusal over unsupported answers (16 of 100 Category B queries falsely refused),
qualiﬁed by the 94.5% citation-extraction precision of the underlying graph. Third,
chronological CITES edges alongside typed OVERRULES relations let TERRA model
precedent shifts only where curated annotations exist.

7.2 Empirical Analysis of Firewall Component Contributions
and Scale Dynamics
The domain helper combines an indexed-title match with a strict distance-below-1.30
condition; the distance-only diagnostic ﬂags the complementary boundary. A prelim-
inary 50-query Category C audit on the initial 10,009-opinion index ﬂagged 0/50
queries by distance (reported range 0.281–0.859, units unveriﬁed). Safety in Table 4
(100.0% refusal on Category C; 64.0% refusal and 33.3% veriﬁed refutation on Cate-
gory D) was therefore delivered by downstream refusal rather than by the proximity
gate. In the canonical run, 81 of the 139 refusals occurred on an empty retrieved con-
text (74 in Category C, 7 in Category D) and 58 on a non-empty one (2, 16, 1 and
39 in Categories A–D). In the 35-query pilot ablation (Table 2, Ablation 2), removing
NLI reduced adversarial safety (scored as refusal) to 0.0% on ﬁve Category D queries,
so the grader was necessary there; the ablation does not apportion the Category C
refusals between empty-context and entailment-based refusal.
The contemporary distance-only diagnostic reproduces all eight calibration rows
in Table 12, Panel B and an AUROC of 0.9901 for Category A/B (N = 200) versus
Category C (N = 75). This diagnostic is distinct from the canonical evaluation’s
downstream refusal behavior. The preliminary 10k-index audit is not a matched-scale
comparison: its distance units and conﬁguration have not been reconciled with this
diagnostic, so the contrast does not establish a corpus-density eﬀect or scaling law.
7.3 Limitations
We state the essential limitations here; narrower caveats are noted where the
corresponding results are reported.
1. No licensed-practitioner evaluation. All faithfulness and safety scores come
from automated LLM judges and ROUGE-L against researcher-constructed refer-
ences, and cannot replicate practitioner judgment of legal correctness, temporal
consistency or citation validity; 880 of the 1,750 canonical evaluations (50.3%)
score exactly 1.0, which limits discrimination among high-scoring systems. The
NLI Smart Grader has no labelled validation set, so its false-non-entailment rate is
unquantiﬁed beyond ablation evidence, and the author spot-check in Section 6.9 is
a single non-expert review. The additional two-author assessment was AI-assisted
and non-expert; its reconciled ratings were not blinded and do not establish
legal correctness or veriﬁer error rates. Expert evaluation is a prerequisite for
deployment.
2. Shared model family. Generation, NLI grading and the Category D veri-
ﬁer, which produces the refusal, refutation, fatal-error and hedge labels, all use
databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct, whereas the two faithfulness judges
are from other families; Category D labels may therefore inherit shared Llama
biases, and a cross-family veriﬁer ablation has not been run.
3. Safety depends on curated labels. Full TERRA’s advantage on obscure over-
ruled precedents is contingent on curated OVERRULES annotations, which carry a
relation tag and a larger excerpt budget, and the obscure pairs were drawn from
these edges; the bundled ablation cannot isolate the tag, the suite has thirty items,
58


and judges were not fully blind to arm identity. Three of the 56 tagged edges were
also found mis-resolved on audit (Section 3.1); none was serialised in a deployed
context, but indirect eﬀects are not excluded.
7.4 Future Work
Future research will: (1) conduct licensed practitioner evaluation across the 350-query
benchmark; (2) scale corpus ingestion to the full federal repository; and (3) codify
OVERRULES annotations as explicit weighted graph edges with adaptive invalidation-
path traversal.
8 Conclusion
This paper introduced TERRA, an experimental dual-phase GraphRAG architec-
ture addressing knowledge drift and multi-step legal reasoning on historical SCOTUS
jurisprudence (1850–1980). Structuring 20,000 authentic decisions (145,366 citation
edges) with generic procedural scaﬀolds, TERRA enables inference via Traﬃc Cop
routing, BFS traversal, and an NLI grader evaluating retrieved evidence. The historical
multi-baseline evaluation across 350 queries (1,750 evaluations) shows higher Cate-
gory B multi-hop faithfulness than Flat Vector RAG, HippoRAG, and GraphRAG
(∆from +0.066 to +0.119; q < 0.05; the paired interval against Flat includes zero),
though not over Direct LLM (−0.081); higher overall faithfulness (0.695 vs. 0.670,
overlapping CIs; 0.586); and 97.3% adversarial safe handling (48/75(64.0%) refusals,
25/75 (33.3%) refutations). Constraints include 2/75 (2.7%) fatal errors, false refusals
on 2 Category A and 16 Category B queries, and reliance on curated overruling anno-
tations (Section 6.9). Critically, because evaluations rely on automated LLM judges
without licensed attorneys (Section 7.3), human expert review remains an essential pre-
requisite for deployment. In a standalone traversal test over four snapshot expansions,
pooled recall shifted by 0.03 ± 0.16 (Section 6.15).
In the separate prospective controlled comparison, among the 100 assessable Cat-
egory B pairs out of 100 assigned, the T-minus-H composite diﬀerence was 0.26885
(paired percentile-bootstrap 95% CI [0.16035, 0.37205]; two-sided paired randomiza-
tion p = 0.000010). T produced 69/100 veriﬁer-triggered refusals on Category B
versus 4/100 for H; this coverage tradeoﬀprevents interpreting the faithfulness diﬀer-
ence as better answer coverage or legal correctness. This evidence-faithfulness result
is conﬁned to the matched implementation and does not establish legal validity.
