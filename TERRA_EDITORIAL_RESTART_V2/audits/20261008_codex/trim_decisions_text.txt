1. Purpose and bottom line
The paper currently builds to
 
28 pages
 
in IEEEtran two-column format (it was 39 before the editorial restart; 29 after the prose compression; 28 after the layout fixes). The advisor’s ceiling is
 
25 pages including references and the appendix
. This report lists what could be removed or shrunk, what each removal saves, what the paper would lose, and which claims would be left without their supporting table. It is meant to let the reviewer decide what is expendable. Nothing below has been applied; the current paper is unchanged.
Bottom line.
 
Prose compression is largely used up: further sentence-level cuts would save well under one page and would start to remove method detail. Reaching 25 pages therefore requires removing or moving
 
about three pages of tables
. Scratch builds show that removing the appendix FDR table, the per-category refusal breakdown, the router table and the related-work comparison table brings the paper to 26 pages; adding the temporal-probe and obscure-suite tables gives 25. Alternatives are listed in Section 5.
Constraints that apply to every option (set by the author and the advisor): all 18 limitations and the adverse findings stay in the paper; no new experiments; nothing is deleted without a pointer to where the evidence is (summarise, narrow a claim, or move it to a limitation instead of dropping a contribution).
2. How the page costs were measured
Each table or figure was removed alone from the 28-page source and the paper was rebuilt (XeLaTeX, three passes). Page counts are knife-edge: a floating table that disappears often just lets the next float move up, so a single removal frequently saves nothing, and several removals together save more than the sum suggests. The numbers below are measured, not estimated, but they are for removing the float only. The sentences that cite a removed table would also need rewording, which adds back a few lines. Treat every figure as plus or minus a fraction of a page.

6. What the reviewer should weigh
Which results are the paper’s contribution?
 
The supported contributions are: higher Category B retrieval coverage and faithfulness than the three retrieval baselines (but not Direct LLM), out-of-domain refusal, and the graph and benchmark construction. Their tables are VII, VIII, XVIII and XIX. These should stay.
How much adverse evidence must remain visible as a table?
 
The obscure suite shows that safety depends on curated labels and the Direct LLM comparison shows no advantage on Category B. These are in prose and tables now. Removing the obscure table keeps the sentence but loses the numbers a reader can check.
Is multiple-testing transparency required in the paper itself?
 
The full FDR listing is the cheapest large saving, but it is the evidence for the q-values quoted in the abstract. A shortened version (the 20-row table at smaller size, or only the rows quoted) is a middle path.
Can supplementary material be used?
 
If yes, option F preserves every number and hits 25 pages with the least loss.
External benchmarks (CaseFacts, CLERC):
 
they were added to answer a circularity concern and give mixed results. They are the most natural candidates to condense if the reviewer judges the in-house benchmark enough.
The temporal probe table:
 
a verification issue (Section 3 note), so the reviewer may want it out regardless of page count.
