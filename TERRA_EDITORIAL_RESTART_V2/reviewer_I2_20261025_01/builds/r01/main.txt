Unifying Event Evolution Graphs and Generic
Procedural Scaffolds via Semantic Entailment: A
Dual-Phase Architecture for Judicial GraphRAG
Md. Emam Zafor Saadik and Md. Raihan Molla
Department of Computer Science and Engineering
Bangladesh University of Business and Technology (BUBT), Dhaka, Bangladesh
Abstract—Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for his-
torical law must retrieve connected authorities without treating
overruled doctrine as current law. We introduce TERRA,
an experimental GraphRAG pipeline combining an Event
Evolution Graph over 20,000 Supreme Court opinions, generic
five-step procedural scaffolds, EASY/HARD routing and LLM-
prompted evidence checking. Its scaffolds are not case-specific
judicial reasoning. A historical 350-query, five-system eval-
uation uses two non-Llama judges and paired tests with
Benjamini–Hochberg correction. TERRA’s Category B faith-
fulness exceeds the retrieval baselines but remains below Direct
LLM; its overall and answer-only scores describe different
refusal and answered subsets. Adversarial safe handling is
97.3%, with 2/75 (2.7%) fatal errors and false refusals on 2
Category A and 16 Category B queries. The obscure-precedent
advantage depends on curated overruling annotations. A sep-
arate prospective comparison evaluates graph retrieval (T)
against dense–BM25 retrieval (H) on the same 350 queries with
shared evidence budgets, verification, generation and judges.
On 100 Category B pairs, mean evidence faithfulness is 0.76400
for T and 0.49515 for H (paired difference 0.26885; bootstrap
95% CI [0.16035, 0.37205]; randomization p ≈0.000010). T
triggers 69/100 verifier refusals versus H’s 4/100: the gain
concerns the gated pipeline, not demonstrated answer coverage
or legal correctness. External and temporal evaluations remain
mixed. Historical configuration gaps, annotation dependence
and absent licensed-practitioner validation restrict the findings
to an experimental research framework.
Keywords—Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Large Lan-
guage Models, Temporal Knowledge Graph, Agentic Reason-
ing, Semantic Entailment, Explainable AI, Temporal Leakage

I. Introduction
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) decouples the
parametric memory of Large Language Models (LLMs)
from external knowledge bases, enabling factual grounding
and source attribution without continuous retraining. In
high-stakes settings, where errors and hallucinations carry
real consequences, the fidelity of the retrieved context is
a strict operational requirement, and naive “retrieve-then-
generate” pipelines over static vector databases and fixed-
length chunks can struggle with conflicting authorities and
multi-step evidence.
The bottleneck arises with continuous knowledge drift
and multi-step reasoning. Static vector indices can accumu-
late conflicting, time-stamped versions of the same fact;
a retriever ranking by semantic similarity may surface
several together, leaving a frozen generator without a basis
for choosing the chronologically authoritative one and
prone to state outdated law. For multi-step derivations,
similarity-based pipelines can retrieve fragments that omit
precedential and doctrinal dependencies. We motivate the
design with these mechanisms and do not measure them
in isolation.
Recent work addresses these bottlenecks in silos. Graph-
based retrievers organize facts chronologically but lack the
procedural logic to guide multi-hop generation; retrieval
of intermediate reasoning traces improves deduction but
is temporally blind and mostly confined to mathematics
or coding; and corrective Agentic RAG frameworks rely
on retrieval evaluators that may not capture conceptual
relevance, as a SHAP-based study of the CRAG T5
evaluator found it relies mainly on named-entity alignment
rather than semantic similarity [1]. Many hybrid systems
route among a small fixed set of channels, while adaptive
multi-strategy routing is emerging [2].
We therefore introduce a dual-phase GraphRAG archi-
tecture for evolving, knowledge-intensive domains (traver-
sal stability under temporal snapshot expansion is evalu-
ated in Section VI-O). Offline corpus construction builds
a dual-index corpus that cross-links an Event Evolution
Graph (EEG), which encodes precedent relationships
across 20,000 judicial decisions and 145,366 directed cita-
tion edges, with a node-level generic procedural scaffold,
a fixed five-step template instantiated per case. Online,
a small language model acts as a Traﬀic Cop router
classifying each query as EASY or HARD, selecting anchor
retrieval without graph expansion for EASY queries or
citation-graph traversal for HARD queries, which yields
a bimodal execution profile (gross median 5.83 s on
Category A factual lookups vs. 17.94 s on Category B
multi-hop chains). Before generation, an LLM-prompted
entailment grader decides whether the retrieved evidence
suﬀices; its accuracy was not independently validated.
The historical evaluation compares TERRA with four
baselines, including Direct LLM and Flat Vector RAG,
on a 350-query benchmark with a 35-query pilot ablation.
We did not test attention dilution or context-length
effects directly, and TERRA’s mean retrieved context is
longer than the retrieval baselines’ (about 18,400 versus

2,900–4,100 characters). In a 50-query temporal leakage
probe, TERRA did not leak less than Flat Vector RAG
under default prompting (the recorded hypothesis H1 is
not supported, and Flat RAG refused 23 of 50 queries)
but received more automated historical-correctness la-
bels (76.0% versus 38.0%), without expert validation. A
separate prospective dense–BM25 comparison holds the
surrounding pipeline constant (Section VI-C); its higher
T faithfulness coincides with 69/100 versus 4/100 verifier
refusals on Category B, not superior answer coverage. The
work is an experimental framework for context fidelity, not
deployment validation.
A. Summary of Contributions
• Dual-Index Architecture: a judicial GraphRAG cor-
pus cross-linking an EEG of 20,000 SCOTUS de-
cisions (145,366 citation edges) with a node-level
generic procedural scaffold (one five-step template per
case, with no case-specific issue, test or holding) and
no synthetic cases.
• Traﬀic Cop Routing with a Coarse Domain Flag:
an SLM-driven complexity router with a domain-
relevance flag derived from a title match or nearest-
neighbour proximity (dL2 < 1.3 in the current source).
This flag is not itself an early-refusal rule; the canon-
ical evaluation terminated no query before retrieval
(Sections VI-N and VII-B).
• NLI-Based Entailment Evaluation and XAI Attri-
bution: an LLM-prompted entailment grader that
evaluates retrieved evidence and gates generation,
with generic procedural scaffolds and citation prove-
nance; its interception reliability is unvalidated; a
pilot ablation included five unique adversarial queries.
• Leakage Safeguards and Audits: safeguards against
temporal precedent inversion, benchmark retrieval
contamination and cross-domain feature bleeding,
with mixed results in the leakage probe.
• Historical Empirical Evaluation: across 1,750 evalu-
ations (350 queries × 5 pipelines, T = 0.0) with
paired Wilcoxon tests, Cohen’s dz and 95% bootstrap
CIs, TERRA has the highest Answer-Only Faith-
fulness among RAG architectures (0.586 ± 0.399 vs.
HippoRAG’s 0.392 ± 0.370), outperforms Flat Vector
RAG (∆= +0.070, p = 0.0032, dz = 0.166) and
HippoRAG (∆= +0.066, p = 0.0068, dz = 0.207) on
Category B, delivers 97.3% adversarial safe handling
(73/75(97.3%): 48/75 (64.0%) refusals, 25/75 (33.3%)
refutations) with 2/75 (2.7%) fatal errors vs. 4–8 for
baselines, and incurs 2 false refusals on Category A
(16 on B). Direct LLM scores higher on Category B
(∆= −0.081), and the Flat margin’s bootstrap CI
includes zero. Evaluations rely on automated LLM
judges without licensed attorneys, making human
review an essential prerequisite (Section VII-C).
II. Related Work
Retrieval-Augmented Generation [3], [4] decoupled para-
metric memory from external knowledge, but standard
RAG struggles with knowledge drift and multi-step rea-
soning. Graph-based architectures structure retrieved evi-
dence: GraphRAG [5] builds hierarchical entity-relation
communities for global summarization, and Think-on-
Graph [6] runs iterative LLM-guided reasoning over
knowledge graphs. In temporal domains, Liu and Cao
[7] introduced a continuous-knowledge-drift benchmark
and Chronos, a time-aware method organizing evidence
into an Event Evolution Graph (EEG); Zhang et al. [8]
proposed E2RAG for temporal-causal consistency across
entity-event subgraphs; and Zhu et al. [9] developed STAR-
RAG for time-sensitive rule graph summarization. In law,
Chen et al. [10] proposed LegalGraphRAG, a Researcher,
Auditor and Adjudicator multi-agent system over a hier-
archical legal graph, and de Martim [11] introduced SAT-
Graph RAG to trace diachronic statutory norm versions
across constitutional amendments. These systems organize
sources by abstraction level or model formal amendment
events, whereas common-law systems also require tracking
how precedent evolves across citation networks, where
earlier decisions remain presumptive authority unless
distinguished, limited or overruled; and motivates testing
graph-grounded retrieval with explicit evidence checking
on historical precedent questions.
To supply that logic, recent work augments retrieval
with intermediate reasoning trajectories. CoT-RAG [12]
combines knowledge-graph-guided chain-of-thought gener-
ation with retrieval and reports gains of 4.0% to 44.3%
across nine public reasoning datasets, and surveys of
agentic reasoning [13], [14] describe pipelines in which
agents identify knowledge gaps and generate intermedi-
ate traces to inform search. Legal benchmarks include
CaseHOLD [15], which evaluates holding identification,
and CLERC [16], which evaluates U.S. case retrieval
and analysis drafting; as we read them, neither targets
superseded or overruled authority, the gap TERRA’s
evaluation addresses. LegalBench [17] broadens coverage
to 162 tasks across six types of legal reasoning. Earlier legal
NLP work includes outcome prediction for Supreme Court
cases [18] and domain-pretrained language models such as
LEGAL-BERT [19], and an audit of leading commercial
legal research tools found that they hallucinated in more
than 17% of queries [20]. Trace-retrieval frameworks have
been evaluated mainly on quantitative domains or static
statutory collections, leaving longitudinal common-law
settings largely untested.
Query routers and evaluators form the connective tissue
of the pipeline. Hashmi [2] highlighted the rigidity of single-
channel retrieval and introduced an Adaptive Hybrid
Retrieval framework, noting that pure vector similarity
often fails to capture structural navigation. Yalavarthi
[1] found through SHAP analysis that the T5-based

evaluator in Corrective RAG (CRAG) prioritizes named-
entity matching over semantic understanding and transfers
poorly to scientific questions. Putta et al. [21] showed with
CaseFacts that unrestricted web search degrades legal fact-
checking relative to closed-book baselines. Retrievers rank-
ing by lexical or embedding similarity can return overruled
precedents alongside current ones without marking which
was overturned, which motivates the explicit entailment
check tested below.
TERRA combines temporal precedent management,
generic procedural scaffolds and LLM-prompted evidence
checking in one experimental pipeline. Its node-level scaf-
folds repeat a fixed five-step template, rather than provid-
ing case-specific legal reasoning. The evaluation tests the
combined system and its trade-offs; it does not isolate the
scaffolds’ effect or establish individual-component novelty.
A binary Traﬀic Cop router selects anchor retrieval or
citation-graph traversal without retraining the foundation
model.

TERRA
is

as

and knowledge structure construction from online query
routing and inference. Citation extraction, procedural-

retrieval, HARD-route graph traversal, entailment grad-

Category A and Category B queries have different latency

A. Phase 1: Offline Ingestion and Knowledge Graph Con-
struction
1) Authentic Judicial Opinion Corpus (20,000 Cases):
TERRA operates on 20,000 full-text Supreme Court opin-
ions spanning 1850–1980 (Volumes 50–448), curated from
an initial harvest of 23,010 public-domain federal decisions
from CourtListener and the Harvard Caselaw repositories
(public domain under 17 U.S.C. § 105). To remove
non-precedential noise, opinions were filtered by length
(character count ≥4,000). Of the 131,315 decisions in
399 scanned U.S. Reports volumes, 102,235 (77.85%) are
shorter than 500 characters (procedural orders, summary
dispositions and docket records, subject to audit). The
length filter nevertheless induces bias: it excludes 8,324
intermediate decisions (500–3,999 characters, 6.34%), of
which 46 rank in the top 0.5% of citation authority
under Harvard CAP PageRank (e.g., Haines v. Kerner,
Dusky v. United States, Lucas v. Earl, Sipuel v. Board
of Regents, Gayle v. Browder). The corpus contains no
synthetic cases. A core set of 34 landmark civil rights
decisions, from Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857) through
Plessy v. Ferguson (1896), Missouri ex rel. Gaines v.
Canada (1938) and Brown v. Board of Education (1954)
to Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg (1971), anchors the
multi-hop benchmark.

3) NLI Smart Grader and Self-Correction Expansion
Loop: Before generation, C(q) is evaluated by the NLI
Smart Grader, a structured-prompt call to databricks-
meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct [24] (T = 0.0) given the query
and context and instructed to return JSON with entails
(boolean), confidence_score (float) and reasoning (string).
This allows single-call verification without a separate NLI
cross-encoder (e.g., DeBERTa [25]), while routing stays
with the 12B Gemma SLM. No labeled validation set
was built for the verifier; the only empirical evidence of
its role is the pilot ablation, in which removing the gate
(Ablation 2, Table II) lowers adversarial safety rejection
from 100.0% to 0.0%, which is consistent with the gate
contributing to safety but not a validation of its accu-
racy. False non-entailment (supported context rejected)
and false entailment (insuﬀicient context accepted) are
unquantified failure modes (Section VII-C). If entails ==
True, the context goes to the generator. If entails ==
False, a Self-Correction Expansion Loop widens retrieval
(k ←k + 2), re-queries ChromaDB and re-runs BFS.
The recovered HARD path permits two attempts in total
(initial retrieval plus one fallback), not two retries; EASY
has no expansion retry. Historical logs do not record
intermediate retry transitions. If the permitted checks fail,
the Safety Refusal Firewall halts generation with a deter-
ministic refusal: “I apologize, but I do not have suﬀicient
validated legal context in my databases to answer this
query accurately without risking hallucination.”
4) Data Leakage Mitigation Framework: TERRA ap-
plies four safeguards against leakage. (1) Temporal direc-
tionality: an earlier case cannot cite later jurisprudence,
so forward CITES edges require t(vcite) ≥t(vcited), and
retrospective invalidation uses CRS-derived OVERRULES

relations (Section III-A) rather than unrestricted back-
ward edges, providing a temporal constraint without
guaranteeing historically correct answers. (2) Vector-store
isolation: benchmark queries, rubrics and reference an-
swers are segregated from the index, which holds only
opinion text and generic procedural scaffolds. (3) Cross-
domain bleed prevention: title matching and the prox-
imity flag (which terminated no query in the canonical
run) are coarse first-line signals, and NLI verification
is designed to suppress non-entailed subgraphs before
synthesis. (4) Temporal parametric containment: to limit
post-T doctrine entering historical answers, traversals and
retrieved corpora are bounded by provenance timestamps
(KnowledgeProvenance ≤T), and candidate generations
ˆycandidate pass an entailment gate against contemporane-
ous evidence before release. In the leakage probe (Sec-
tion VI-H) the gate made six interventions, but its bulk
leakage reduction was not statistically established.

We analyze the pre-built dual-index corpus for compo-

A. Corpus Statistics and Doctrinal Taxonomy
The knowledge base holds 20,000 substantive opinions
connected by 145,366 directed citation edges across 1850–
1980 (Volumes 50–448) in seven doctrinal categories
(Table I). Each opinion receives exactly one primary
domain from a deterministic rule-based taxonomy with
an exact 69-phrase lexicon over six substantive domains:
Substantive Private Law (10 phrases, 5,707 opinions,
28.54%), Administrative Law and Federal Courts (11,
4,501, 22.50%), Criminal Procedure (13, 1,765, 8.82%),
Commerce Clause and Economic Regulation (12, 1,614,
8.07%), Civil Rights and Equal Protection (12, 1,153,
5.76%) and First Amendment (11, 666, 3.33%). The
seventh domain, General Constitutional and Statutory
(4,594 opinions, 22.97%), is an unkeyed residual fallback
assigned only when an opinion has no keyword hit in any
lexicon; positive-score ties resolve by dictionary insertion
order and never to the fallback. Opinions also carry
secondary multi-label doctrinal tags.
To audit the taxonomy without manual labelling, we
evaluated a stratified sample of N

B. Graph Topology Analysis
The EEG is sparse with high-degree authority hubs
and a heavy-tailed degree distribution, as in judicial cita-
tion networks. The largest weakly connected component
(WCC) spans 18,558 of 20,000 nodes (92.8%), so multi-
hop paths remain navigable across eras (density 0.000363,
mean degree 14.54, maximum degree 276 at Simpson
v. Shepard, average clustering 0.2047, largest strongly
connected component 2 nodes). Its diameter of 13 hops
and average shortest path of 4.305 hops indicate compact
reachability, consistent with a BFS depth of d = 2 captur-

the context.

V. Experimental Setup
This section describes the benchmark suite, baselines
and evaluation metrics.
A. Benchmark Suite Design
The evaluation suite comprises 350 unique queries evalu-
ated across 5 pipelines (1,750 evaluations; dates and model
identifiers are given under Multi-Run Provenance below),
plus a foundational N = 35 pilot ablation set (5 repeated
trials per query, 700 executions across four configurations),
across four categories isolating factual accuracy, multi-
hop reasoning, domain safety and adversarial robustness.
Temperature T=0.0 was used throughout but does not
guarantee identical outputs across runs (Section VII-C).
Faithfulness scores cover all four categories, including
refusals; ROUGE-L evaluates generative Categories A
and B, and safety labels evaluate Categories C and D:
• Category
A
—

(decision

scored

• Category B — Multi-Hop Evolutionary: multi-decade
precedent-tracking queries scored against researcher-
constructed reference answers with ROUGE-L. To
test non-trivial traversal while reflecting the hub
topology of citation networks, all chains span 3–5
nodes (2–4 hops): 63 three-node, 32 four-node and 5
five-node arcs, with no 2-node pairs. Category B gold
paths derive from the EEG’s own citations (circularity
risk), and Categories A and B use three and one
question templates.
• Category C — Out-of-Context Safety: 75 queries
(N = 15 each) on topics absent from indexed Supreme
Court law (1850–1980): natural sciences, foreign and
treaty law, state and municipal ordinances, post-2020
software and culture, and corporate accounting. With
no controlling precedent, the required behavior is
refusal.
• Category D — Adversarial Deception: queries pairing
real in-domain case titles with fabricated premises or
temporal falsehoods (e.g., governing the 1905 Lochner
ruling by the 1938 Fair Labor Standards Act), built
to trick entity-matching routers. A safe response is
an explicit refusal or a verified refutation of the false
premise citing authentic authority.
Task Scope vs. Background Corpus. The EEG covers
the full 20,000-case docket across seven areas (including
5,707 Substantive Private Law and 4,594 General Statu-
tory decisions; Table I), but Category B concentrates
on constitutional and federal public-law trajectories (20
queries each across Civil Rights, Criminal Procedure, First
Amendment, Commerce Clause and Administrative Law;
100.0%). Of the 275 in-domain queries (Categories A, B
and D), 232 (84.4%) are constitutional or public law,
23 (8.4%) private law and 20 (7.3%) general statutory.
Private-law disputes were not modeled as 3–5 node cita-
tion chains, whereas constitutional jurisprudence shows
longitudinal evolution and explicit overruling; retaining
the full repository gives a high-distractor environment
where constitutional reasoning must avoid 51.5% commer-
cial and statutory background nodes.
Category C and D Construction. The 75 Category C
queries were pre-registered across five domains (Subtypes
C1–C5) before evaluation, and an automated audit against
the indexed repository found 0/75 title, citation or holding

overlaps. The 75 Category D queries were built by the
research team under a pre-registered taxonomy: D1 (N =
19) explicit overruled-precedent traps, D2 (N = 19) title
substitution, D3 (N = 19) temporal inversion with a ≥5-
year gap, and D4 (N = 18) procedural-posture deceptions,
sampled with RANDOM_SEED = 42 from 19 verified
doctrine-invalidation pairs. All queries were locked into
terra_350_benchmark.json (SHA256: 8144fa81...) before
any pipeline ran, with no queries or references indexed
into ChromaDB (Section III-B4); the domain-labeling
protocol and Category C catalog are identified in the local
supplementary documentation; public availability is not
certified here. A standalone 15-query stress test (10 out-

B. Comparative Baselines
TERRA is benchmarked against four representative
baseline architectures spanning parametric and retrieval-
augmented paradigms: (1) Direct LLM (No RAG), where
query q is passed directly to the generator without
retrieval, evaluating raw parametric knowledge and un-
gated hallucination risks; (2) Flat Vector RAG, where
ChromaDB retrieves top-k scaffold-bearing opinion doc-
uments by semantic embedding similarity (nresults = 2)
and passes them directly to the generator without citation
graph traversal or NLI entailment grading; (3) GraphRAG-
Adapted (Louvain implementation), adapting Microsoft
GraphRAG [5] by partitioning opinion entities into Lou-
vain graph communities (Q = 0.6110) and generating
responses from local subgraph plus parent community sum-
maries, omitting directed chronological precedent edges;
and (4) HippoRAG-Adapted, adapting hippocampal mem-
ory indexing [26] by constructing a symmetric bipartite
citation graph (290,706 non-zero entries) and executing
Personalized PageRank (PPR) associative diffusion seeded
from query entity mentions, without typed directional
citation constraints or downstream NLI self-correction
gates.

H. Temporal Parametric Leakage Stress Test
Models pretrained on post-T legal history risk temporal
parametric leakage, anachronistically importing later doc-
trinal shifts or overrulings into analyses framed at cutoff T.
We ran an exploratory 50-query probe across five horizons
(T ∈{1890, 1930, 1950, 1970, 1980}), spanning seven doc-
trinal domains and balanced across case prominence and
reasoning depth. Six probes (12%) target cases decided
between 1857 and 1905 (cutoffs T = 1890 and 1930)
whose nodes have zero out-edges in the EEG owing to the
citation-parsing boundary; their arcs are verified against
primary sources but are not EEG edges, and all metrics
are computed identically across the 50 probes.
1) Design, Metrics and Evaluator Reliability:
The
probe evaluates 400 responses from Direct LLM, Flat
Vector RAG, TERRAungated (Lgen) and TERRAgated
(Lfinal), each under Condition A (standard prompt) and
Condition B (explicit instruction to adhere to year T),
under a recorded 3-tier rubric: Level 0 (clean), Level 1
(non-prejudicial mention or disclaimer) and Level 2 (fatal
reliance on post-T
law). The primary metric is the
Substantive Post-T Leakage Rate (SPTLR), the share of
Level 2 failures. Because refusing systems generate no post-
T text and look “leak-free”, we decouple containment from
problem-solving with the Refusal Rate and the Historical
Legal Correctness Rate (HLCR), an automated rubric
label for whether the system states the operative rule
as of T, not expert-certified correctness, and we measure
the NLI Gate Leakage Interception Rate (NGLIR), the
share of ungated leaking candidates intercepted by the
gate (denominator D).
Responses were judged by qwen3-next-80b and gpt-oss-
120b. Over N = 315 complete pairs the judges reached
68.6% raw concordance and 100% monotonic inclusion
(every GPT-OSS leak was within Qwen’s flags), but
quadratic weighted Cohen’s κ on the three-level severity
rating was 0.260, lower than elsewhere (e.g., κ = 0.5746
on the obscure suite) and consistent with the Kappa
Paradox [29]: GPT-OSS rated 91.7% of responses Level 0
(289/315), inflating chance agreement to ≈60%. Qwen
also flagged any passing historical reference as Level 1
or 2, whereas GPT-OSS called a response clean if post-
T references did not alter the outcome. Evaluations were
reconciled by the recorded 5-tier protocol: (1) safe refusals
resolve to Level 0; (2) unanimity is preserved; (3) lexical
breach of forbidden phrases and (4) substantive quotation
of post-T law assign Level 2; (5) subtle mentions resolve
to max(s1, s2) = Level 1. Tiers 3–5 bias toward flagging
leakage, so adjudication is conservative against TERRA.

Antitrust (23.2%, 13/56) and Commerce (23.2%, 13/56);
Direct LLM accounted for 30 of the 85. Its complete-
case Condition A leakage was 28.0%, but the direction of
missingness bias is unknown. We report the retained sen-
sitivity envelopes [SPTLRlower, SPTLRupper] under ex-

3) Primary Hypothesis H1: Metric Decoupling and
Refusal Masking: The recorded H1 predicted lower raw
SPTLR for TERRAgated than Flat Vector RAG un-
der Condition A. It is not supported: complete cases
(Ncc = 36) give b = 0, c = 3 (one-sided exact McNemar
p = 1.0000; two-sided p = 0.2500, or 0.0625 if miss-

are b ∈[0, 5], c ∈[7, 8], and Flat RAG’s raw leakage
([2.0%, 14.0%]) is lower than TERRA’s ([18.0%, 18.0%]).
The discordant pairs show why: Flat RAG produced
no leaks where TERRA was clean (b = 0) because it
refused 46.0% of queries (23/50), and a system that
refuses cannot leak, whereas TERRA engaged (refusal
rate 8.0%) and incurred c = 3 complete-case Level-2
classifications on complex multi-jurisdictional tests. On
HLCR, TERRAgated reaches 76.0% automated historical-
correctness labels against 38.0% for Flat RAG (b = 20, c =
1; exact McNemar p = 2.1 × 10−5, p < 0.0001); The
leakage contrast must be read alongside refusal rates; these
observations do not isolate refusal as a causal explanation.
4) Hypotheses H2–H6, Directional Stability and Gate
Attribution: For H2 (TERRA vs. Direct LLM, Condition
A), complete cases (Ncc = 32) give b = 2, c = 4; bounding
over the 18 missing Direct LLM evaluations gives b ∈
[11, 16], c ∈[5, 6] (p ∈[0.0133, 0.1662]). Since cmax = 6 <
bmin = 11, the sign is stable (Directional Stability) under

b = 1, c = 1; even when instructed, Direct LLM leaks
([26.0%, 32.0%]); bounds are b ∈[8, 11], c ∈[2, 6] (p ∈

Benchmark
Generalization
and
Anti-
Circularity Audit (CaseFacts)
Evaluating only on researcher-constructed queries risks
circular alignment or benchmark overfitting, so we also
evaluate on CaseFacts [21], a legal fact-checking dataset
built from U.S. Supreme Court decisions by an external
team.
1) Anti-Circularity and Graph Substrate Audit:
Of
the 20,000 nodes, exactly 34 landmark decisions (0.17%,

34/20, 000) received curated reasoning traces during early
development (not in the evaluated index), whereas the
350-query benchmark touches 284 distinct nodes (1.42%,
284/20, 000; 100 in Category A, 151 in B, 0 in C, 58 in D,
with 25 nodes of multi-category overlap), leaving 98.58%
of the substrate (19, 716/20, 000 nodes) unvisited. The 19
Category-D queries map to 9 distinct edges among the 56
tagged overrulings (list from CRS R45319 via a Wikipedia
mirror; 53 audited as consistent, 3 mis-resolved and unused
by Category D); this shows they are recognised overrulings,
not unfitted selection. Category B chains span 3 to 5 nodes
(2 to 4 hops): 63 have 3 nodes, 32 have 4 and 5 have 5, and
Full TERRA’s full-path recovery falls with chain length
(77.8%, 50.0% and 20.0%), so performance is not invariant
across lengths.
2) Splits, Protocol and Findings: We use two splits:
Track 1 (N = 120; 67 Supported, 45 Refuted and 8
Overruled claims anchored in pre-1980 decisions, 1850 ≤
T ≤1980) and Track 2 (N = 50; 50 modern decisions
from 1982–2023, e.g., Citizens United v. FEC and United
States v. Windsor) as an out-of-corpus refusal control.
All 510 evaluations (360 Track 1, 150 Track 2 across
Direct LLM, Flat Vector RAG and Full TERRA) used
strict payload parity (T = 0.0, max tokens 4096, identical
LLaMA 3.3 70B generator) and two non-LLaMA judges
(qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct, gpt-oss-120b); the judges
agreed on 100.0% of classification labels parsed from each
completion’s header (not an independent legal judgement),
with zero LLaMA fallbacks.
3) Empirical Findings: Track 1 (Table VIII, Panel A)
is mixed. Full TERRA’s three-class Macro-F1 is 54.48%,
above Flat Vector RAG (48.26%; +6.22 pp) and Direct
LLM (46.51%; +7.97 pp), but the gain comes entirely from
the Overruled class (n = 8): over Supported and Refuted
alone the macro is 53.16% for TERRA against 55.72% for
Flat and 51.59% for Direct, TERRA’s Supported F1 is
the lowest of the three, and its Refuted F1 is below Flat’s.
Accuracy, counting out-of-corpus predictions as errors,
is lowest for TERRA (62/120 against 64/120 for each
baseline); the NLI gate converted 25 borderline in-corpus
queries into out-of-corpus refusals when retrieved context
was legally incomplete. As a diagnostic with exact Clopper-
Pearson 95% intervals, TERRA identifies 4/8(50.0%)
Overruled cases (95% CI: [15.70%, 84.30%]), double Flat

L. Direct Evidence Retrieval Quality and Precedent-
Chain Coverage
Do downstream generative results reflect better evi-
dence retrieval, or only the generator’s ability to synthesize
plausible analyses? Because TERRA’s expansion uses
breadth k = 2 and depth d = 2 while Category B arcs
span 3 to 5 nodes (2 to 4 hops), we evaluate retrieval
as a function of chain length. To avoid conflating single-
point lookups (routed through anchor retrieval and entail-
ment grading without graph expansion) or unanswerable
probes, we score raw evidence retrieval on the N = 100
Category B trajectories (342 gold landmark precedents)
before generation. The four systems return heterogeneous
representations (ranked chunks, unranked community re-
ports, unranked PPR clusters, and a 2-hop directed EEG
subgraph), so ranked-list metrics such as MRR or nDCG
cannot be applied uniformly; we report deterministic dual-
resolution entity recall (CAP Node ID and canonical title)
and full-target coverage in Table X, computed on the
stored context_full fields of the canonical v2 file (SHA256
prefix 95e775e2) that the generator actually received. Here
“full-path recovery” denotes matching every gold target by
ID or title; connected-path recovery was not independently
verified.

O. Benchmark-Graph Traversal Stability Under Temporal
Snapshot Expansion
To test traversal stability under corpus growth without
generative confounding, we run standalone breadth-first
traversal over four snapshot expansions: 1890 →1930
(Step N = 40 yr), 1930 →1950 (20 yr), 1950 →1970
(20 yr) and 1970 →1980 (10 yr). Over N = 106 qualifying
Category B observations (82 unique queries anchored at an
origin decided at or before T, with at least one later gold
authority in (T, T +N]), BFS traversal (k = 2, d = 2) was
run on G≤T and G≤T +N seeded by the origin precedent
s = q[target_nodes][0]. Grand pooled recall moved from
0.35 ± 0.13 to 0.38 ± 0.14 (∆Recall = 0.03 ± 0.16), with
90.6% of queries showing ∆Recall ≥0, a Discovery
Rate on newly eligible authorities of 0.12, and median
traversal latency under one millisecond (< 0.01 ms; mean
0.15±0.18 ms); by snapshot pair, ∆Recall is −0.10±0.25
(1890 →1930), 0.01 ± 0.15 (1930 →1950), 0.08 ± 0.14
(1950 →1970) and −0.00 ± 0.10 (1970 →1980), and the
full per-snapshot table is in the supplementary material.
Three boundaries apply: absolute recall includes the depth-
0 seed (while ∆Recall is invariant to that offset); negative
shifts stem from degree caps when expanding nodes
become hubs; and the experiment evaluates topology over
generic CITES edges on NetworkX DiGraphs, decoupled
from vector retrieval (Section VII-C) and from live index
upsert, which remains future work.
VII. Discussion
The historical evaluation indicates gains in Category B
target coverage and out-of-domain refusal, with weak-
nesses against Direct LLM and on obscure precedents with-
out curated labels. The prospective hybrid comparison
instead measures a shared gated pipeline: higher T faith-
fulness coincides with 69/100 versus 4/100 Category B
verifier refusals, so it does not establish better answer
coverage or legal correctness.
A. Architectural Differentiation and System Scope
Compared with Microsoft Research’s GraphRAG [5],
which builds static hierarchical entity-relation communi-
ties for dataset summarization, TERRA models precedent
evolution through dated citation edges (CITES) and typed
OVERRULES edges imported from CRS annotations (56
tagged, 3 of them found mis-resolved on audit; the 19
Category D queries cover 9 distinct edges; Section III-A),
together with node-level generic procedural scaffolds; live
updates are untested. The NLI Smart Grader is designed
to reduce unsupported generations, although residual
errors remain [27], [30]. The offline pipeline indexes full-
docket Supreme Court jurisprudence (1850–1980) as a
high-distractor graph, while the evaluated online task is
longitudinal precedent tracking, temporal drift, and safety
over constitutional and federal public-law doctrine. Three
design choices distinguish TERRA, examined only in a
35-query pilot ablation. First, domain-boundary safety
pairs lexical anchoring with downstream NLI gating and
an optional, scale-dependent geometric proximity flag
that terminated no query in the canonical run; 74 of
75 Category C refusals followed empty retrieval. Second,
entailment-gated generation favours refusal over unsup-
ported answers (16 of 100 Category B queries falsely
refused), qualified by the 94.5% citation-extraction preci-
sion of the underlying graph. Third, chronological CITES
edges alongside typed OVERRULES relations let TERRA
model precedent shifts only where curated annotations
exist.
B. Empirical Analysis of Firewall Component Contribu-
tions and Scale Dynamics
The domain helper combines an indexed-title match
with a strict distance-below-1.30 condition; the distance-
only diagnostic flags the complementary boundary. A pre-
liminary 50-query Category C audit on the initial 10,009-
opinion index flagged 0/50 queries by distance (reported
range 0.281–0.859, units unverified). Safety in Table IV
(100.0% refusal on Category C; 64.0% refusal and 33.3%
verified refutation on Category D) was therefore delivered
by downstream refusal rather than by the proximity gate.
In the canonical run, 81 of the 139 refusals occurred
on an empty retrieved context (74 in Category C, 7 in
Category D) and 58 on a non-empty one (2, 16, 1 and 39 in
Categories A–D). In the 35-query pilot ablation (Table II,
Ablation 2), removing NLI reduced adversarial safety

(scored as refusal) to 0.0% on five Category D queries,
so the grader was necessary there; the ablation does not
apportion the Category C refusals between empty-context
and entailment-based refusal.
The contemporary distance-only diagnostic reproduces
all eight calibration rows in Table XII, Panel B and an
AUROC of 0.9901 for Category A/B (N = 200) versus
Category C (N = 75). This diagnostic is distinct from
the canonical evaluation’s downstream refusal behavior.
The preliminary 10k-index audit is not a matched-scale
comparison: its distance units and configuration have not
been reconciled with this diagnostic, so the contrast does
not establish a corpus-density effect or scaling law.
C. Limitations
We state the essential limitations here; narrower caveats
are noted where the corresponding results are reported.
1) No licensed-practitioner evaluation. All faithfulness
and safety scores come from automated LLM judges
and ROUGE-L against researcher-constructed refer-
ences, and cannot replicate practitioner judgment of
legal correctness, temporal consistency or citation va-
lidity; 880 of the 1,750 canonical evaluations (50.3%)
score exactly 1.0, which limits discrimination among
high-scoring systems. The NLI Smart Grader has
no labelled validation set, so its false-non-entailment
rate is unquantified beyond ablation evidence, and
the author spot-check in Section VI-I is a single
non-expert review. The additional two-author assess-
ment was AI-assisted and non-expert; its reconciled
ratings were not blinded and do not establish legal
correctness or verifier error rates. Expert evaluation
is a prerequisite for deployment.
2) Shared model family. Generation, NLI grading and
the Category D verifier, which produces the re-
fusal, refutation, fatal-error and hedge labels, all
use databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct, whereas
the two faithfulness judges are from other families;
Category D labels may therefore inherit shared
Llama biases, and a cross-family verifier ablation
has not been run.
3) Safety depends on curated labels. Full TERRA’s
advantage on obscure overruled precedents is contin-
gent on curated OVERRULES annotations, which
carry a relation tag and a larger excerpt budget,
and the obscure pairs were drawn from these edges;
the bundled ablation cannot isolate the tag, the suite
has thirty items, and judges were not fully blind to
arm identity. Three of the 56 tagged edges were also
found mis-resolved on audit (Section III-A); none
was serialised in a deployed context, but indirect
effects are not excluded.
D. Future Work
Future research will: (1) conduct licensed practitioner
evaluation across the 350-query benchmark; (2) scale
corpus ingestion to the full federal repository; and (3) cod-
ify OVERRULES annotations as explicit weighted graph
edges with adaptive invalidation-path traversal.
VIII. Conclusion
This paper introduced TERRA, an experimental dual-
phase GraphRAG architecture addressing knowledge drift
and multi-step legal reasoning on historical SCOTUS
jurisprudence (1850–1980). Structuring 20,000 authentic
decisions (145,366 citation edges) with generic procedural
scaffolds, TERRA enables inference via Traﬀic Cop rout-
ing, BFS traversal, and an NLI grader evaluating retrieved
evidence. The historical multi-baseline evaluation across
350 queries (1,750 evaluations) shows higher Category B
multi-hop faithfulness than Flat Vector RAG, HippoRAG,
and GraphRAG (∆from +0.066 to +0.119; q < 0.05;
the paired interval against Flat includes zero), though
not over Direct LLM (−0.081); higher overall faithfulness
(0.695 vs. 0.670, overlapping CIs; 0.586); and 97.3%
adversarial safe handling (48/75(64.0%) refusals, 25/75
(33.3%) refutations). Constraints include 2/75 (2.7%) fatal
errors, false refusals on 2 Category A and 16 Category B
queries, and reliance on curated overruling annotations
(Section VI-I). Critically, because evaluations rely on
automated LLM judges without licensed attorneys (Sec-
tion VII-C), human expert review remains an essential
prerequisite for deployment. In a standalone traversal test
over four snapshot expansions, pooled recall shifted by
0.03 ± 0.16 (Section VI-O).
In the separate prospective controlled comparison,
among the 100 assessable Category B pairs out of 100
assigned, the T-minus-H composite difference was 0.26885
(paired percentile-bootstrap 95% CI [0.16035, 0.37205];
two-sided paired randomization p = 0.000010). T pro-
duced 69/100 verifier-triggered refusals on Category B
versus 4/100 for H; this coverage tradeoff prevents in-
terpreting the faithfulness difference as better answer
coverage or legal correctness. This evidence-faithfulness
result is confined to the matched implementation and does
not establish legal validity.
