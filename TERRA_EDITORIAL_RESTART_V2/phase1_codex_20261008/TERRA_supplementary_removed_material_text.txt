II. Original eighteen limitations (full text, removed from
the paper)
We identify eighteen methodological limitations:
1) Absence of Licensed Legal Practitioner Evaluation
(Most Critical for Deployment): owing to geograph-
ical and institutional constraints, no licensed practi-
tioner evaluated the outputs. All faithfulness and
safety assessments rely on automated LLM-as-a-
judge scoring (Section ??) and ROUGE-L against
researcher-constructed references cross-referenced to
public sources (Cornell LII, Oyez, CRS Constitu-
tion Annotated). Dual-judge faithfulness correlates
only modestly with lexical grounding (Pearson r =
0.555, p = 1
10001 ≈ 9.999×10−5) and cannot replicate
practitioner judgment of legal correctness, tempo-
ral consistency, citation validity and hallucination.
Lacking a labeled validation set for the NLI Smart
Grader (Section ??), its false-non-entailment rate is
unquantified beyond ablation evidence. We consider
expert evaluation of output quality and verifier reli-
ability an essential prerequisite for deployment and
release the benchmark and code to invite replication.
The author spot-check in Section ?? is a single non-
expert review and no substitute.
2) Metric Disentanglement and Refusal-Volume Mask-
ing: aggregate faithfulness rewards non-committal
answers on unanswerable queries and can mask gen-
erative weaknesses. TERRA has the highest overall
(0.695 vs. 0.670; intervals overlap) and answer-only
faithfulness ( 0.586 vs. 0.392) with fewer flagged
refusals ( 139 vs. 165), but answer-only scores con-
dition on different answered subsets, and it leads
HippoRAG on Category B by +0.066 (q = 0 .0124).
Benchmarks should report refusal rates alongside
answer-only faithfulness.
3) Score Ceiling and Rubric Granularity: 880 of the
1,750 canonical evaluations ( 50.3%) score exactly
1.0 under the five-point Likert rubric normalized
to [0, 1], which limits discrimination among high-
scoring systems.
4) Jurisdictional and Temporal Corpus Boundaries: the
EEG covers 20,000 U.S. Supreme Court opinions
spanning 1850–1980 in seven doctrinal domains
(Table ??); state, municipal, foreign and post-1980
law require further ingestion. Although Section ??
shows traversal stability under snapshot expansion
(T → T + N ), live vector-index insertion and
streaming graph maintenance remain unaddressed.
5) Hosted API Output Variability at T = 0.0, Run-to-
Run Identity, and Sensitivity Verification: primary
evaluations used greedy decoding with databricks-
meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct and databricks-gemma-
3-12b, but hosted APIs can vary across serving
sessions; query D066 shifted from EASY to HARD,
consistent with serving-level variability whose cause
cannot be isolated from the logs. Between two 14-
September TERRA executions, answers were string-
identical on 58.9% of queries (206/350): 95.0% on
Category A (95/100), 82.7% on C (62/75), 52.0% on
D (39/75) and 10.0% on B (10/100), with generation
and retrieval paths differing as documented. Both
were judged under the dual-judge protocol with the
frozen RAG prompt (SHA256: 6d18fc14…). A sensi-
tivity check with an 80B model (databricks-qwen3-
next-80b-a3b-instruct) on a separate 15-query subset
(10 Category B, 5 Category D) gave the same
ordering (no statistical test; not re-verified): TERRA
1.000 Category B faithfulness (Flat RAG 0.900,
Direct LLM 0.800) and 100% Category D safety
rejection (Flat RAG 40%, Direct LLM 0%). All anal-
yses are bound to SHA256-verified raw completion
archives; rerunning the endpoint may yield different
completions.
6) Parametric Pretraining Confounders, Latent
Weights, and Temporal Containment Bounds: on
continuous Category D faithfulness, Direct LLM
scores higher ( 0.850 vs. 0.650) and the paired
Wilcoxon test significantly favours it ( ∆ = −0.200,

refutations ( 71/75) with 0% refusal on all 350
queries and 0/75 on Category C, whereas TERRA
reaches 97.3% (73/75: 48/75(64.0%) refusals,

Truncation, and Residual Fallback Bias: the seven-

lexicon over texts truncated to ≤ 4, 000 charac-

adjudicator (34.15% weighted accuracy, 95% CI:
[25.27%, 43.02%]), not practitioner annotation, so
labels are not certified human ground truth.
