\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in law must trace multi-hop precedent chains and must not present overruled doctrine as current law, yet common evaluators reward entity overlap rather than grounded reasoning. We introduce TERRA (Temporal-Evolution and Reasoning-Trace RAG), an experimental dual-phase GraphRAG architecture. Offline, it builds an Event Evolution Graph over \CorpusTotalOpinions\ Supreme Court opinions connected by \CorpusPrecedentEdges\ directed citation edges, plus a procedural reasoning-trace index. Online, a small-model Traffic Cop router sends single-point lookups down a fast path and multi-hop questions through citation traversal, and an autonomous semantic entailment grader evaluates retrieved evidence before generation. We evaluate on a \numUniqueQueries-query benchmark against four baselines (\numTotalEvals\ evaluations) using two non-Llama judges, paired Wilcoxon tests and Benjamini--Hochberg correction. TERRA scores higher overall faithfulness ($\TerraOverallFaithfulnessMean$ vs.\ $\HippoRAGOverallFaithfulnessMean$ for the next best system, overlapping 95\% CIs) and answer-only faithfulness ($\TerraAnswerOnlyFaithfulnessMean$, computed on differing answered subsets), and significantly outperforms all three retrieval baselines on Category~B multi-hop reasoning ($\Delta$ from $\CatBTerraVsHippoRAGDelta$ [HippoRAG] and $\CatBTerraVsFlatDelta$ [Flat] to $\CatBTerraVsGraphRAGDelta$ [GraphRAG]; FDR $q<0.05$), although a no-retrieval Direct LLM scores significantly higher on Category~B ($\Delta=\CatBTerraVsDirectLLMDelta$, $q=\CatBTerraVsDirectLLMAdjQ$). On adversarial Category~D queries TERRA safely handles \TerraCatDSafeHandlingRate\ of traps (\TerraCatDRejectionRate\ explicit refusals, \TerraCatDRefutationRate\ verified refutations) with \TerraCatDFatalErrors\ fatal errors, but refuses \TerraCatAFalseRefusals\ Category~A and \TerraCatBFalseRefusals\ Category~B queries, at \TerraLatencyMedianSeconds~s median latency. On \ObscCohortSize\ obscure overruled precedents, Direct LLM safe handling falls to \ObscDirectSafePct\ versus \ObscFullSafePct\ for full TERRA, but TERRA without curated overruling labels drops to \ObscUnlabeledSafePct, not significantly different from flat retrieval ($p\,\ObscPrimaryMcNemarPRel$); safety depends on curated annotations. Absent licensed attorney evaluation, human review remains an essential deployment prerequisite.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:Introduction}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has rapidly evolved into a foundational architecture for knowledge-intensive artificial intelligence. By explicitly decoupling the parametric memory of Large Language Models (LLMs) from dynamic external knowledge bases, RAG enables real-time factual grounding and transparent source attribution without the prohibitive computational costs of continuous model retraining. In high-stakes, knowledge-intensive environments---where factual errors and hallucinations carry severe real-world consequences---the fidelity of the retrieved context is not merely an optimization preference, but a strict operational requirement. As enterprise applications scale, the early paradigm of naive ``retrieve-then-generate'' pipelines characterized by static vector databases and fixed-length document chunking has proven structurally inadequate.

The fundamental bottleneck in modern RAG systems arises when navigating complex data landscapes characterized by continuous knowledge drift and multi-step reasoning demands. Real-world data evolves dynamically; consequently, static vector indices inevitably accumulate conflicting, time-stamped versions of the same fact. When queried, standard retrievers surface all of these conflicting versions simultaneously based on semantic similarity, leaving the frozen generative model unable to resolve the chronological authority, which induces severe temporal hallucinations. Furthermore, when processing complex logical derivations, standard pipelines retrieve fragmented textual snippets that sever precedential and doctrinal dependencies, proving entirely insufficient for tasks that require deep, multi-hop reasoning.

Recent advancements have attempted to address these bottlenecks in isolated silos, exposing critical failure modes. While structural graph-based retrievers organize facts chronologically, they act merely as static data foundations lacking the procedural logic to guide multi-hop generative reasoning. Conversely, prior paradigms retrieving intermediate structured reasoning traces significantly enhance logical deduction but remain temporally blind and have been primarily restricted to quantitative domains like mathematics or coding. More critically, current Agentic RAG frameworks rely heavily on corrective evaluators that fail fundamentally on conceptual tasks. Mathematical explainability analyses, such as SHAP, have proven that standard evaluators act merely as superficial named entity alignment detectors, failing to comprehend genuine semantic relevance. Compounding this, existing query routers are largely restricted to rigid, binary-channel systems (e.g., pure dense versus pure sparse search), resulting in severe evidence-intent mismatches when unrestricted search floods the context window with noisy, irrelevant data.

To bridge these isolated gaps, this paper introduces an integrated dual-phase GraphRAG architecture purpose-built for dynamically evolving, knowledge-intensive environments (with empirical graph traversal stability across temporal snapshot expansions evaluated in Section~\ref{subsec:temporal-update-stability}). The proposed system confines all computationally heavy operations to an offline preprocessing phase, constructing a dual-index corpus that cross-links an Event Evolution Graph (EEG)---which encodes chronological precedent relationships across \CorpusTotalOpinions\ authentic judicial decisions and \CorpusPrecedentEdges\ directed citation edges---with a node-level procedural reasoning trace database that captures structured legal analysis trajectories (constitutional issue, procedural history, legal test, holding). During online inference, a small language model acts as a Traffic Cop router, dynamically allocating retrieval priority across text chunks, graph subgraphs, and procedural reasoning traces, enabling a bimodal execution profile (median \TerraFastPathMedianSeconds~s on single-point factual lookups vs. \TerraHardPathMedianSeconds~s on multi-hop precedent chains). Before the final response generation, an autonomous semantic entailment grader replaces flawed keyword-matching heuristics, verifying the true conceptual and structural relevance of the retrieved evidence.

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
The primary contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{An Authentic Dual-Index Architecture:} We design and implement an authentic dual-index judicial GraphRAG corpus that cross-links a structural Event Evolution Graph (EEG) of \CorpusTotalOpinions\ authentic SCOTUS decisions (\CorpusPrecedentEdges\ citation edges) with a node-level procedural reasoning trace database, supporting joint chronological and logical evidence alignment with zero synthetic cases.
    \item \textbf{Traffic Cop Routing with Coarse Domain Pre-filtering:} We introduce an SLM-driven complexity router paired with a two-stage coarse proximity pre-filter ($d_{L2} < 1.3$ + Title Match) that provides scale-dependent domain boundary filtering without maintaining static allowlists (empirical coverage and calibration detailed in Section~\ref{subsec:computational-budget} and Section~\ref{subsec:firewall-analysis}).
    \item \textbf{NLI-Based Entailment Evaluation and XAI Attribution:} We replace superficial keyword-matching heuristics with a specialized Natural Language Inference (NLI) grader that evaluates retrieved evidence to intercept ungrounded generations, providing qualitative reasoning traces and citation provenance.
    \item \textbf{Data Leakage Mitigation Framework:} We formalize explicit safeguards against temporal precedent inversion, benchmark retrieval contamination, and cross-domain feature bleeding.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Rigor and Statistical Validation:} Through benchmarking (\numTotalEvals\ evaluations across \numUniqueQueries\ queries $\times$ \numComparativePipelines\ pipelines at $T=0.0$) with paired Wilcoxon tests, Cohen's $d_z$, and 95\% bootstrap CIs, TERRA achieves the highest Answer-Only Faithfulness among RAG architectures ($\TerraAnswerOnlyFaithfulness$ vs.\ HippoRAG's $\HippoRAGAnswerOnlyFaithfulness$), outperforms Flat Vector RAG ($\Delta = \CatBTerraVsFlatDelta$, $p = \CatBTerraVsFlatWilcoxonP$, $d_z = \CatBTerraVsFlatDz$) and HippoRAG ($\Delta = \CatBTerraVsHippoRAGDelta$, $p = \CatBTerraVsHippoRAGWilcoxonP$, $d_z = \CatBTerraVsHippoRAGDz$) on Category~B reasoning, delivers $\TerraCatDSafeHandlingRate$ adversarial safe handling ($\TerraCatDSafeHandling$: \TerraCatDRejection\ refusals, \TerraCatDRefutations\ refutations) with \TerraCatDFatalErrors\ fatal errors vs.\ $\BaselineFatalMin$--$\BaselineFatalMax$ for baselines, and incurs \TerraCatAFalseRefusals\ false refusals on Category~A (\TerraCatBFalseRefusals\ on Category~B). Evaluations rely on automated LLM judges without licensed attorneys, establishing human review as an essential prerequisite (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}).

\end{itemize}

\section{Results and Empirical Evaluation}
\label{sec:results-analysis}

To rigorously evaluate TERRA GraphRAG, we adopt an ablation-first empirical methodology. Rather than presenting aggregated metrics that obscure internal component dynamics, we first dissect the architecture into its constituent mechanisms to isolate the empirical contribution of each subsystem. We then evaluate multi-hop evolutionary reasoning via pairwise win rates and semantic faithfulness, examine factual precision and safety firewalling across the complete \numTotalEvals-evaluation benchmark, profile stage-level latency distributions, and conduct formal statistical significance and power audits.

\subsection{Architectural Component Ablation and System Anatomy}
\label{subsec:ablation-study}

To quantify the direct empirical contribution of each architectural component, the ablation results reports the component-level ablation matrix evaluated across the foundational \numPilotQueries-query benchmark suite ($N=\numPilotRepeatedRuns$ repeated runs, \numPilotEvals\ evaluations). We note that this ablation study evaluates component degradation over repeated runs on the focused 35-query pilot subset (reporting Full TERRA Category B faithfulness as $\AblationFullCatBFaithfulness$ and Category D safety as $\AblationFullCatDSafetyRate$ on $n=\IssueTwentyFourDevCatDCount$ unique adversarial traps, 25 run observations). This configuration isolates architectural drops under repeated sampling prior to full scaling, whereas the primary $\numUniqueQueries$-query benchmark (Sections~\ref{subsec:evolutionary-reasoning} and \ref{subsec:safety-and-factual}, Tables~\ref{tab:cat_b_performance} and \ref{tab:overall_summary}) evaluates the expanded 100-query Category B suite ($\TerraCatBFaithfulness$) and broader 75-query Category D suite in a single-run protocol ($T=0.0$), where TERRA achieves an explicit refusal count of $\TerraCatDRejection$ alongside $\TerraCatDRefutationRate$ verified refutations ($\TerraCatDRefutations$), for $\TerraCatDSafeHandlingRate$ total safe handling ($\TerraCatDSafeHandling$); the two fatal errors ($\TerraCatDFatalErrors$, D033 and D034) are procedural misattributions (Table~\ref{tab:refusal_breakdown}). Category~D labels come from a calibrated semantic verifier served by the same model family as the generator (Limitation~\ref{item:samefamily}).

\noindent\textit{Complete evaluation-block dates, endpoints, and sample counts are preserved in Supplementary Table S14.}

The ablation results confirm three fundamental architectural insights:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Architectural Necessity of EEG Graph Traversal:} Disabling multi-hop citation graph traversal (Ablation 1) drops evolutionary reasoning faithfulness from $\AblationFullCatBFaithfulness$ to $\AblationOneCatBFaithfulness$ (a relative reduction of $\AblationEEGFaithfulnessRelDrop$). Semantic vector retrieval over isolated reasoning traces fails to capture the chronological directionality of legal precedents; when retrieved text segments lack explicit bridge citations connecting non-contiguous decisions, generative synthesis degrades.
    \item \textbf{Critical Function of the NLI Smart Grader:} Disabling the semantic entailment gate (Ablation 2) removes the system's defensive verification firewall, dropping adversarial safety rejection on Category D from $\AblationFullCatDSafetyRate$ to $\AblationTwoCatDSafetyRate$. Without the autonomous entailment audit, the generator credulously complies with deceptive queries regarding overruled precedent.
    \item \textbf{Operational Efficiency of Traffic Cop Routing:} Bypassing the SLM complexity router (Ablation 3) forces straightforward single-point factual lookups through heavy multi-hop BFS graph expansion and NLI grading, increasing execution latency on Category A queries from $\AblationFullLatencyMedianSeconds\text{ s}$ to $\AblationThreeLatencyMedianSeconds\text{ s}$ ($\AblationRouterLatencySlowdownFactor\times$ slowdown) while introducing $\AblationThreeCatAFalseRefusalCount$ ($\AblationThreeCatAFalseRefusalRate$) false refusals due to context pollution from extraneous citing precedents. Notably, Ablation 3 reports a marginally higher Category B faithfulness ($\AblationThreeCatBFaithfulness$) than Full TERRA ($\AblationFullCatBFaithfulness$): because the forced hard path enforces BFS graph traversal for every query without exception, it eliminates any residual router misclassification of borderline evolutionary queries, yielding a minor uplift on multi-hop reasoning. However, the operational cost---a $\AblationRouterLatencySlowdownFactor\times$ latency increase and a $\AblationThreeCatAFalseRefusalRate$ false refusal rate on Category A factual queries---makes the unrouted configuration disadvantageous for latency-sensitive applications. The router provides critical operational triage, enabling fast sub-two-second execution on direct factual inquiries while reserving graph traversal for complex multi-decade doctrines.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Temporal Parametric Leakage Stress Test}
\label{subsec:temporal-leakage}

While retrospective legal retrieval requires evaluating questions strictly under historical jurisprudence as of temporal cutoff $T$, modern generative language models possess extensive pretraining corpora containing post-$T$ legal history. Consequently, models risk emitting \textit{temporal parametric leakage}---anachronistically importing future doctrinal shifts, statutory overhauls, or later overrulings into historical analyses. To rigorously quantify and stress-test this vulnerability, we preregistered and executed a dedicated 50-query temporal leakage probe across five historical Supreme Court horizons ($T \in \{1890, 1930, 1950, 1970, 1980\}$), spanning seven major doctrinal domains and balanced across case prominence and multi-hop reasoning depths. Six probes (12\%) targeting historical cases decided between 1857 and 1905 (evaluated at cutoffs $T=1890$ and $T=1930$) have target nodes with zero out-edges in the EEG due to the corpus citation-parsing boundary. Arc evolution cases for these entries are historically verified against primary legal sources but are not EEG graph edges. All leakage metrics are computed identically across all 50 probes regardless of arc status.


\noindent\textit{Extended tabulation is provided in Supplementary Material S3.}

\subsubsection{Experimental Design and Metric Decoupling}
The probe evaluates \TemporalTotalEvaluations\ responses generated across four comparative architectures: Direct LLM, Flat Vector RAG, $\text{TERRA}_{\text{ungated}}$ ($L_{\text{gen}}$), and $\text{TERRA}_{\text{gated}}$ ($L_{\text{final}}$). To separate baseline capabilities from prompt compliance, each architecture is tested under two distinct regimes: \textbf{Condition A} (standard task prompt without explicit temporal constraint, measuring default end-to-end containment) and \textbf{Condition B} (explicit instruction to strictly adhere to year $T$ and ignore future precedents, measuring prompt-assisted containment). All candidate generations were evaluated under a preregistered 3-tier severity rubric: Level~0 (clean adherence to pre-$T$ doctrine), Level~1 (non-prejudicial historical mention or temporal disclaimer), and Level~2 (fatal substantive reliance on post-$T$ law). The primary metric, \textbf{Substantive Post-$T$ Leakage Rate (SPTLR)}, captures the proportion of Level~2 fatal failures.

Critically, evaluating temporal containment solely via SPTLR creates a severe vulnerability to metric distortion: systems that simply refuse to answer generate zero post-$T$ text, appearing perfectly ``leak-free'' despite failing the underlying legal task. To eliminate this confound, we decouple containment from legal problem-solving by measuring: (1)~\textbf{Refusal Rate}, isolating epistemic or safety rejections; and (2)~\textbf{Historical Legal Correctness Rate (HLCR)}, assessing whether the system correctly articulates the operative contemporaneous rule as of year $T$. Finally, we measure the \textbf{NLI Gate Leakage Interception Rate (NGLIR)}, defined as the proportion of ungated candidate leaks intercepted by the entailment gate on the conditioning denominator of leaking candidates ($D$).

\subsubsection{Evaluator Reliability, Concordance, and Consensus Protocol}
Responses were independently evaluated by two distinct model-based judges: \texttt{qwen3-next-80b} and \texttt{gpt-oss-120b}. Across $N=\TemporalValidPairs$ complete pairs, the evaluators achieved $\TemporalRawConcordance$ raw concordance and $100\%$ monotonic inclusion (every leak identified by GPT-OSS was a strict subset of Qwen's flags). However, the quadratic weighted Cohen's $\kappa$ was $\TemporalQwk$, appearing considerably lower than the benchmark-wide agreement ($\kappa \approx 0.70$). Forensic diagnosis confirms that this divergence is an artifact of the \textit{Kappa Paradox}~\cite{feinstein1990high}: GPT-OSS exhibited a severe $91.7\%$ base-rate skew toward Level~0 ($289/315$ clean), which mechanically inflated expected chance agreement $P_e$ to $\approx 60\%$, deflating $\kappa$ despite high raw agreement. Furthermore, qualitative analysis revealed an evaluator threshold asymmetry: Qwen operated as a hyper-vigilant lexical scanner (flagging any passing historical reference as Level~1 or Level~2), whereas GPT-OSS evaluated holistic legal operability (classifying responses as clean if post-$T$ references did not alter the substantive legal outcome).

To guarantee objective reproducibility, all evaluations were reconciled via a pre-specified, deterministic 5-tier consensus protocol: (1)~safe refusals automatically resolve to Level~0; (2)~unanimous agreement is preserved; (3)~lexical breach of forbidden historical phrases assigns Level~2; (4)~substantive quotation of post-$T$ law assigns Level~2; and (5)~subtle mentions resolve conservatively to $\max(s_1, s_2) = \text{Level~1}$. Crucially, tiers 3--5 bias strictly \textit{toward} flagging leakage, ensuring that consensus adjudication is conservative against TERRA.

\subsubsection{Anti-Circularity and Graph Substrate Audit}
To formally falsify the hypothesis of circular alignment between graph construction and benchmark evaluation, we executed an exhaustive topological audit of the 20,000-opinion Event Evolution Graph:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Curated Backbone Seed Density vs. Canonical Benchmark Footprint:} We draw a fundamental scientific distinction between corpus-construction seed density and benchmark query footprint. During offline graph construction, exactly $34$ landmark decisions out of the $20,000$-node corpus ($\CaseFactsSeedDensity$, $\CaseFactsSeedDensityRaw$) received curated procedural reasoning traces. \subsection{Standardized External Benchmark Evaluation on CLERC (NAACL 2025)}
\label{subsec:clerc-generalization}

While the internal \numUniqueQueries-query benchmark rigorously isolates specific algorithmic mechanisms (Sections~\ref{subsec:safety-and-factual}--\ref{subsec:ablation-study}) and CaseFacts establishes external fact-checking and overruling recognition (Section~\ref{subsec:casefacts-generalization}), evaluating on researcher-crafted queries leaves open whether the architecture generalizes to standardized legal retrieval and generative drafting benchmarks designed by independent natural language processing teams. To address this question, we evaluate TERRA on a stratified sample of \textbf{CLERC} (\textit{Case Law Evaluation and Retrieval Corpus}; Findings of NAACL 2025) \cite{hou2024clerc}, a standardized legal benchmark constructed by Johns Hopkins University and collaborating institutions.

\subsubsection{Four-Layer Scientific Evaluation Architecture}
\label{subsubsec:four-layer-eval}
To establish methodological clarity and prevent confounding between internal mechanism isolation, standardized retrieval benchmarks, and external generalization scope, our empirical validation adheres to a structured four-layer evaluation architecture:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layer 1: Internal Controlled Benchmark ($N=\numUniqueQueries$):} Isolates fine-grained algorithmic mechanisms (temporal citation graphs, procedural reasoning traces, NLI entailment gate) across four controlled experimental categories (Sections~\ref{subsec:safety-and-factual}--\ref{subsec:ablation-study}).
    \item \textbf{Layer 2: Standardized Precedent Retrieval and Generative Legal Drafting (CLERC Sample, NAACL 2025):} Evaluates general-purpose precedent case retrieval (Task 1A) and unredacted judicial analysis generation (Task 1B) on external, uncurated judicial opinions from a stratified sample of CLERC ($N=120$), complemented by an engineered out-of-corpus refusal stress test ($N=50$).
    \item \textbf{Layer 3: External Temporal Fact-Checking and Overruling Recognition (CaseFacts, ACL 2026):} Evaluates temporal proposition classification, doctrinal overruling recognition, and anti-circularity under third-party adjudication ($N=120$ in-corpus, $N=50$ modern refusal controls; Section~\ref{subsec:casefacts-generalization}).
    \item \textbf{Layer 4: Licensed Legal Practitioner Evaluation:} Parked as essential future work (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}, Limitation~1) due to resource constraints; all evaluations at Layers 1--3 rely on reproducible algorithmic and non-LLaMA dual-judge protocols.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Benchmark Protocol, Provenance Hardening, and Anti-Leakage Audit}
\label{subsubsec:clerc-protocol}
The evaluation protocol is divided into two distinct tracks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Track 1: Standardized CLERC Precedent Retrieval and Generative Drafting Sample ($N=\ClercNQueries$):} To evaluate on external common-law contexts while maintaining reproducible execution, we extracted a stratified sample of 120 authentic pre-1980 legal drafting contexts from the 1,498 candidate instances in the CLERC test split. The sample was drawn pre-hoc (prior to observing any model outputs) with a fixed random seed (\texttt{SEED=42}), balanced equally between 60 Direct Precedent Citation queries and 60 Indirect Synthesis queries across five historical eras (1850--1880: $11.7\%$; 1881--1910: $20.0\%$; 1911--1940: $26.7\%$; 1941--1960: $21.7\%$; 1961--1980: $20.0\%$).
    \item \textbf{Track 2: TERRA-Specific Modern and Cross-Jurisdictional Refusal Stress Test ($N=\ClercNRefusal$):} Rather than a native CLERC benchmark task, Track 2 serves as a dedicated architectural safety stress test comprising 50 negative control queries (39 post-1980 modern Supreme Court decisions and 11 non-SCOTUS federal circuit/statutory queries) to rigorously evaluate epistemic boundary enforcement and anti-hallucination guardrails under out-of-corpus pressure.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Provenance Audit and Seed Purge}
To guard against latent circularity, we executed an automated cryptographic provenance audit. Out of 120 Track 1 target cases, exactly zero ($0.0\%$, 0/120) overlap with the 34 hand-curated landmark seed nodes that received bespoke reasoning traces during graph construction; all overlapping queries were purged during split generation. Track 1 evaluates on a certified $100.0\%$ uncurated graph substrate. Crucially, this exclusion specifically guarantees that evaluation is not artificially aided by the bespoke reasoning-trace seeds created during system development; it does not preclude legitimate retrieval over ordinary authentic Supreme Court opinions in the 20,000-opinion corpus, which serves as the uniform, shared knowledge substrate for all evaluated retrieval systems. As a documented limitation and scope distinction, the broader corpus-wide substantive holding extraction cascade (Tier~1 syllabi and Tier~2 headnotes covering $51.1\%$ of the 20,000 opinions) was not separately screened, as it constitutes the uniform, automated knowledge substrate available to all in-corpus GraphRAG retrievers.

Following the fatal-interception safeguards developed in prior evaluation phases, the batch harness enforces a mandatory pre-flight assertion over three known-refusal probes (Probe 1: Modern Post-1980 Authority; Probe 2: Fabricated Holding Trap; Probe 3: Non-SCOTUS Circuit Rule). The harness verifies that the NLI Smart Grader successfully short-circuits execution before launching the full evaluation batch.

All completions across Direct LLM, Flat Vector RAG, and Full TERRA were executed under strict payload parity ($T=0.0$, max tokens 4096, identical LLaMA 3.3 70B generator) and judged by independent, non-LLaMA dual evaluators (\texttt{qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct} and \texttt{gpt-oss-120b}). \subsection{Direct Evidence Retrieval Quality and Precedent-Chain Coverage}
\label{subsec:retrieval-quality}

A critical question in retrieval-augmented legal architectures is whether downstream generative advantages stem from superior evidence retrieval or merely reflect the generator's ability to synthesize plausible analyses from average context. Furthermore, Second Review Issue~\#11 noted that while TERRA's canonical graph expansion operates with breadth $k=2$ and depth $d=2$, Category~B queries comprise 3-to-5 node evolutionary arcs (spanning 2 to 4 citation hops), requiring explicit evaluation of retrieval success as a function of precedent chain length.

The core judicial benchmark encompasses $N=350$ queries across four operational strata: Category~A ($N=100$ single-point factual lookups), Category~B ($N=100$ multi-hop evolutionary chains), Category~C ($N=75$ out-of-context unanswerable refusal probes where target authorities are absent from the corpus, evaluated for gate interception in Section~\ref{subsec:safety-and-factual}), and Category~D ($N=75$ adversarial temporal overruling pairs). \subsubsection{Formal Definition and Seed Positioning of Multi-Seed Anchoring}
The ability of TERRA's nominal $d=2$ graph traversal to achieve complete path recovery on 3-hop and 4-hop lineages is governed by \textit{multi-seed anchoring}. For an evolutionary query $q$, entity and legal concept extraction identifies a set of seed nodes $S_q \subset V$ with $|S_q| \ge 2$ (empirical mean of $\CatBMeanSeedsPerQuery$ seeds per query across Category~B; $\CatBTwoSeedsPct$ with $|S_q|=2$, $\CatBThreeSeedsPct$ with $|S_q|=3$, and $\CatBFourPlusSeedsPct$ with $|S_q| \ge 4$). From each seed $s \in S_q$, directed EEG traversal expands a $d=2$ graph ball $B(s, 2)$ along forward and backward citation edges with breadth $k=2$.

Crucially, empirical auditing of seed placements confirms that multi-seed extraction anchors the extremities of the precedent trajectory: in $\CatBEndpointsAnchoredPct$ of 5-node chains (5/5) and $\CatBFourNodeEndpointsAnchoredPct$ of recovered 4-node chains (20/20), seeds anchor both the historical origin (index 0) and the culminating modern authority (index $m-1$). For an evolutionary chain $v_0 \to \dots \to v_{m-1}$, the forward 2-hop ball $B(v_0, 2)$ covers $\{v_0, v_1, v_2\}$ while the reverse 2-hop ball $B(v_{m-1}, 2)$ covers $\{v_{m-1}, v_{m-2}, v_{m-3}\}$. When intermediate branching along the precedent spine remains within the $k=2$ beam, the forward and backward neighborhoods intersect at intermediate stepping-stone authorities ($v_2$ in 5-node chains), seamlessly covering 3-hop and 4-hop doctrinal paths without requiring explosive unconstrained search depths ($d \ge 4$). Conversely, in the 12 non-recovered 4-node cases, high citation fan-out at intermediate nodes exceeded the $k=2$ beam width, causing the traversal to prune intermediate nodes despite correct endpoint seeding.

These empirical findings confirm that TERRA's high generative win rates (Table~\ref{tab:overall_summary}) and low fabrication rates (Supplementary Table S9) are fundamentally anchored in direct evidence retrieval quality rather than ungrounded parametric fluency.

Under TERRA's operational threshold $\tau=1.30$, the pre-filter functions with zero false refusal while intercepting $\TerraGateTPRTauOneThreeZero$ ($\TerraGateTPRCountTauOneThreeZero$) of out-of-domain probes before retrieval. The remaining $\TerraGateFNRTauOneThreeZero$ ($\TerraGateFNRCountTauOneThreeZero$) of out-of-domain queries slip past the pre-filter and enter retrieval; however, these deferred queries are subsequently intercepted by downstream NLI entailment grading, which halts generation in a safe refusal. We emphasize that geometric proximity serves strictly as an \textit{optional coarse pre-filter} to conserve retrieval and grading compute on obvious out-of-domain inputs; embedding distance metrics do not prove legal validity or domain membership, which remains the exclusive operational responsibility of the downstream NLI Smart Grader.

\subsection{Benchmark-Graph Traversal Stability Under Temporal Snapshot Expansion}
\label{subsec:temporal-update-stability}

To evaluate precedent traversal stability under corpus growth without generative confounding, we test standalone breadth-first graph traversal across four chronological snapshot expansions: $1890\to1930$ (Pair~I, Step $N=40$~yr), $1930\to1950$ (Pair~II, Step $N=20$~yr), $1950\to1970$ (Pair~III, Step $N=20$~yr), and $1970\to1980$ (Pair~IV, Step $N=10$~yr).



Across $N=\IssueNineteenTotalEvals$ qualifying Category~B observations ($\IssueNineteenUniqueQueries$ unique queries anchored at an origin precedent decided at or before Year~$T$, with at least one subsequent gold authority in $(T, T+N]$), BFS citation traversal ($k=2, d=2$, matching production parameters) was run on base subgraph $G_{\le T}$ and expanded subgraph $G_{\le T+N}$ seeded by benchmark origin precedent ($s = q[\texttt{target\_nodes}][0]$). As reported in Supplementary Table S13, expanding from $T$ to $T+N$ shifts grand pooled recall from $\IssueNineteenOverallRecallTMeanStd$ to $\IssueNineteenOverallRecallTNMeanStd$ ($\Delta\text{Recall} = \IssueNineteenOverallDeltaRecallMeanStd$), with $\IssueNineteenStabilityCoefficient$ of queries exhibiting non-negative recall shift ($\Delta\text{Recall} \ge 0$). On newly eligible authorities ($S_{\text{new}}$), traversal attains a grand mean Discovery Rate of $\IssueNineteenDiscoveryRateMean$, with median traversal latency under one millisecond (\IssueNineteenBFSLatencyMedianMs~ms; grand mean $0.15 \pm 0.18$~ms). Three methodological boundaries govern these findings: (1)~Because gold sequences include seed anchor $s$ (retrieved at depth 0), absolute recall reflects baseline seed inclusion; $\Delta\text{Recall}$ is invariant to this offset. (2)~Negative shifts on specific queries stem from degree caps when expanding nodes become hubs. (3)~This standalone experiment evaluates topological traversal over available directed edges (generic \texttt{CITES}) on NetworkX DiGraphs; it is decoupled from upstream vector retrieval (subject to the category filter limitation in Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}) and does not evaluate live vector index upsert, which remains future systems engineering.

\section{Discussion and High-Level Synthesis}
\label{sec:discussion}

The empirical evaluation validates that TERRA addresses fundamental failure modes of standard RAG architectures in knowledge-intensive, dynamic environments. This section contextualizes our primary architectural contributions, candidly examines current system limitations, and outlines future research directions.

\subsection{Architectural Differentiation and System Scope}
\label{subsec:contrib-discussion}
Compared to Microsoft Research's GraphRAG framework \cite{edge2024graphrag}---which constructs static hierarchical entity-relation communities for dataset summarization---our architecture directly addresses temporal precedent evolution and knowledge drift. While Microsoft GraphRAG requires substantial offline LLM summarization costs and remains temporally blind to overruled holdings, our dual-index Event Evolution Graph (EEG) cross-links chronological citation edges (\texttt{CITES}) with authoritative typed \texttt{OVERRULES} edges imported from CRS annotations (\IssueTwentyOneOverrulesEdges\ total, from which the 19 Category~D invalidation pairs are drawn; see Section~\ref{subsec:phase1-ingestion}) and node-level procedural reasoning traces. Furthermore, our NLI Smart Grader is designed to evaluate semantic entailment and reduce unsupported generations, although residual errors remain \cite{asai2024selfrag, zheng2023judging}. We formally delineate TERRA's architectural scope: the offline ingestion pipeline indexes full-docket Supreme Court jurisprudence (1850--1980) as a broad, high-distractor knowledge graph, while the online evaluated task focuses specifically on longitudinal precedent tracking, temporal drift mitigation, and safety firewalling over constitutional and federal public-law doctrine.

Three core design innovations drive TERRA's performance:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Tier Domain Boundary Safety:} Rather than relying on static allowlists, TERRA couples lexical anchoring with downstream NLI entailment gating and scale-dependent geometric filtering.
    \item \textbf{Entailment-Gated Generation and XAI Attribution:} By replacing keyword heuristics with an NLI Smart Grader, TERRA operationalizes a ``prefer silence over hallucination'' principle with transparent reasoning paths, qualified by our \IssueTwentyOneAuditTargetConfirmedPct\ citation extraction precision on the underlying precedent graph (Section~\ref{subsec:phase1-ingestion}).
    \item \textbf{Citation Graphs as Temporal Reasoning Structures:} By structuring opinions as chronological citation edges (\texttt{CITES}) alongside authoritative typed \texttt{OVERRULES} relations within an Event Evolution Graph (Section~\ref{subsec:phase1-ingestion}), TERRA explicitly models chronological precedent shifts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Empirical Analysis of Firewall Component Contributions and Scale Dynamics}
\label{subsec:firewall-analysis}

A critical finding from our systematic post-hoc auditing concerns the empirical division of labor within TERRA's safety architecture. The original design conceptualized the Domain Boundary Firewall as an active two-stage pre-retrieval filter where vector distance ($d_{L2} < 1.3$) flags out-of-domain (OOD) queries before graph traversal. However, systematic auditing across the preliminary 50-query Category C audit set on the initial 10,009-opinion index revealed that the vector proximity gate was non-functional at this scale ($0/50$ intercepted) because unconstrained OOD distances ($0.281 \le d_{L2} \le 0.859$) fell entirely beneath $1.3$.

Consequently, safety performance in Table~\ref{tab:overall_summary} (\TerraCatCRejectionRate\ refusal on Category C, \TerraCatDRejectionRate\ refusal and $\TerraCatDRefutationRate$\ verified refutation on Category D) was delivered almost entirely by the downstream NLI Smart Grader (Tier 3) via double-failure refusal. When OOD queries entered retrieval, the resulting lack of semantic entailment triggered a safe refusal. Removing NLI in ablation (the ablation results, Ablation 2) caused an immediate collapse of adversarial safety to 0.0\%, confirming NLI as the true empirical safeguard.

Crucially, empirical calibration on the expanded 20,000-opinion substantive corpus demonstrates that geometric pre-filtering exhibits scale- and density-dependent behavior. As reported in Supplementary Table S12 (Panel~B), evaluating the full $N=75$ Category~C benchmark suite against the 20k index yields an isolated AUROC of $\TerraGateIsolatedAUROC$. At the operational threshold $\tau = 1.30$, geometric pre-filtering achieves $\TerraGateFPRTauOneThreeZero$ in-domain false refusal ($\TerraGateFPRCountTauOneThreeZero$) while intercepting $\TerraGateTPRTauOneThreeZero$ ($\TerraGateTPRCountTauOneThreeZero$) of out-of-domain probes pre-retrieval. Comparing the 10k index (where 50/50 probes fell below $1.3$) to the 20k index (where 41/75 probes exceed $1.3$) reflects an empirical observation of corpus manifold density: expanding corpus volume across seven verified constitutional domains creates denser semantic clustering, pushing extraneous out-of-domain queries farther into the embedding margin. We do not assert any generalized theoretical scaling law from these two empirical corpus snapshots; rather, we treat geometric proximity strictly as an optional coarse pre-filter that saves retrieval and NLI compute on obvious outliers, while downstream NLI entailment verification serves as the indispensable primary safety firewall.


\subsection{Methodological Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

To ensure complete scientific transparency and maintain the highest standard of academic integrity, we identify eighteen methodological limitations of the current architecture, grouped into five cohesive thematic subsections (with unabridged registers provided in Supplementary Sections S2.1--S2.18):

\subsubsection{Section~VII-C.1: Evaluator Scope and Absence of Licensed Legal Practitioners}
\label{subsec:lim-evaluator-scope}
Due to geographical and institutional resource constraints, this study did not include evaluation by licensed legal practitioners (Limitation~1). All faithfulness and safety assessments rely on automated LLM-as-a-judge scoring (Section~\ref{subsec:eval-metrics}) and continuous lexical metrics (ROUGE-L) evaluated against researcher-constructed reference answers cross-referenced against public legal reference sources (Cornell LII, Oyez, CRS Constitution Annotated). While the adversarial critic persona exhibits a modest, statistically significant correlation with reference lexical grounding (Pearson $r = \JudgePearsonR, p = \JudgePearsonP$), automated scoring cannot replicate practitioner judgment on subtle constitutional doctrine. Specifically, practitioners would assess legal correctness, temporal consistency, citation validity, and hallucination behavior beyond continuous metrics. Furthermore, lacking an independent labeled validation set for the NLI Smart Grader (Section~\ref{subsubsec:terra-nli-grader}), the false-non-entailment rate remains empirically unquantified beyond ablation evidence. We consider human expert evaluation of both legal output quality and NLI verifier reliability essential prerequisites for production deployment, and release the complete 350-query benchmark suite and codebase to invite independent expert replication. The author spot-check described in Section~\ref{subsec:obscure-generalization} is a single non-expert review of a stratified sample and does not substitute for licensed practitioner review.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.1.}

\subsubsection{Section~VII-C.2: Evaluation Methodology, Rubric Resolution, and Metric Disentanglement}
\label{subsec:lim-eval-methodology}
\label{item:hosted-api-determinism}
\label{item:samefamily}
\noindent\textbf{Metric Disentanglement and Refusal-Volume Masking (Limitation~2):} Evaluating complex RAG pipelines solely via aggregate faithfulness induces severe metric distortion: systems with high epistemic refusal volume receive top marks for non-committal answers on unanswerable queries, masking generative weaknesses. Under our dual-judge protocol, TERRA has the highest overall ($\TerraOverallFaithfulnessMean$ vs.\ $\HippoRAGOverallFaithfulnessMean$; intervals overlap) and answer-only faithfulness ($\TerraAnswerOnlyFaithfulnessMean$ vs.\ $\HippoRAGAnswerOnlyFaithfulnessMean$) with fewer flagged refusals ($\TerraFlaggedRefusals$ vs.\ $\HippoFlaggedRefusals$), but answer-only scores are conditional on different answered subsets. On multi-hop reasoning TERRA leads HippoRAG by $\CatBTerraVsHippoRAGDelta$ ($q=\CatBTerraVsHippoRAGAdjQ$). Future benchmark protocols must mandate disentangled reporting of refusal rates alongside answer-only faithfulness.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.2.}

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation Rubric Resolution and Likert Normalization (Limitation~3):} Coarse evaluation scales (e.g., discrete binary pass/fail or $\{0.0, 0.8, 1.0\}$ heuristics) induce artificial ceiling compression, clustering over $75\%$ of responses at $1.0$. Implementing a 5-point Likert scale normalized to $[0, 1]$ effectively resolves this compression, exposing genuine variances in doctrinal nuance.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.3.}

\noindent\textbf{Hosted API Output Variability at $T=0.0$, Run-to-Run Identity, and Sensitivity Verification (Limitation~5):} Primary evaluations were conducted under greedy decoding ($T=0.0$) using \texttt{databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct} and \texttt{databricks-gemma-3-12b}. While setting $T=0.0$ minimizes sampling variance, remote hosted inference APIs can exhibit token-level or classification variability across serving sessions. Across benchmark evaluations, query \texttt{D066} exhibited an empirical classification shift (EASY $\to$ HARD), consistent with numerical or serving-level variability in hosted inference; the precise backend cause cannot be isolated from available logs. Comparing two 14-September TERRA executions across the 350-query benchmark suite, exact string-identical generated answers occurred on \TerraIdenticalAnswerFraction\ of queries overall (206/350), with pronounced category divergence: \TerraIdenticalAnswerCatA\ on Category~A (95/100) and \TerraIdenticalAnswerCatC\ on Category~C (62/75), versus \TerraIdenticalAnswerCatD\ on Category~D (39/75) and \TerraIdenticalAnswerCatB\ on Category~B (10/100). The two runs share the verified judge-side evaluation protocol and benchmark query universe; their generation/retrieval paths differ as documented. Both runs were evaluated under the standardized dual-judge protocol (\texttt{databricks-qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct} and \texttt{databricks-gpt-oss-120b}) using the frozen RAG prompt template (SHA256: \texttt{6d18fc14\dots}) and normalized Likert rubric. Sensitivity verification on an 80B model (\texttt{databricks-qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct}) across a separate 15-query subset (10 Cat~B, 5 Cat~D; distinct from the firewall stress test in Section~\ref{subsec:benchmark-design}) confirmed invariant relative rankings: TERRA achieved $1.000$ Category~B Faithfulness (vs. $0.900$ Flat RAG, $0.800$ Direct LLM) and $100\%$ Category~D safety rejection (vs. $40\%$ Flat RAG, $0\%$ Direct LLM). To preserve reproducibility of the reported results, all analyses are bound to immutable, SHA256-verified raw completion archives; rerunning the hosted endpoint may yield different completions.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.5.}

\noindent\textbf{Same-Family Generation, NLI Grading, and Category~D Verifier (Limitation~11):} Generation, NLI grading, and the Category~D verifier all deploy \texttt{databricks-meta-llama-3-3-70b-instruct} (same family and endpoint). Faithfulness judges are \texttt{databricks-qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct} and \texttt{databricks-gpt-oss-120b} (independent families). Category~D four-class labels (refusal, refutation, fatal error, hedge) may inherit shared Llama biases; cross-family verifier ablation has not been run.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.11.}

\noindent\textbf{Mixed-Date Evaluation Blocks (Limitation~13):} Not all blocks were re-evaluated under v2 dual-judge protocols; Supplementary Table S14 details dates and backends. Blocks lacking timestamps (pilot ablation, temporal leakage probe, CaseFacts, CLERC) carry ``date not recorded''; scores derive from earlier single-judge evaluation and are not numerically comparable to v2 evaluations.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.13.}

\subsubsection{Section~VII-C.3: Graph Traversal Horizons, Doctrinal Taxonomies, and Hyperparameters}
\label{subsec:lim-traversal-horizons}
\noindent\textbf{Fixed Graph Traversal Horizon and Multi-Hop Boundary Limits (Limitation~7):} TERRA's online retrieval operates with a calibrated 2-hop BFS graph expansion radius to bound latency and token overhead. While 2-hop traversal captures direct citing and invalidating authorities across most evolutionary arcs, it can miss subsequent modifying precedents when citation chains exceed two steps. An empirical boundary emerged during our CaseFacts audit on \textit{Chimel v. California} (query \texttt{CF\_INC\_058}; Section~\ref{subsec:casefacts-generalization}), where the modifying precedent (\textit{United States v. Robinson} [1973]) sat beyond the 2-hop boundary; the generator cited \textit{Chimel}'s 1969 wingspan doctrine but found no modifying precedent in context. This multi-hop horizon likewise accounts for the gold-precedent omission rate during drafting on CLERC ($\ClercTerraMisapplicationTrackOne$; Section~\ref{subsec:clerc-generalization}), where distant authorities prompted analogical reasoning from historical cases rather than fabrication. Developing adaptive traversal horizons along invalidation paths represents a high-priority architectural extension.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.7.}

\noindent\textbf{Oracle Relation Labels, Exact-Title Seeding, and a Bundled Ablation (Limitation~8):} In Full TERRA, curated \texttt{OVERRULES} edges are serialized with an explicit relation tag, dedicated heading, expanded excerpt budget, and prompt instruction; obscure benchmark pairs were drawn from these edges. The Full-TERRA advantage on that suite is therefore contingent on curated annotations, and the bundled ablation cannot isolate the tag alone. For named-case queries, seeding relied on exact caption matches (category-filtered vector retrieval returned no candidates), causing two queries to produce empty context and safe-rated refusals, one to omit the overruling case, and two to take the fast path; we evaluated TERRA as deployed and report sensitivity subsets excluding these items. The primary comparison contrasts complete systems, and Direct LLM receives no instruction block. Outcomes are judge-dependent in magnitude, the suite has thirty items, and judges were not fully blind to arm identity.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.8.}

\noindent\textbf{Citation Extraction Precision, Same-Year Chronology, and Provenance Uncertainty (Limitation~15):} Automated citation extraction via \texttt{eyecite} establishes citation existence rather than doctrinal endorsement. Our stratified audit ($N=\IssueTwentyOneAuditSampleN$, seed \IssueTwentyOneAuditSeed) confirms \IssueTwentyOneAuditCitationPresentPct\ of sampled edges contain reporter strings; direct precedential reliance is verified in \IssueTwentyOneAuditTargetConfirmedPct\ (95\% CI: \IssueTwentyOneAuditTargetConfirmedCI), with \IssueTwentyOneAuditQuotationPct\ in quotations. Furthermore, \IssueTwentyOneSameYearEdges\ edges (\IssueTwentyOneSameYearEdgesPct) connect same-year opinions, with \IssueTwentyOneIntraYearReversals\ intra-year date reversals from publication artifacts and zero cross-year forward violations. The legacy claim of ``$99.91\%$ chronological validity / $0.09\%$ noise'' in earlier working drafts was untraceable to repository artifacts, mandating this audit. Citation trails provide candidate authorities with \IssueTwentyOneAuditTargetConfirmedPct\ precision rather than certified doctrinal paths.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.15.}

\noindent\textbf{Rule-Based Doctrinal Taxonomy, Lexical Polysemy, Truncation, and Residual Fallback Bias (Limitation~16):} The seven-domain classification is generated by a deterministic \IssueTwentyTwoTotalLexiconPhrases-phrase rule-based lexicon over truncated texts ($\le \IssueTwentyTwoTextTruncationChars$ characters), inducing four limitations: (1)~\textit{Lexical polysemy}: Keyword matching without contextual disambiguation risks misclassification when terms appear in procedural dicta or dissenting quotes. (2)~\textit{Truncation artifacts}: Truncating texts to \IssueTwentyTwoTextTruncationChars\ characters restricts matching to introductory syllabi, occasionally missing dispositive holdings. (3)~\textit{Residual fallback bias}: The \texttt{general\_constitutional\_statutory} category acts as an unkeyed fallback for \IssueTwentyTwoGeneralConstitutionalCorpus\ decisions (\IssueTwentyTwoGeneralConstitutionalCorpusPct) lacking substantive keywords, functioning as a catch-all with \IssueTwentyTwoGeneralConstitutionalBaselineAcc\ baseline precision despite \IssueTwentyTwoFallbackValidityPct\ validity as a residual class. (4)~\textit{Absence of human expert annotation}: Taxonomy validation relies on dual automated evaluators (IEA \IssueTwentyTwoIEARawAgreement, Cohen's $\kappa=\IssueTwentyTwoIEACohensKappa$) and a blinded automated adjudicator (\IssueTwentyTwoHorvitzThompsonAccuracy\ weighted accuracy, 95\% CI: \IssueTwentyTwoHorvitzThompsonCI), not licensed practitioner annotation; classifications are not certified human ground truth.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.16.}

\noindent\textbf{A Priori Hyperparameter Selection and Sensitivity Analysis Scope (Limitation~17):} Core retrieval and graph expansion parameters ($k=\IssueTwentyFourSeedK$, depth $d=\IssueTwentyFourBfsDepth$, hub cap $\IssueTwentyFourHubCap$, geometric threshold $\tau=\IssueTwentyFourDistThreshold$) were fixed prior to benchmark construction and evaluation based on topological network constraints and preliminary feasibility testing on an independent \IssueTwentyFourDevQueryCount-query development suite (\IssueTwentyFourDevDate). While this frozen protocol prevents the audited forms of test-set leakage and hyperparameter overfitting, it precludes claiming global parameter optimality. Empirical sensitivity sweeps across \IssueTwentySixConfigCount\ configurations (Section~\ref{subsec:retrieval-quality}, Supplementary Table S10 Panel~C) profile performance across horizons ($k \in \{1,2,4\}$, $d \in \{1,2,3\}$, $C \in \{25,50,100\}$), confirming empirical stability around the historical configuration ($k=2, d=2, C=50$, \IssueTwentySixHistMacro\ macro recall, \IssueTwentySixHistFullPath\ full path recall) while quantifying the coverage-efficiency trade-off under bounded operational caps (\IssueTwentySixGoldRetentionPct\ landmark retention, \IssueTwentySixContextReductionPct\ context reduction).

\subsubsection{Section~VII-C.4: Failure Modes, Fatal Errors, and Refusal Asymmetry}
\label{subsec:lim-failure-modes}
\label{item:falserefusal}
\noindent\textbf{Parametric Pretraining Confounders, Latent Weights, and Temporal Containment Bounds (Limitation~6):} On continuous evaluation of Category D (Adversarial Deception), Direct LLM recorded a higher continuous Faithfulness score ($\DirectLLMCatDFaithfulness$ vs. $\TerraCatDFaithfulness$), yielding a non-significant Wilcoxon signed-rank comparison in Direct LLM's favor ($\Delta = -0.083, p = 0.0629$, Supplementary Table~S1, Rank 11). Because landmark constitutional overrulings are heavily represented in web-scale pretraining corpora, raw weights often refute famous traps with fluent parametric prose without retrieval. However, evaluating discrete outcome safety via the formal rubric (Section~\ref{subsec:benchmark-design}, Table~\ref{tab:refusal_breakdown}) reveals that Direct LLM achieves its $94.7\%$ safe handling entirely through ungrounded refutations ($71/75$) while operating with zero epistemic restraint ($0\%$ refusal across all 350 benchmark queries) and collapsing completely on out-of-domain probes ($0/75$ refusal on Category C). In contrast, TERRA achieves $\mathbf{\TerraCatDSafeHandlingRate}$ total safe handling ($73/75$: $\TerraCatDRejection$ explicit refusals and $\TerraCatDRefutations$ verified refutations), delivering a $+\TerraCatDRejectionRate$ explicit refusal advantage ($p = \TerraVsDirectLLMCatDRefusalMcNemarP$), perfect monotonic containment ($c=0$), and an $80.0\%$ reduction in fatal constraint violations (from $10/75$ to $\TerraCatDFatalErrors$) with zero uncommitted hedges ($0/75$). While the pre-specified hypothesis test in Supplementary Table~S1 (Rank 11) evaluates continuous faithfulness, TERRA's true structural advantage lies in verifiable citation provenance, epistemic restraint, and temporal boundary integrity. Forensic auditing reveals that the two remaining fatal errors (queries D033 and D034) are reciprocal misattributions between coupled apportionment landmarks (\textit{Baker v. Carr} [1962] and \textit{Reynolds v. Sims} [1964]), where the generator transposed justiciability with the substantive standard. Adaptive edge-weighting to prioritize invalidation paths constitutes essential future work. Similarly, on Category~B (Evolutionary Precedents), Direct LLM achieves higher judge-scored faithfulness ($\DirectLLMCatBFaithfulness$ vs.\ $\TerraCatBFaithfulness$, $\Delta=\CatBTerraVsDirectLLMDelta$, $q=\CatBTerraVsDirectLLMAdjQ$), consistent with base model fluency on generic historical narratives, whereas TERRA's retrieval evaluation shows stronger Category~B citation coverage and full-precedent-path recovery ($\CatBTerraMacroCitationRecall$ macro citation recall vs.\ $\CatBDirectMacroCitationRecall$ for Direct LLM; Section~\ref{subsec:citation-correctness}).

More fundamentally, we distinguish \textit{latent parametric knowledge} from \textit{operative doctrinal leakage}. Because modern foundation models are pretrained on web-scale text, weights encode post-$T$ legal history (e.g., \textit{Plessy} overruled by \textit{Brown} in 1954). TERRA does not purge latent weights; its provenance-bounded retrieval and temporal gate prevent latent memory from intruding into operative pre-$T$ reasoning. While forensic auditing (Section~\ref{subsec:temporal-leakage}) confirms suppression of Level-2 leakage in $\text{TERRA}_{\text{gated}}$, future work should explore circuit editing and machine unlearning for weight-level guarantees. Provider-side safety filtering on historical legal topics (terminating 85 evaluations in Section~\ref{subsec:temporal-leakage}) highlights the operational dependency of LLM-as-a-judge pipelines on third-party API policies, motivating our sensitivity-bounded framework. Section~\ref{subsec:obscure-generalization} tests this confounder directly: on obscure overruled precedents Direct LLM safe handling was \ObscDirectSafePct\ (\ObscCrossDirectFamousSafe\ on the famous subset), consistent with famous-landmark performance partly reflecting parametric memory.
\textit{For complete unabridged discussion, see Supplementary Section~S2.6.}

\noindent\textbf{False Refusals on Answerable Queries (Categories A and B) (Limitation~9):} The safety gate produces false refusals on non-adversarial inquiries: \TerraCatAFalseRefusals/100 Category~A (Factual) and \TerraCatBFalseRefusals/100 Category~B (Evolutionary) queries receive system-level safety rejections (\texttt{safety\_rejected = True}). (2)~\textit{HTTP~429 throttling}: \TerraThrottledRows/350 TERRA queries incurred rate-limiting back-off accumulating \TerraThrottleSleepSeconds\,s of sleep; reported medians are gross with net values alongside. (3)~\textit{Bimodal routing}: The fast route contains \RouterFastPathCount\ queries (median \TerraFastRouteMedianS); the hard route contains \RouterHardPathCount\ queries (median \TerraHardRouteMedianS). Aggregate medians obscure this bimodal structure; baseline and TERRA comparisons are indicative only.

Adding textual refusals (asserting contextual absence without gate triggering) raises counts to \TerraEpistemicRefusals/350 for TERRA, \FlatEpistemicRefusals/350 for Flat Vector RAG, \GraphEpistemicRefusals/350 for GraphRAG-Adapted, and \HippoEpistemicRefusals/350 for HippoRAG-Adapted. \subsubsection{Section~VII-C.5: Corpus Boundaries, Filtering Biases, and Domain Generalizability}
\label{subsec:lim-corpus-boundaries}
\noindent\textbf{Jurisdictional and Temporal Corpus Boundaries (Limitation~4):} The pre-built Event Evolution Graph is anchored in \CorpusTotalOpinions\ authentic substantive U.S. Supreme Court opinions spanning \CorpusTemporalSpan, encompassing seven doctrinal domains of federal jurisprudence (Table~\ref{tab:corpus_stats}). Deterministic audit of 63 landmarks reveals \IssueTwentyLandmarksExcludedByLength\ (\IssueTwentyLandmarksExcludedByLengthPct) were purged, including procedural standards (\textit{Haines}, \IssueTwentyHainesCharCount\ chars, \IssueTwentyHainesCAPPRPercentile th percentile; \textit{Diebold}, \IssueTwentyDieboldCharCount\ chars, \IssueTwentyDieboldCAPPRPercentile th percentile), competency (\textit{Dusky}, \IssueTwentyDuskyCharCount\ chars, \IssueTwentyDuskyCAPPRPercentile th percentile), tax milestones (\textit{Lucas v. \noindent\textbf{Task-Corpus Domain Asymmetry and Private-Law Generalizability Scope (Limitation~18):} A core methodological boundary is the intentional asymmetry between corpus composition and benchmark task scope. While the indexed repository reflects the complete 20,000-case docket spanning seven domains (\IssueTwentyFiveCorpusCombinedPrivateStatutoryPct\ private and general statutory law), the evaluated multi-hop reasoning benchmark (Category~B) evaluates constitutional and public-law precedent exclusively (\IssueTwentyFiveCatBConstitutionalPublicPct\ across five public-law domains; zero private-law chains). While the evaluation demonstrates TERRA's measured performance on the evaluated constitutional citation chains across a dense background network containing \CorpusPrivateLawOpinions\ private-law distractors, the system's performance on complex multi-hop statutory or patent reasoning remains unvalidated. Multi-hop evolutionary retrieval over complex commercial codes and specialized regulatory dockets constitutes an important open frontier for future domain-specific evaluation.

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{subsec:future-work}
Future research will: (1)~conduct licensed practitioner evaluation across the 350-query benchmark; (2)~scale corpus ingestion to the full federal repository; and (3)~codify \texttt{OVERRULES} annotations as explicit weighted graph edges with adaptive invalidation-path traversal.

\section{Conclusion and Broader Impacts}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduced TERRA, an experimental dual-phase GraphRAG architecture addressing knowledge drift and multi-step legal reasoning on historical SCOTUS jurisprudence (\CorpusTemporalSpan). Structuring \CorpusTotalOpinions\ authentic decisions (\CorpusPrecedentEdges\ citation edges) with procedural reasoning traces, TERRA enables inference via Traffic Cop routing, BFS traversal, and an NLI grader evaluating retrieved evidence. Benchmarking across \numUniqueQueries\ queries (\numTotalEvals\ evaluations) shows higher Category~B multi-hop faithfulness than Flat Vector RAG, HippoRAG, and GraphRAG ($\Delta$ from $\CatBTerraVsHippoRAGDelta$ to $\CatBTerraVsGraphRAGDelta$; $q<0.05$), though not over Direct LLM ($\CatBTerraVsDirectLLMDelta$); higher overall faithfulness ($\TerraOverallFaithfulnessMean$ vs.\ $\HippoRAGOverallFaithfulnessMean$, overlapping CIs; $\TerraAnswerOnlyFaithfulnessMean$); and $\TerraCatDSafeHandlingRate$ adversarial safe handling ($\TerraCatDRejection$ refusals, \TerraCatDRefutations\ refutations). Constraints include \TerraCatDFatalErrors\ fatal errors, false refusals on \TerraCatAFalseRefusals\ Category~A and \TerraCatBFalseRefusals\ Category~B queries, and reliance on curated overruling annotations (Section~\ref{subsec:obscure-generalization}). Critically, because evaluations rely on automated LLM judges without licensed attorneys (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}), human expert review remains an essential prerequisite for deployment. Citation graphs grounded with procedural traces preserve longitudinal stability (Section~\ref{subsec:temporal-update-stability}) \cite{edge2024graphrag, sun2024thinkongraph, zheng2023judging}.

\subsection{Broader Impacts and Ethical Governance}
\label{subsec:broader-impacts}
The deployment of agentic retrieval-augmented architectures in judicial and legal domains carries critical societal responsibilities. While TERRA demonstrates that temporal citation graphs and semantic entailment gating can significantly reduce temporal hallucination and enforce epistemic restraint on overruled precedents, autonomous legal advice remains strictly outside the intended scope of this research. AI architectures operating in judicial domains must function exclusively as decision-support or research tools under direct human-in-the-loop oversight by licensed legal practitioners. Moreover, because our automated evaluations demonstrate non-zero error rates---including \TerraCatDFatalErrors\ fatal misattributions and false refusals on valid inquiries---unsupervised deployment in real-world legal adjudication would introduce severe risks of misapplied precedent. By publishing our complete benchmark suite, evaluation methodology, and unabridged limitations register (Supplement~S2), we aim to promote transparency, rigorous safety auditing, and responsible governance in AI-assisted legal research.
