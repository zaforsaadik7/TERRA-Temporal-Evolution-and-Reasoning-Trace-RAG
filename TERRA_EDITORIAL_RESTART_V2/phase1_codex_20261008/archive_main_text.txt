Abstract—Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in law
must trace multi-hop precedent chains and must not present
overruled doctrine as current law, yet common evaluators
reward entity overlap rather than grounded reasoning. We
introduce TERRA (Temporal-Evolution and Reasoning-Trace
RAG), an experimental dual-phase GraphRAG architecture.
Offline, it builds an Event Evolution Graph over 20,000
Supreme Court opinions connected by 145,366 directed citation
edges, plus a procedural reasoning-trace index. Online, a small-
model Traﬀic Cop router sends single-point lookups down a
fast path and multi-hop questions through citation traversal,
and an autonomous semantic entailment grader evaluates re-
trieved evidence before generation. We evaluate on a 350-query
benchmark against four baselines (1,750 evaluations) using
two non-Llama judges, paired Wilcoxon tests and Benjamini–
Hochberg correction. TERRA scores higher overall faithfulness
(0.695 vs. 0.670 for the next best system, overlapping 95%
CIs) and answer-only faithfulness ( 0.586, computed on differing
answered subsets), and significantly outperforms all three
retrieval baselines on Category B multi-hop reasoning ( ∆ from
+0.066 [HippoRAG] and +0.070 [Flat] to +0.119 [GraphRAG];
FDR q < 0.05), although a no-retrieval Direct LLM scores
significantly higher on Category B ( ∆ = −0.081, q = 0 .0023).
On adversarial Category D queries TERRA safely handles
97.3% of traps (64.0% explicit refusals, 33.3% verified refuta-
tions) with 2/75 (2.7%) fatal errors, but refuses 2 Category A
and 16 Category B queries, at 9.19 s median latency. On 30
obscure overruled precedents, Direct LLM safe handling falls
to 0.0% versus 63.3% for full TERRA, but TERRA without
curated overruling labels drops to 26.7%, not significantly
different from flat retrieval ( p = 0 .726562); safety depends
on curated annotations. Absent licensed attorney evaluation,
human review remains an essential deployment prerequisite.

I. Introduction
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has rapidly
evolved into a foundational architecture for knowledge-
intensive artificial intelligence. By explicitly decoupling
the parametric memory of Large Language Models (LLMs)
from dynamic external knowledge bases, RAG enables real-
time factual grounding and transparent source attribution
without the prohibitive computational costs of continu-
ous model retraining. In high-stakes, knowledge-intensive
environments—where factual errors and hallucinations
carry severe real-world consequences—the fidelity of the
retrieved context is not merely an optimization prefer-
ence, but a strict operational requirement. As enterprise
applications scale, the early paradigm of naive “retrieve-
then-generate” pipelines characterized by static vector
databases and fixed-length document chunking has proven
structurally inadequate.
The fundamental bottleneck in modern RAG systems
arises when navigating complex data landscapes char-
acterized by continuous knowledge drift and multi-step
reasoning demands. Real-world data evolves dynamically;
consequently, static vector indices inevitably accumulate
conflicting, time-stamped versions of the same fact. When
queried, standard retrievers surface all of these conflict-
ing versions simultaneously based on semantic similarity,
leaving the frozen generative model unable to resolve the
chronological authority, which induces severe temporal
hallucinations. Furthermore, when processing complex
logical derivations, standard pipelines retrieve fragmented
textual snippets that sever precedential and doctrinal
dependencies, proving entirely insuﬀicient for tasks that
require deep, multi-hop reasoning.
Recent advancements have attempted to address these
bottlenecks in isolated silos, exposing critical failure
modes. While structural graph-based retrievers organize
facts chronologically, they act merely as static data foun-
dations lacking the procedural logic to guide multi-hop
generative reasoning. Conversely, prior paradigms retriev-
ing intermediate structured reasoning traces significantly
enhance logical deduction but remain temporally blind
and have been primarily restricted to quantitative domains
like mathematics or coding. More critically, current Agen-
tic RAG frameworks rely heavily on corrective evaluators
that fail fundamentally on conceptual tasks. Mathematical
explainability analyses, such as SHAP, have proven that
standard evaluators act merely as superficial named entity
alignment detectors, failing to comprehend genuine seman-
tic relevance. Compounding this, existing query routers
are largely restricted to rigid, binary-channel systems
(e.g., pure dense versus pure sparse search), resulting
in severe evidence-intent mismatches when unrestricted
search floods the context window with noisy, irrelevant
data.
To bridge these isolated gaps, this paper introduces an

integrated dual-phase GraphRAG architecture purpose-
built for dynamically evolving, knowledge-intensive envi-
ronments (with empirical graph traversal stability across
temporal snapshot expansions evaluated in Section VI-N).
The proposed system confines all computationally heavy
operations to an offline preprocessing phase, constructing
a dual-index corpus that cross-links an Event Evolu-
tion Graph (EEG)—which encodes chronological prece-
dent relationships across 20,000 authentic judicial deci-
sions and 145,366 directed citation edges—with a node-
level procedural reasoning trace database that captures
structured legal analysis trajectories (constitutional issue,
procedural history, legal test, holding). During online
inference, a small language model acts as a Traﬀic Cop
router, dynamically allocating retrieval priority across
text chunks, graph subgraphs, and procedural reasoning
traces, enabling a bimodal execution profile (median 5.83 s
on single-point factual lookups vs. 17.94 s on multi-hop
precedent chains). Before the final response generation, an
autonomous semantic entailment grader replaces flawed
keyword-matching heuristics, verifying the true conceptual
and structural relevance of the retrieved evidence.
The proposed framework was rigorously evaluated
against comparative baselines, including Direct LLM gen-
eration and Flat Vector RAG, alongside component-level
ablation studies utilizing comprehensive temporal and
multi-perspective reasoning benchmarks. Experimental re-
sults demonstrate that the model significantly mitigates
the attention dilution and “context cliff” limitations in-
herent in processing overly broad retrieved contexts. By
seamlessly aligning chronological facts with procedural
reasoning, the model substantially reduces temporal hallu-
cinations in grounded retrieval, providing an experimental
research framework for context fidelity in temporal legal
AI on historical SCOTUS jurisprudence.
A. Summary of Contributions
The primary contributions of this work are as follows:
• An Authentic Dual-Index Architecture: We design
and implement an authentic dual-index judicial
GraphRAG corpus that cross-links a structural Event
Evolution Graph (EEG) of 20,000 authentic SCOTUS
decisions (145,366 citation edges) with a node-level
procedural reasoning trace database, supporting joint
chronological and logical evidence alignment with
zero synthetic cases.
• Traﬀic Cop Routing with Coarse Domain Pre-
filtering: We introduce an SLM-driven complexity
router paired with a two-stage coarse proximity pre-
filter ( dL2 < 1.3 + Title Match) that provides scale-
dependent domain boundary filtering without main-
taining static allowlists (empirical coverage and cali-
bration detailed in Section VI-M and Section VII-B).
• NLI-Based Entailment Evaluation and XAI Attribu-
tion: We replace superficial keyword-matching heuris-
tics with a specialized Natural Language Inference
(NLI) grader that evaluates retrieved evidence to in-
tercept ungrounded generations, providing qualitative
reasoning traces and citation provenance.
• Data Leakage Mitigation Framework: We formalize
explicit safeguards against temporal precedent inver-
sion, benchmark retrieval contamination, and cross-
domain feature bleeding.

refutations) with 2/75 (2.7%) fatal errors vs. 4–8

gory A (16 on Category B). Evaluations rely on
automated LLM judges without licensed attorneys,
establishing human review as an essential prerequisite
(Section VII-C).
II. Related Work
The foundational paradigm of Retrieval-Augmented
Generation (RAG) [ 1], [ 2] decoupled parametric LLM
memory from external knowledge bases. However, stan-
dard RAG suffers from severe vulnerabilities when man-
aging continuous knowledge drift and multi-step reasoning.
To structure retrieved evidence, recent work has pivoted
toward graph-based architectures. Microsoft Research in-
troduced GraphRAG [ 3], which constructs hierarchical
entity-relation communities for global query summariza-
tion. Similarly, Sun et al. [ 4] developed Think-on-Graph
(ToG) to execute iterative LLM-guided reasoning over
knowledge graphs. In temporal domains, Liu and Cao [ 5]
introduced Chronos to track dynamic events in an Event
Evolution Graph (EEG), Zhang et al. [ 6] proposed E2RAG
to preserve temporal-causal consistency across entity-
event subgraphs, and Zhu et al. [ 7] developed STAR-RAG
for time-sensitive rule graph summarization. Within the le-
gal domain, recent architectures have explored structured
representations: Chen et al. [ 8] proposed LegalGraphRAG,
deploying multi-agent reasoning over a tripartite legal
knowledge graph (ontology, fact, and rule layers) for
statutory civil and criminal law, while de Martim [ 9]
introduced SAT-Graph RAG to trace diachronic statutory
norm versions across formal constitutional amendments.
However, these systems model codified statutory rules or
formal legislative amendment events; in contrast, common-
law judicial systems require tracking the longitudinal evo-
lution of precedent across citation networks, where earlier
decisions remain presumptive authority unless explicitly
distinguished, limited, or overruled. Furthermore, static

PAGE 3
graph models retrieve structural summaries but lack the
procedural logic required to execute multi-hop reasoning
over evolving precedents.
To bridge this gap in procedural logic, recent research
has explored augmenting retrieval with intermediate rea-
soning trajectories rather than static factual snippets
alone. Li et al. [ 10] demonstrated this through the CoT-
RAG framework, establishing that retrieving structured
reasoning sub-cases and decision trees significantly mit-
igates logical biases compared to unstructured text re-
trieval. The broader transition toward System 2 agen-
tic reasoning has been mapped by Lin et al. [ 11] and
Liang et al. [ 12], who detail multi-stage pipelines where
autonomous agents dynamically identify knowledge gaps
and generate intermediate reasoning traces to inform
subsequent search actions. In parallel, the legal AI com-
munity has established rigorous evaluation benchmarks
to measure procedural reasoning capabilities: CaseHOLD
[13] evaluates judicial holding identification, while the
Common Law Case Law Retrieval Benchmark (CLERC)
[14] measures multi-step precedent retrieval. These bench-
marks confirm that standard lexical or dense retrievers
frequently retrieve irrelevant or superseded authority un-
less procedural reasoning context is explicitly preserved.
However, existing trace-retrieval frameworks have been
predominantly evaluated on quantitative domains or static
statutory collections, leaving them largely untested in
longitudinal common-law environments where precedent
evolution alters substantive legal authority over time.
Beyond the challenge of retrieving complex reason-
ing traces and temporal data, the successful integration
of this multi-faceted information relies entirely on the
“connective tissue” of the RAG pipeline: query routers
and autonomous evaluators. Hashmi [ 15] highlighted the
rigidity of standard single-channel retrieval, introducing
an Adaptive Hybrid Retrieval framework to route queries
dynamically, noting that pure vector similarity frequently
fails to capture structural navigation. More critically,
the evaluation mechanisms governing these routers are
fundamentally flawed. Yalavarthi [ 16] conducted a SHAP
explainability analysis on the widely adopted Correc-
tive RAG (CRAG) architecture, mathematically proving
that its standard T5-based evaluator functions purely as
a named entity alignment detector. Consequently, the
CRAG evaluator misclassified 88.3% of conceptual science
tasks as ambiguous and achieved only 5.0% accuracy
on non-entity queries. The real-world consequences of
these shallow heuristics were exposed by Putta et al. [ 17]
via the CaseFacts benchmark, which demonstrated that
augmenting models with unrestricted web search actively
degrades performance in legal fact-checking compared to
closed-book baselines. Because standard retrievers rely on
lexical matching, they flood the context window with
noisy, overruled precedents, failing to recognize when a
fact has been logically overturned. These findings highlight
a critical architectural gap: current systems lack the
genuine Natural Language Inference (NLI) capabilities
required for true semantic entailment.
Ultimately, a comprehensive review of this literature
reveals that addressing these vulnerabilities in isolation
is insuﬀicient; no prior architecture unifies temporal
precedent management, node-level procedural reasoning
trace retrieval, and semantic-entailment evaluation into a
single, cohesive pipeline. Graph models track chronological
topology but lack procedural reasoning logic; trace models
enhance reasoning but lack temporal awareness; and
current routers rely on superficial keyword-matching that
triggers catastrophic failure in high-stakes domains. The
proposed architecture directly addresses this convergence
gap, as summarized in the comprehensive architectural
and doctrinal feature comparison of Table I. By cross-
linking a temporal Event Evolution Graph with a node-
level procedural reasoning trace database, it ensures that
structural precedent topology (what happened and when)
and operative judicial analysis (issue, test, and holding)
are retrieved as a semantically aligned pair. Furthermore,
it replaces flawed entity-alignment evaluators with an
autonomous NLI entailment grader and utilizes a contin-
uous three-way Traﬀic Cop router to dynamically balance
textual, graphical, and procedural data. This design es-
tablishes a dual-phase framework capable of addressing
continuous knowledge drift (empirically examined under
incremental corpus snapshot expansions in Section VI-N)
and multi-hop reasoning without requiring continuous
foundational model retraining.
III. System Architecture and Methodology
TERRA (Temporal-Evolution and Reasoning-Trace
RAG) is designed as a two-phase, closed-loop GraphRAG
architecture for temporal legal reasoning on historical
common-law jurisprudence. The core engineering philos-
ophy strictly separates offline corpus ingestion and knowl-
edge structure construction from online query routing and
inference. This separation ensures that all computationally
intensive graph traversals, citation link extractions, and
reasoning trace generations are performed offline, main-
taining responsive online inference with a bimodal latency
profile (median 5.83 s on factual lookups vs. 17.94 s on
multi-hop citation chains).

3) Tier 3: Semantic Entailment Verification (Primary
Safeguard): Because geometric proximity alone can-
not verify semantic authority or prevent doctrinal
misapplication, the primary empirical safeguard is
enforced downstream by the autonomous NLI Smart
Grader. If retrieved legal subgraphs fail logical
entailment across two expansion attempts, genera-
tion terminates deterministically in a safety refusal,
designed to systematically intercept out-of-domain
queries passing Tier 2 before answer synthesis.
Within this multi-tier verification framework, a fatal
constraint violation is formally defined as generating an
aﬀirmative, factual-sounding response that validates an
overruled legal doctrine, misattributes controlling constitu-
tional authority, or credulously adopts a legally impossible
premise embedded within an adversarial prompt (Cate-
gory D). Conversely, a false refusal is formally defined as
issuing an unwarranted epistemic refusal on an in-domain
factual lookup query (Category A) where controlling
historical precedent is fully present and unambiguous in
the corpus.
2) Multi-Hop Citation Graph Traversal: For HARD-

1) Seed Retrieval and Error Propagation Insulation:

synthesis from vector seeding inaccuracies: while

precedent node recall and 1.0% path recovery, two-

• Entailment Verified (entails == True): The verified

• Entailment Failed (entails == False): The engine trig-

Retrieval breadth expands ( k ← k + 2), ChromaDB

• Double Failure Termination: If entailment fails after
two expansion attempts, the Safety Refusal Firewall
halts generation, outputting a deterministic refusal:
“I apologize, but I do not have suﬀicient validated
legal context in my databases to answer this query
accurately without risking hallucination. ”
4) Data Leakage Mitigation Framework: In legal tempo-
ral GraphRAG, data leakage represents a major method-
ological failure mode. TERRA implements four strict
safeguards:
1) Temporal Directionality and Precedent Inversion
Protection: In legal precedent networks, an earlier
case cannot cite future jurisprudence. Directed EEG
edges enforce chronological integrity: forward CITES
edges strictly require t(vcite) ≥ t(vcited). Retrospec-
tive invalidations are evaluated via authoritative
OVERRULES relations imported from CRS annota-
tions (Section III-A) rather than unrestricted back-
ward edges, preventing the retriever from anachro-
nistically projecting modern legal standards onto
historical inquiries.
2) Vector Store Contamination Isolation: Benchmark
test queries, evaluation rubrics, and reference an-
swers are strictly segregated from the retrieval index.
ChromaDB contains exclusively authentic opinion
text and procedural reasoning traces; zero evaluation
queries or answers exist in the vector database.
3) Cross-Domain Feature Bleed Prevention: Out-of-
domain concepts are prevented from contaminating
generated answers through a defense-in-depth hier-
archy: title matching and scale-dependent geometric
boundary gating provide initial coarse filtering, while
downstream NLI entailment verification strictly sup-
presses non-entailed retrieved subgraphs before an-
swer synthesis.
4) Temporal Parametric Containment Firewall: While
modern foundation models encode extensive post-
training parametric knowledge, historical legal rea-
soning requires strict fidelity to jurisprudence as of
a specific temporal horizon T . To prevent temporal
parametric leakage—wherein the generator inadver-
tently incorporates subsequent abrogations, modern
statutory standards, or post- T precedents into histor-
ical evaluations—TERRA implements a dual-layer
containment firewall. First, graph traversals and
retrieved opinion corpora are strictly bounded by
provenance timestamps (KnowledgeProvenance ≤
T ), ensuring that retrieved evidence contains no
anachronistic materials. Second, candidate gener-
ations ˆycandidate are evaluated by an entailment-
based NLI gate against contemporaneous opinion
evidence prior to output release. As demonstrated
in Section VI-G, this mechanism intercepts anachro-
nistic doctrine and ungrounded hallucinations, re-
ducing operative temporal leakage while preserving
historical correctness without requiring costly model
retraining or fine-tuning.

To validate the structural and topological integrity of

A. Corpus Statistics and Doctrinal Taxonomy
The indexed legal knowledge base contains 20,000 au-
thentic substantive judicial opinions connected by 145,366
directed citation edges across 1850–1980 (Volumes 50–
448), partitioned across seven doctrinal categories. To
structure graph traversal and domain-routed retrieval,
each opinion is assigned to exactly one mutually exclusive
primary doctrinal domain via an automated deterministic
rule-based taxonomy comprising an exact 69-phrase legal
lexicon across six substantive domains: Substantive Pri-
vate Law (10 phrases, 5,707 opinions, 28.54%), Administra-
tive Law and Federal Courts (11 phrases, 4,501 opinions,
22.50%), Criminal Procedure (13 phrases, 1,765 opinions,
8.82%), Commerce Clause and Economic Regulation (12
phrases, 1,614 opinions, 8.07%), Civil Rights and Equal
Protection (12 phrases, 1,153 opinions, 5.76%), and First
Amendment (11 phrases, 666 opinions, 3.33%).
The seventh domain, General Constitutional and Statu-
tory (4,594 opinions, 22.97%), functions as an unkeyed
residual fallback category assigned strictly when an opin-
ion contains zero substantive keyword hits across all six
explicit lexicons. Positive-score ties between substantive
domains are resolved deterministically by documented
dictionary insertion order; ties never assign the resid-
ual fallback category. In addition to mutually exclu-
sive primary domain partitioning, opinions independently
support secondary multi-label doctrinal tags capturing
multidimensional jurisprudence.
To audit taxonomy quality without circularity or man-
ual inflation, we evaluated a stratified sample of N = 210
opinions (140 baseline stratified cases and 70 edge cases:
20 ties, 30 multi-tag cases, and 20 residual fallback cases;
random seed 20260925). Validation was conducted using
two independent automated evaluators: Judge A (a deter-
ministic statutory/constitutional parser scanning opinion
texts and oﬀicial headnotes) and Judge B (databricks-
gpt-oss-120b with logged fallback to databricks-qwen3-
next-80b-a3b-instruct; zero LLaMA calls). Inter-evaluator
agreement yielded raw agreement of 44.29% (Cohen’s
κ = 0 .3395). Disagreements and edge cases were ad-
judicated by a third independent automated adjudica-
tor, Judge C (databricks-gemma-3-12b), evaluated strictly
blinded to the assigned taxonomy and prior evaluator
votes, with strictly zero endpoint overlap with Judge B
(0/210). We explicitly disclose that this protocol repre-
sents automated model adjudication and parser evaluation,
not human legal-expert validation. Against this indepen-
dent model-derived reference adjudication standard, the
primary-domain taxonomy achieves a Horvitz-Thompson
prevalence-weighted accuracy of 34.15% (SE: 4.53%, 95%
CI: [25.27%, 43.02%]). Across the complete 210-case audit,
the taxonomy exhibits 33.33% strict coherence (70/210),
36.67% partial alignment (77/210), yielding an overall sub-
stantively grounded rate of 70.00% (147/210), with 30.00%
incoherent classifications (63/210). In edge-case strata, tie

V. Experimental Setup
To rigorously assess TERRA’s performance in legal
temporal reasoning and safety firewalling, we establish
a comprehensive comparative benchmarking suite. This
section outlines the benchmark query suite design, baseline
architectures, and evaluation metrics.
A. Benchmark Suite Design
The evaluation suite comprises an expanded benchmark
of 350 unique queries evaluated across 5 comparative
pipelines (1,750 total evaluations; see Table V for execu-
tion dates and model identifiers), alongside a foundational
N = 35 unique-query pilot ablation set (5 repeated
stability trials per query, 700 total executions across four
configurations) structured across four distinct categories
designed to isolate factual accuracy, multi-hop reasoning,
domain safety, and adversarial robustness. Temperature
T =0.0 was used throughout, but does not guarantee iden-
tical outputs across runs (see Section VII-C and Table V).
Quantitative paired text generation metrics (Faithfulness
and ROUGE-L) are evaluated across in-domain queries
(Categories A and B) that produce generative text outputs,
while safety firewall performance is evaluated across out-
of-domain and deceptive queries (Categories C and D):
• Category A — Single-Point Factual: Single-fact re-
trieval queries (e.g., decision years, specific party

names) evaluated against researcher-constructed
ground-truth reference answers using ROUGE-L.
• Category B — Multi-Hop Evolutionary: Complex le-
gal queries requiring multi-decade precedent tracking
across citation chains, evaluated against researcher-
constructed reference answers using ROUGE-L. To
test non-trivial multi-hop graph traversal while re-
flecting the small-world hub topology of judicial cita-

three-node, 32 four-node, and 5 five-node evolutionary
arcs with zero 2-node direct citation pairs.
• Category C — Out-of-Context Safety: Queries cov-

distinct non-indexed domains ( N = 15 each, total

SCOTUS precedent exists for these inquiries, the
ground-truth system requirement is strict safety re-
fusal.
• Category D — Adversarial Deception: Queries that

legal premises or temporal falsehoods (e.g., asserting
that the 1905 Lochner ruling was governed by the
1938 Fair Labor Standards Act, or asking for Section

Cases), designed to trick entity-matching routers.
The acceptable safe response is either explicit safety
refusal or verified substantive legal refutation that
actively exposes and refutes the false premise citing

Task Scope vs. Background Corpus Scope Partitioning.
To evaluate multi-step legal temporal reasoning without
artificial domain simplifications, we establish a deliberate
structural distinction between the broad background re-
trieval corpus and the evaluated benchmark task scope.
While the pre-built EEG encompasses the full 20,000-case
Supreme Court docket across all seven substantive areas
(including 5,707 Substantive Private Law decisions and
4,594 General Statutory decisions; Table II), the multi-hop
evolutionary reasoning benchmark (Category B) intention-

public-law precedent trajectories (20 queries each across
Civil Rights, Criminal Procedure, First Amendment, Com-

all 275 in-domain queries (Categories A, B, and D), con-
stitutional and public-law questions comprise 232 queries
(84.4%), while Substantive Private Law comprises 23
(8.4%) and General Statutory comprises 20 (7.3%). This
partitioning reflects our task-design rationale: private-law
disputes (e.g., patent claim construction, maritime salvage,
diversity contract disputes) were not modeled as 3–5
node multi-decade citation chains under our Category B
construction criteria, whereas constitutional jurisprudence
exhibits longitudinal precedent evolution and explicit
doctrinal overruling. Retaining the complete 20,000-case
repository provides a realistic, high-distractor topological
environment where constitutional reasoning must navigate
past 51.5% commercial and statutory background nodes

the directed chronological graph via BFS ( k = 2 , depth
d = 2, hub cap 50, dL2 ≤ 1.3). All context ceilings in code
are character-based string operations ([:N]): Flat RAG
(≈3,200 chars), GraphRAG ( ≈5,500 chars), HippoRAG
(≈3,500 chars), whereas TERRA enforces a 60,000-char
ceiling, representing an intentional 18.8× context volume
asymmetry (17.3× prompt tokens, Table XVI) required for
multi-decade precedent chains. The critic judge applies a
50,000-char middle-omission split ([:25000] + [-25000:]),
evaluating 100% of baseline context ( < 6,000 chars)
without truncation while preserving TERRA’s initial seeds
and terminal citing authorities. Finally, baseline adapters
incorporate post-hoc amendments rather than frozen pre-
registration: Amendments F and G (HippoRAG regex
and GraphRAG formatting fixes) and Amendments K,
L, and M (judge JSON parse clamping, refusal string
harmonization, and fast-path ground-truth floor).
C. Evaluation Metrics
System performance is evaluated across five quantitative
dimensions:
• Faithfulness ( 0.0–1.0): Evaluated via an LLM-as-a-
judge framework [ 23] using databricks-meta-llama-3-
3-70b-instruct operating with an adversarial “skep-
tical critic” persona. The judge model is explicitly
decoupled from the inference models to eliminate
circular self-evaluation bias.
• Relevance ( 0.0–1.0): Scored by the LLM judge to
measure query-answer directness and structural com-
pleteness.
• ROUGE-L F1 and Recall (Supplementary): Com-
puted for Categories A and B against researcher-
constructed ground-truth reference answers via de-
terministic tokenized LCS matching, providing an
LLM-independent supplementary lexical alignment

PAGE 12
metric. Ground-truth doctrinal claims and reference
holdings were cross-referenced against standard pub-
lic legal reference sources (Cornell LII, Oyez, CRS
Constitution Annotated) without independent legal
certification. ROUGE-L is strictly supplementary:
exact n-gram matching cannot distinguish active
doctrine from overruled precedent, heavily penalizes
substantive paraphrasing and detailed analyses via
candidate length, and scores defensive refusals as zero.
The modest judge–ROUGE correlation ( r = 0 .555;
Section V-D) confirms that lexical matching captures
only a fraction of legal semantic correctness; primary
conclusions are grounded in dual-judge Faithfulness,
citation graph recovery, and adversarial safety rates.
• Safety Rejection and Multi-Class Robustness Rate
(%): Percentage of out-of-domain (Category C) and
adversarial deception (Category D) queries correctly
neutralized. For Category D, metrics are formally
decoupled into: Explicit Safety Refusal Rate ( Rref,
declining to aﬀirm the invalid prompt), Verified Sub-
stantive Refutation Rate ( Rrefut, actively refuting the
false premise without fatal error), Total Safe Handling
Rate ( Rsafe = Rref ∪ Rrefut), Fatal Error Rate ( Rfatal,
credulously validating false or overruled doctrine),
and Uncommitted Hedge Rate ( Rhedge, equivocal non-
refusal non-answers).
• Stage Latency (ms): Mean ± standard deviation
execution time for routing, retrieval, NLI grading,
and generation stages, recorded across all benchmark
runs.

G. Temporal Parametric Leakage Stress Test
While retrospective legal retrieval requires evaluating
questions strictly under historical jurisprudence as of
temporal cutoff T , modern generative language models
possess extensive pretraining corpora containing post- T
legal history. Consequently, models risk emitting tem-
poral parametric leakage—anachronistically importing fu-
ture doctrinal shifts, statutory overhauls, or later over-
rulings into historical analyses. To rigorously quantify
and stress-test this vulnerability, we preregistered and
executed a dedicated 50-query temporal leakage probe
across five historical Supreme Court horizons ( T ∈
{1890, 1930, 1950, 1970, 1980}), spanning seven major doc-
trinal domains and balanced across case prominence and
multi-hop reasoning depths. Six probes (12%) targeting
historical cases decided between 1857 and 1905 (evaluated
at cutoffs T = 1890 and T = 1930 ) have target nodes
with zero out-edges in the EEG due to the corpus citation-
parsing boundary. Arc evolution cases for these entries are
historically verified against primary legal sources but are
not EEG graph edges. All leakage metrics are computed
identically across all 50 probes regardless of arc status.
1) Experimental Design and Metric Decoupling: The
probe evaluates 400 responses generated across four com-
parative architectures: Direct LLM, Flat Vector RAG,
TERRAungated (Lgen), and TERRA gated (Lfinal). To sep-
arate baseline capabilities from prompt compliance, each
architecture is tested under two distinct regimes: Condi-
tion A (standard task prompt without explicit temporal
constraint, measuring default end-to-end containment)
and Condition B (explicit instruction to strictly adhere to
year T and ignore future precedents, measuring prompt-
assisted containment). All candidate generations were
evaluated under a preregistered 3-tier severity rubric:
Level 0 (clean adherence to pre- T doctrine), Level 1 (non-
prejudicial historical mention or temporal disclaimer),
and Level 2 (fatal substantive reliance on post- T law).
The primary metric, Substantive Post- T Leakage Rate
(SPTLR), captures the proportion of Level 2 fatal failures.
Critically, evaluating temporal containment solely via
SPTLR creates a severe vulnerability to metric distortion:
systems that simply refuse to answer generate zero post-
T text, appearing perfectly “leak-free” despite failing
the underlying legal task. To eliminate this confound,
we decouple containment from legal problem-solving by
measuring: (1) Refusal Rate, isolating epistemic or safety
rejections; and (2) Historical Legal Correctness Rate
(HLCR), assessing whether the system correctly articu-
lates the operative contemporaneous rule as of year T .
Finally, we measure the NLI Gate Leakage Interception
Rate (NGLIR), defined as the proportion of ungated
candidate leaks intercepted by the entailment gate on the
conditioning denominator of leaking candidates ( D).
2) Evaluator Reliability, Concordance, and Consensus
Protocol: Responses were independently evaluated by two
distinct model-based judges: qwen3-next-80b and gpt-oss-
120b. Across N = 315 complete pairs, the evaluators
achieved 68.6% raw concordance and 100% monotonic
inclusion (every leak identified by GPT-OSS was a strict
subset of Qwen’s flags). However, the quadratic weighted
Cohen’s κ was 0.260, appearing considerably lower than
the benchmark-wide agreement ( κ ≈ 0.70). Forensic
diagnosis confirms that this divergence is an artifact of
the Kappa Paradox [ 25]: GPT-OSS exhibited a severe
91.7% base-rate skew toward Level 0 ( 289/315 clean),
which mechanically inflated expected chance agreement
Pe to ≈ 60%, deflating κ despite high raw agreement.
Furthermore, qualitative analysis revealed an evaluator
threshold asymmetry: Qwen operated as a hyper-vigilant
lexical scanner (flagging any passing historical reference as
Level 1 or Level 2), whereas GPT-OSS evaluated holistic
legal operability (classifying responses as clean if post- T
references did not alter the substantive legal outcome).
To guarantee objective reproducibility, all evaluations
were reconciled via a pre-specified, deterministic 5-tier
consensus protocol: (1) safe refusals automatically re-
solve to Level 0; (2) unanimous agreement is preserved;
(3) lexical breach of forbidden historical phrases assigns
Level 2; (4) substantive quotation of post- T law assigns
Level 2; and (5) subtle mentions resolve conservatively
to max(s1, s2) = Level 1. Crucially, tiers 3–5 bias strictly
toward flagging leakage, ensuring that consensus adjudi-
cation is conservative against TERRA.

audited failure distribution across queries and domains.

K. Direct Evidence Retrieval Quality and Precedent-
Chain Coverage
A critical question in retrieval-augmented legal archi-
tectures is whether downstream generative advantages
stem from superior evidence retrieval or merely reflect the
generator’s ability to synthesize plausible analyses from
average context. Furthermore, Second Review Issue #11
noted that while TERRA’s canonical graph expansion
operates with breadth k = 2 and depth d = 2, Category B
queries comprise 3-to-5 node evolutionary arcs (spanning
2 to 4 citation hops), requiring explicit evaluation of
retrieval success as a function of precedent chain length.
The core judicial benchmark encompasses N = 350
queries across four operational strata: Category A ( N =
100 single-point factual lookups), Category B ( N = 100
multi-hop evolutionary chains), Category C ( N = 75
out-of-context unanswerable refusal probes where target
authorities are absent from the corpus, evaluated for
gate interception in Section VI-C), and Category D
(N = 75 adversarial temporal overruling pairs). To rigor-
ously isolate evidence-retrieval quality on complex multi-
hop common-law reasoning without conflating single-point
lookups (which TERRA intentionally routes through its
parametric Fast Path) or unanswerable negative probes
(where no target nodes exist in the corpus), we evaluate
raw evidence retrieval on the N = 100 Category B multi-
hop trajectories ( 342 gold landmark precedents) prior to
generator invocation.
Because comparative architectures deliver heteroge-
neous retrieval representations—Flat Vector RAG returns
a linear ranked list of document chunks, GraphRAG
delivers unranked modularity community reports, Hip-
poRAG yields unranked Personalized PageRank (PPR)
activation clusters, and TERRA produces a 2-hop directed
EEG subgraph structured by temporal edges and holding
headnotes—linear ranked-list metrics such as MRR or
nDCG cannot be applied uniformly across community
and subgraph representations without imposing arbitrary
ranking assumptions. We therefore evaluate deterministic
dual-resolution entity recall (exact CAP Node ID and

Seed Anchoring: The ability of TERRA’s nominal d = 2
graph traversal to achieve complete path recovery on 3-hop
and 4-hop lineages is governed by multi-seed anchoring.
For an evolutionary query q, entity and legal concept
extraction identifies a set of seed nodes Sq ⊂ V with
|Sq| ≥ 2 (empirical mean of 2.41 seeds per query across
Category B; 73.0% with |Sq| = 2 , 19.0% with |Sq| = 3 ,
and 8.0% with |Sq| ≥ 4). From each seed s ∈ Sq, directed
EEG traversal expands a d = 2 graph ball B(s, 2) along
forward and backward citation edges with breadth k = 2.
Crucially, empirical auditing of seed placements con-
firms that multi-seed extraction anchors the extremities
of the precedent trajectory: in 100.0% of 5-node chains
(5/5) and 100.0% of recovered 4-node chains (20/20),
seeds anchor both the historical origin (index 0) and
the culminating modern authority (index m − 1). For
an evolutionary chain v0 → · · · → vm−1, the forward
2-hop ball B(v0, 2) covers {v0, v1, v2} while the reverse
2-hop ball B(vm−1, 2) covers {vm−1, vm−2, vm−3}. When
intermediate branching along the precedent spine remains
within the k = 2 beam, the forward and backward
neighborhoods intersect at intermediate stepping-stone
authorities ( v2 in 5-node chains), seamlessly covering 3-
hop and 4-hop doctrinal paths without requiring explosive
unconstrained search depths ( d ≥ 4). Conversely, in the
12 non-recovered 4-node cases, high citation fan-out at
intermediate nodes exceeded the k = 2 beam width,
causing the traversal to prune intermediate nodes despite
correct endpoint seeding.

shifts grand pooled recall from 0.35 ± 0.13 to 0.38 ± 0.14
(∆Recall = 0.03 ± 0.16), with 90.6% of queries exhibiting
non-negative recall shift ( ∆Recall ≥ 0). On newly eligible
authorities ( Snew), traversal attains a grand mean Dis-
covery Rate of 0.12, with median traversal latency under
one millisecond ( < 0.01 ms; grand mean 0.15 ± 0.18 ms).
Three methodological boundaries govern these findings:
(1) Because gold sequences include seed anchor s (retrieved
at depth 0), absolute recall reflects baseline seed inclusion;
∆Recall is invariant to this offset. (2) Negative shifts on
specific queries stem from degree caps when expanding
nodes become hubs. (3) This standalone experiment eval-
uates topological traversal over available directed edges
(generic CITES) on NetworkX DiGraphs; it is decoupled
from upstream vector retrieval (subject to the category
filter limitation in Section VII-C) and does not evaluate
live vector index upsert, which remains future systems
engineering.
VII. Discussion
The empirical evaluation validates that TERRA ad-
dresses fundamental failure modes of standard RAG ar-
chitectures in knowledge-intensive, dynamic environments.
This section contextualizes our primary architectural con-
tributions, candidly examines current system limitations,
and outlines future research directions.
A. Architectural Differentiation and System Scope
Compared to Microsoft Research’s GraphRAG frame-
work [ 3]—which constructs static hierarchical entity-
relation communities for dataset summarization—our ar-
chitecture directly addresses temporal precedent evolu-
tion and knowledge drift. While Microsoft GraphRAG
requires substantial offline LLM summarization costs and
remains temporally blind to overruled holdings, our dual-
index Event Evolution Graph (EEG) cross-links chrono-
logical citation edges (CITES) with authoritative typed
OVERRULES edges imported from CRS annotations (56
total, from which the 19 Category D invalidation pairs
are drawn; see Section III-A) and node-level procedural
reasoning traces. Furthermore, our NLI Smart Grader
is designed to evaluate semantic entailment and reduce
unsupported generations, although residual errors remain
[26], [ 27]. We formally delineate TERRA’s architectural
scope: the offline ingestion pipeline indexes full-docket
Supreme Court jurisprudence (1850–1980) as a broad,
high-distractor knowledge graph, while the online evalu-
ated task focuses specifically on longitudinal precedent
tracking, temporal drift mitigation, and safety firewalling
over constitutional and federal public-law doctrine.
Three core design innovations drive TERRA’s perfor-
mance:
1) Multi-Tier Domain Boundary Safety: Rather than
relying on static allowlists, TERRA couples lexical
anchoring with downstream NLI entailment gating
and scale-dependent geometric filtering.
2) Entailment-Gated Generation and XAI Attribution:
By replacing keyword heuristics with an NLI Smart
Grader, TERRA operationalizes a “prefer silence

PAGE 33
over hallucination” principle with transparent rea-
soning paths, qualified by our 94.5% citation ex-
traction precision on the underlying precedent graph
(Section III-A).
3) Citation Graphs as Temporal Reasoning Structures:
By structuring opinions as chronological citation
edges (CITES) alongside authoritative typed OVER-
RULES relations within an Event Evolution Graph
(Section III-A), TERRA explicitly models chrono-
logical precedent shifts.
B. Empirical Analysis of Firewall Component Contribu-
tions and Scale Dynamics
A critical finding from our systematic post-hoc auditing
concerns the empirical division of labor within TERRA’s
safety architecture. The original design conceptualized the
Domain Boundary Firewall as an active two-stage pre-
retrieval filter where vector distance ( dL2 < 1.3) flags
out-of-domain (OOD) queries before graph traversal. How-
ever, systematic auditing across the preliminary 50-query
Category C audit set on the initial 10,009-opinion index
revealed that the vector proximity gate was non-functional
at this scale ( 0/50 intercepted) because unconstrained
OOD distances ( 0.281 ≤ dL2 ≤ 0.859) fell entirely beneath
1.3.
Consequently, safety performance in Table IX (100.0%
refusal on Category C, 64.0% refusal and 33.3% verified
refutation on Category D) was delivered almost entirely
by the downstream NLI Smart Grader (Tier 3) via double-
failure refusal. When OOD queries entered retrieval, the
resulting lack of semantic entailment triggered a safe
refusal. Removing NLI in ablation (Table VI, Ablation
2) caused an immediate collapse of adversarial safety to
0.0%, confirming NLI as the true empirical safeguard.
Crucially, empirical calibration on the expanded 20,000-
opinion substantive corpus demonstrates that geometric
pre-filtering exhibits scale- and density-dependent behav-
ior. As reported in Table XXI (Panel B), evaluating the full
N = 75 Category C benchmark suite against the 20k index
yields an isolated AUROC of 0.9901. At the operational
threshold τ = 1.30, geometric pre-filtering achieves 0.00%
in-domain false refusal ( 0/200) while intercepting 54.67%
(41/75) of out-of-domain probes pre-retrieval. Comparing
the 10k index (where 50/50 probes fell below 1.3) to the
20k index (where 41/75 probes exceed 1.3) reflects an em-
pirical observation of corpus manifold density: expanding
corpus volume across seven verified constitutional domains
creates denser semantic clustering, pushing extraneous
out-of-domain queries farther into the embedding margin.
We do not assert any generalized theoretical scaling law
from these two empirical corpus snapshots; rather, we
treat geometric proximity strictly as an optional coarse
pre-filter that saves retrieval and NLI compute on obvious
outliers, while downstream NLI entailment verification
serves as the indispensable primary safety firewall.
C. Limitations
To ensure complete scientific transparency and maintain
the highest standard of academic integrity, we identify
eighteen methodological limitations of the current archi-
tecture:
1) Absence of Licensed Legal Practitioner Evalua-
tion (Most Critical Limitation for Deployment):
Due to geographical and institutional resource
constraints, this study did not include evalua-
tion by licensed legal practitioners. All faith-
fulness and safety assessments rely on auto-
mated LLM-as-a-judge scoring (Section V-C) and
continuous lexical metrics (ROUGE-L) evalu-
ated against researcher-constructed reference an-
swers cross-referenced against public legal reference
sources (Cornell LII, Oyez, CRS Constitution Anno-
tated). While the adversarial critic persona exhibits
a modest, statistically significant correlation with
reference lexical grounding (Pearson r = 0 .555, p =
1
10001 ≈ 9.999 × 10−5), automated scoring cannot
replicate practitioner judgment on subtle constitu-
tional doctrine. Specifically, practitioners would as-
sess legal correctness, temporal consistency, citation
validity, and hallucination behavior beyond contin-
uous metrics. Furthermore, lacking an independent
labeled validation set for the NLI Smart Grader (Sec-
tion III-B3), the false-non-entailment rate remains
empirically unquantified beyond ablation evidence.
We consider human expert evaluation of both legal
output quality and NLI verifier reliability essential
prerequisites for production deployment, and release
the complete 350-query benchmark suite and code-
base to invite independent expert replication. The
author spot-check described in Section VI-H is a
single non-expert review of a stratified sample and
does not substitute for licensed practitioner review.
2) Metric Disentanglement and Refusal-Volume Mask-
ing: Evaluating complex RAG pipelines solely via ag-
gregate faithfulness induces severe metric distortion:
systems with high epistemic refusal volume receive
top marks for non-committal answers on unan-
swerable queries, masking generative weaknesses.
Under our dual-judge protocol, TERRA has the
highest overall ( 0.695 vs. 0.670; intervals overlap)
and answer-only faithfulness ( 0.586 vs. 0.392) with
fewer flagged refusals ( 139 vs. 165), but answer-only
scores are conditional on different answered subsets.
On multi-hop reasoning TERRA leads HippoRAG
by +0.066 (q = 0.0124). Future benchmark protocols
must mandate disentangled reporting of refusal rates
alongside answer-only faithfulness.
3) Evaluation Rubric Resolution and Likert Normaliza-
tion: Coarse evaluation scales (e.g., discrete binary
pass/fail or {0.0, 0.8, 1.0} heuristics) induce artificial
ceiling compression, clustering over 75% of responses

PAGE 34
at 1.0. Implementing a 5-point Likert scale normal-
ized to [0, 1] effectively resolves this compression,
exposing genuine variances in doctrinal nuance.
4) Jurisdictional and Temporal Corpus Boundaries:
The pre-built Event Evolution Graph is anchored
in 20,000 authentic substantive U.S. Supreme Court
opinions spanning 1850–1980, encompassing seven
doctrinal domains of federal jurisprudence (Table II).
Extending the architecture to state appellate court
dockets, municipal statutory codes, foreign legal
systems, and modern post-1980 jurisprudence re-
quires ongoing bulk ingestion. Furthermore, while
Section VI-N empirically demonstrates graph traver-
sal stability across temporal snapshot expansions
(T → T + N ), live vector index insertion and
streaming graph maintenance remain unaddressed
engineering extensions.
5) Hosted API Output Variability at T = 0 .0, Run-
to-Run Identity, and Sensitivity Verification: Pri-
mary evaluations were conducted under greedy de-
coding ( T = 0 .0) using databricks-meta-llama-3-
3-70b-instruct and databricks-gemma-3-12b. While
setting T = 0 .0 minimizes sampling variance, re-
mote hosted inference APIs can exhibit token-level
or classification variability across serving sessions.
Across benchmark evaluations, query D066 exhibited
an empirical classification shift (EASY → HARD),
consistent with numerical or serving-level variability
in hosted inference; the precise backend cause cannot
be isolated from available logs. Comparing two 14-
September TERRA executions across the 350-query
benchmark suite, exact string-identical generated an-
swers occurred on 58.9% of queries overall (206/350),
with pronounced category divergence: 95.0% on Cat-
egory A (95/100) and 82.7% on Category C (62/75),
versus 52.0% on Category D (39/75) and 10.0% on
Category B (10/100). The two runs share the verified
judge-side evaluation protocol and benchmark query
universe; their generation/retrieval paths differ as
documented. Both runs were evaluated under the
standardized dual-judge protocol (databricks-qwen3-
next-80b-a3b-instruct and databricks-gpt-oss-120b)
using the frozen RAG prompt template (SHA256:
6d18fc14…) and normalized Likert rubric. Sensitivity
verification on an 80B model (databricks-qwen3-
next-80b-a3b-instruct) across a separate 15-query
subset (10 Cat B, 5 Cat D; distinct from the firewall
stress test in Section V-A) confirmed invariant rel-
ative rankings: TERRA achieved 1.000 Category B
Faithfulness (vs. 0.900 Flat RAG, 0.800 Direct LLM)
and 100% Category D safety rejection (vs. 40% Flat
RAG, 0% Direct LLM). To preserve reproducibility
of the reported results, all analyses are bound to im-
mutable, SHA256-verified raw completion archives;
rerunning the hosted endpoint may yield different
completions.
6) Parametric Pretraining Confounders, Latent
Weights, and Temporal Containment Bounds:
On continuous evaluation of Category D
(Adversarial Deception), Direct LLM recorded
a higher continuous Faithfulness score ( 0.850
vs. 0.650), yielding a non-significant Wilcoxon
signed-rank comparison in Direct LLM’s favor
(∆ = −0.083, p = 0 .0629, Table XXIII, Rank
11). Because landmark constitutional overrulings
are heavily represented in web-scale pretraining
corpora, raw weights often refute famous traps with
fluent parametric prose without retrieval. However,
evaluating discrete outcome safety via the formal
rubric (Section V-A, Table XI) reveals that Direct
LLM achieves its 94.7% safe handling entirely
through ungrounded refutations ( 71/75) while
operating with zero epistemic restraint ( 0% refusal
across all 350 benchmark queries) and collapsing
completely on out-of-domain probes ( 0/75 refusal on
Category C). In contrast, TERRA achieves 97.3%
total safe handling ( 73/75: 48/75(64.0%) explicit
refusals and 25/75(33.3%) verified refutations),
delivering a +64.0% explicit refusal advantage
(p = 4 .55 × 10−13), perfect monotonic containment
(c = 0 ), and an 80.0% reduction in fatal constraint
violations (from 10/75 to 2/75(2.7%)) with zero
uncommitted hedges ( 0/75). While the pre-specified
hypothesis test in Table XXIII (Rank 11) evaluates
continuous faithfulness, TERRA’s true structural
advantage lies in verifiable citation provenance,
epistemic restraint, and temporal boundary
integrity. Forensic auditing reveals that the two
remaining fatal errors (queries D033 and D034)
are reciprocal misattributions between coupled
apportionment landmarks (Baker v. Carr [1962]
and Reynolds v. Sims [1964]), where the generator
transposed justiciability with the substantive
standard. Adaptive edge-weighting to prioritize
invalidation paths constitutes essential future
work. Similarly, on Category B (Evolutionary
Precedents), Direct LLM achieves higher judge-
scored faithfulness ( 0.335 vs. 0.254, ∆ = −0.081,
q = 0 .0023), consistent with base model fluency
on generic historical narratives, whereas TERRA’s
retrieval evaluation shows stronger Category B
citation coverage and full-precedent-path recovery
(57.85% macro citation recall vs. 43.55% for Direct
LLM; Section VI-L).
More fundamentally, we distinguish latent paramet-
ric knowledge from operative doctrinal leakage. Be-
cause modern foundation models are pretrained on
web-scale text, weights encode post- T legal history
(e.g., Plessy overruled by Brown in 1954). TERRA
does not purge latent weights; its provenance-
bounded retrieval and temporal gate prevent latent
memory from intruding into operative pre- T reason-

PAGE 35
ing. While forensic auditing (Section VI-G) confirms
suppression of Level-2 leakage in TERRA gated, fu-
ture work should explore circuit editing and machine
unlearning for weight-level guarantees. Provider-side
safety filtering on historical legal topics (terminating
85 evaluations in Section VI-G) highlights the oper-
ational dependency of LLM-as-a-judge pipelines on
third-party API policies, motivating our sensitivity-
bounded framework. Section VI-H tests this con-
founder directly: on obscure overruled precedents
Direct LLM safe handling was 0.0% (15/19 (78.9% )
on the famous subset), consistent with famous-
landmark performance partly reflecting parametric
memory.
7) Fixed Graph Traversal Horizon and Multi-Hop
Boundary Limits: TERRA’s online retrieval operates
with a calibrated 2-hop BFS graph expansion radius
to bound latency and token overhead. While 2-
hop traversal captures direct citing and invalidating
authorities across most evolutionary arcs, it can
miss subsequent modifying precedents when citation
chains exceed two steps. An empirical boundary
emerged during our CaseFacts audit on Chimel
v. California (query CF_INC_058; Section VI-I),
where the modifying precedent (United States v.
Robinson [1973]) sat beyond the 2-hop boundary;
the generator cited Chimel’s 1969 wingspan doctrine
but found no modifying precedent in context. This
multi-hop horizon likewise accounts for the gold-
precedent omission rate during drafting on CLERC
(50.0%/37.5%; Section VI-J), where distant author-
ities prompted analogical reasoning from historical
cases rather than fabrication. Developing adaptive
traversal horizons along invalidation paths repre-
sents a high-priority architectural extension.
8) Oracle Relation Labels, Exact-Title Seeding, and a
Bundled Ablation: In Full TERRA, curated OVER-
RULES edges are serialized with an explicit relation
tag, dedicated heading, expanded excerpt budget,
and prompt instruction; obscure benchmark pairs
were drawn from these edges. The Full-TERRA
advantage on that suite is therefore contingent on cu-
rated annotations, and the bundled ablation cannot
isolate the tag alone. For named-case queries, seeding
relied on exact caption matches (category-filtered
vector retrieval returned no candidates), causing two
queries to produce empty context and safe-rated
refusals, one to omit the overruling case, and two to
take the fast path; we evaluated TERRA as deployed
and report sensitivity subsets excluding these items.
The primary comparison contrasts complete systems,
and Direct LLM receives no instruction block. Out-
comes are judge-dependent in magnitude, the suite
has thirty items, and judges were not fully blind to
arm identity.
9) False Refusals on Answerable Queries (Categories
A and B): The safety gate produces false re-
fusals on non-adversarial inquiries: 2/100 Cate-
gory A (Factual) and 16/100 Category B (Evolu-
tionary) queries receive system-level safety rejections
(safety_rejected = True). All 16 Category B false
refusals originate from the NLI Smart Grader on the
hard route; none occur on the fast route. Individual
causes were not separately diagnosed.
10) Latency Measurement Caveats: Three factors limit
direct latency comparisons. (1) Execution mode:
Baselines ran sequentially on 5–6 September 2026;
TERRA ran with four concurrent workers on
14 September 2026. (2) HTTP 429 throttling: 36/350
TERRA queries incurred rate-limiting back-off accu-
mulating 620 s of sleep; reported medians are gross
with net values alongside. (3) Bimodal routing: The
fast route contains 110 queries (median 5.46 s); the
hard route contains 240 queries (median 14.07 s).
Aggregate medians obscure this bimodal structure;
baseline and TERRA comparisons are indicative
only.
11) Same-Family Generation, NLI Grading, and Cat-
egory D Verifier: Generation, NLI grading, and
the Category D verifier all deploy databricks-meta-
llama-3-3-70b-instruct (same family and endpoint).
Faithfulness judges are databricks-qwen3-next-80b-
a3b-instruct and databricks-gpt-oss-120b (indepen-
dent families). Category D four-class labels (refusal,
refutation, fatal error, hedge) may inherit shared
Llama biases; cross-family verifier ablation has not
been run.
12) Unflagged Textual Refusals: System-level flagging
(safety_rejected = True) undercounts epistemic re-
fusals. Adding textual refusals (asserting contextual
absence without gate triggering) raises counts to
143/350 for TERRA, 204/350 for Flat Vector RAG,
203/350 for GraphRAG-Adapted, and 179/350 for
HippoRAG-Adapted. Unflagged TERRA cases are
D034 (refusal phrasing with fatal error, score 0.000),
D061 (verified refutation, floored at 0.750 under
Amendment M), and D068/D070 (fast-path textual
refusals scoring 1.000). Aggregate Table IX sen-
sitivity results for TERRA are invariant to this
distinction ( ∆ = 0 .000).
13) Mixed-Date Evaluation Blocks: Not all blocks were
re-evaluated under v2 dual-judge protocols; Table V
details dates and backends. Blocks lacking times-
tamps (pilot ablation, temporal leakage probe, Case-
Facts, CLERC) carry “date not recorded”; scores
derive from earlier single-judge evaluation and are
not numerically comparable to v2 evaluations.
14) Corpus Length-Filtering Bias and Concise Land-
mark Exclusion: Filtering by length ( ≥ 4,000
characters) purges noise: across 399 volumes
(131,315 cases), 102,235 records are < 500 char-
acters (77.85%, procedural orders/dockets). How-

PAGE 36
ever, equating length with authority induces bias:
it excludes 8,324 decisions ( 500–3,999 characters,
6.34%), with 46 in the top 0.5% of CAP PageRank
authority. Deterministic audit of 63 landmarks re-
veals 21 (33.33%) were purged, including procedural
standards (Haines, 3,730 chars, 100.00th percentile;
Diebold, 2,441 chars, 100.00th percentile), compe-
tency (Dusky, 1,532 chars, 99.99th percentile), tax
milestones (Lucas v. Earl, 3,654 chars), and desegre-
gation orders (Sipuel, 2,012 chars; Gayle, 712 chars).
Bolling v. Sharpe (5,137 chars) met the threshold but
was omitted in subsampling. Future graphs should
replace static floors with citation retention.
15) Citation Extraction Precision, Same-Year Chronol-
ogy, and Provenance Uncertainty: Automated ci-
tation extraction via eyecite establishes citation
existence rather than doctrinal endorsement. Our
stratified audit ( N = 200 , seed 20260924) confirms
100.0% of sampled edges contain reporter strings;
direct precedential reliance is verified in 94.5% (95%
CI: [90.4%, 96.9%]), with 5.5% in quotations. Fur-
thermore, 4,077 edges (2.81%) connect same-year
opinions, with 13 intra-year date reversals from
publication artifacts and zero cross-year forward
violations. The legacy claim of “ 99.91% chronological
validity / 0.09% noise” in earlier working drafts was
untraceable to repository artifacts, mandating this
audit. Citation trails provide candidate authorities
with 94.5% precision rather than certified doctrinal
paths.
16) Rule-Based Doctrinal Taxonomy, Lexical Polysemy,
Truncation, and Residual Fallback Bias: The seven-
domain classification is generated by a determin-
istic 69-phrase rule-based lexicon over truncated
texts ( ≤ 4, 000 characters), inducing four limi-
tations: (1) Lexical polysemy: Keyword matching
without contextual disambiguation risks misclassi-
fication when terms appear in procedural dicta or
dissenting quotes. (2) Truncation artifacts: Trun-
cating texts to 4,000 characters restricts matching
to introductory syllabi, occasionally missing dispos-
itive holdings. (3) Residual fallback bias: The gen-
eral_constitutional_statutory category acts as an
unkeyed fallback for 4,594 decisions (22.97%) lacking
substantive keywords, functioning as a catch-all with
10.0% baseline precision despite 100.00% validity as
a residual class. (4) Absence of human expert annota-
tion: Taxonomy validation relies on dual automated
evaluators (IEA 44.29%, Cohen’s κ = 0 .3395) and
a blinded automated adjudicator (34.15% weighted
accuracy, 95% CI: [25.27%, 43.02%]), not licensed
practitioner annotation; classifications are not certi-
fied human ground truth.
17) A Priori Hyperparameter Selection and Sensitivity
Analysis Scope: Core retrieval and graph expansion
parameters ( k = 2 , depth d = 2 , hub cap 50,
geometric threshold τ = 1 .30) were fixed prior to
benchmark construction and evaluation based on
topological network constraints and preliminary fea-
sibility testing on an independent 35-query develop-
ment suite (August 2026). While this frozen protocol
prevents the audited forms of test-set leakage and
hyperparameter overfitting, it precludes claiming
global parameter optimality. Empirical sensitivity
sweeps across 22 configurations (Section VI-K, Ta-
ble XIX Panel C) profile performance across horizons
(k ∈ { 1, 2, 4}, d ∈ { 1, 2, 3}, C ∈ { 25, 50, 100}),
confirming empirical stability around the historical
configuration ( k = 2 , d = 2 , C = 50 , 86.08% macro
recall, 62.0% full path recall) while quantifying
the coverage-eﬀiciency trade-off under bounded op-
erational caps (90.85% landmark retention, 97.0%
context reduction).
18) Task-Corpus Domain Asymmetry and Private-
Law Generalizability Scope: A core methodological
boundary is the intentional asymmetry between cor-
pus composition and benchmark task scope. While
the indexed repository reflects the complete 20,000-
case docket spanning seven domains (51.5% private
and general statutory law), the evaluated multi-
hop reasoning benchmark (Category B) evaluates
constitutional and public-law precedent exclusively
(100.0% across five public-law domains; zero private-
law chains). While the evaluation demonstrates
TERRA’s measured performance on the evaluated
constitutional citation chains across a dense back-
ground network containing 5,707 private-law distrac-
tors, the system’s performance on complex multi-
hop statutory or patent reasoning remains unvali-
dated. Multi-hop evolutionary retrieval over complex
commercial codes and specialized regulatory dockets
constitutes an important open frontier for future
domain-specific evaluation.
D. Future Work
Future research will: (1) conduct licensed practitioner
evaluation across the 350-query benchmark; (2) scale
corpus ingestion to the full federal repository; and (3) cod-
ify OVERRULES annotations as explicit weighted graph
edges with adaptive invalidation-path traversal.
VIII. Conclusion
This paper introduced TERRA, an experimental dual-
phase GraphRAG architecture addressing knowledge drift
and multi-step legal reasoning on historical SCOTUS ju-
risprudence (1850–1980). Structuring 20,000 authentic de-
cisions (145,366 citation edges) with procedural reasoning
traces, TERRA enables inference via Traﬀic Cop routing,
BFS traversal, and an NLI grader evaluating retrieved
evidence. Benchmarking across 350 queries (1,750 evalu-
ations) shows higher Category B multi-hop faithfulness
than Flat Vector RAG, HippoRAG, and GraphRAG ( ∆

PAGE 37
from +0.066 to +0.119; q < 0.05), though not over Direct
LLM (−0.081); higher overall faithfulness ( 0.695 vs. 0.670,
overlapping CIs; 0.586); and 97.3% adversarial safe han-
dling ( 48/75(64.0%) refusals, 25/75 (33.3%) refutations).
Constraints include 2/75 (2.7%) fatal errors, false refusals
on 2 Category A and 16 Category B queries, and reliance
on curated overruling annotations (Section VI-H). Criti-
cally, because evaluations rely on automated LLM judges
without licensed attorneys (Section VII-C), human expert
review remains an essential prerequisite for deployment.
Citation graphs grounded with procedural traces preserve
longitudinal stability (Section VI-N) [ 3], [ 4], [ 27].
