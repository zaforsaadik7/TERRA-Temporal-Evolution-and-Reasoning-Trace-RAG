from 100.0% to 0.0%, which is consistent with the gate
contributing to safety but not a validation of its accu-

and false entailment (insuﬀicient context accepted) are
unquantified failure modes (Section VII-C). If entails ==
True, the context goes to the generator. If entails ==
False, a Self-Correction Expansion Loop widens retrieval
(k ←k + 2), re-queries ChromaDB and re-runs BFS.
The recovered HARD path permits two attempts in total
(initial retrieval plus one fallback), not two retries; EASY
has no expansion retry. Historical logs do not record
intermediate retry transitions. If the permitted checks fail,
the Safety Refusal Firewall halts generation with a deter-
ministic refusal: “I apologize, but I do not have suﬀicient
validated legal context in my databases to answer this
query accurately without risking hallucination.”
4) Data Leakage Mitigation Framework: TERRA ap-
plies four safeguards against leakage. (1) Temporal direc-
tionality: an earlier case cannot cite later jurisprudence,
so forward CITES edges require t(vcite) ≥t(vcited), and
retrospective invalidation uses CRS-derived OVERRULES
relations (Section III-A) rather than unrestricted back-
ward edges, providing a temporal constraint without
guaranteeing historically correct answers. (2) Vector-store
isolation: benchmark queries, rubrics and reference an-
swers are segregated from the index, which holds only
opinion text and generic procedural scaffolds. (3) Cross-
domain bleed prevention: title matching and the prox-
imity flag (which terminated no query in the canonical
run) are coarse first-line signals, and NLI verification
is designed to suppress non-entailed subgraphs before
synthesis. (4) Temporal parametric containment: to limit
post-T doctrine entering historical answers, traversals and
retrieved corpora are bounded by provenance timestamps
(KnowledgeProvenance ≤T), and candidate generations
ˆycandidate pass an entailment gate against contemporane-
ous evidence before release. In the leakage probe (Sec-
tion VI-H) the gate made six interventions, but its bulk
leakage reduction was not statistically established.

IV. Dataset and Corpus Analysis
We analyze the pre-built dual-index corpus for compo-
sition, topology and centrality.
A. Corpus Statistics and Doctrinal Taxonomy
The knowledge base holds 20,000 substantive opinions
connected by 145,366 directed citation edges across 1850–
1980 (Volumes 50–448) in seven doctrinal categories
(Table I). Each opinion receives exactly one primary
domain from a deterministic rule-based taxonomy with
an exact 69-phrase lexicon over six substantive domains:
Substantive Private Law (10 phrases, 5,707 opinions,
28.54%), Administrative Law and Federal Courts (11,
4,501, 22.50%), Criminal Procedure (13, 1,765, 8.82%),
Commerce Clause and Economic Regulation (12, 1,614,
8.07%), Civil Rights and Equal Protection (12, 1,153,
5.76%) and First Amendment (11, 666, 3.33%). The
seventh domain, General Constitutional and Statutory
(4,594 opinions, 22.97%), is an unkeyed residual fallback
assigned only when an opinion has no keyword hit in any
lexicon; positive-score ties resolve by dictionary insertion
order and never to the fallback. Opinions also carry
secondary multi-label doctrinal tags.
