J. External
Benchmark
Generalization
and
Anti-
Circularity Audit (CaseFacts)
Evaluating only on researcher-constructed queries risks
circular alignment or benchmark overfitting, so we also
evaluate on CaseFacts [21], a legal fact-checking dataset
built from U.S. Supreme Court decisions by an external
team.
1) Anti-Circularity and Graph Substrate Audit:
Of
the 20,000 nodes, exactly 34 landmark decisions (0.17%,
34/20, 000) received curated reasoning traces during early
development (not in the evaluated index), whereas the
350-query benchmark touches 284 distinct nodes (1.42%,
284/20, 000; 100 in Category A, 151 in B, 0 in C, 58 in D,
with 25 nodes of multi-category overlap), leaving 98.58%
of the substrate (19, 716/20, 000 nodes) unvisited. The 19
Category-D queries map to 9 distinct edges among the 56
tagged overrulings (list from CRS R45319 via a Wikipedia
mirror; 53 audited as consistent, 3 mis-resolved and unused
by Category D); this shows they are recognised overrulings,
not unfitted selection. Category B chains span 3 to 5 nodes
(2 to 4 hops): 63 have 3 nodes, 32 have 4 and 5 have 5, and
Full TERRA’s full-path recovery falls with chain length
(77.8%, 50.0% and 20.0%), so performance is not invariant
across lengths.
2) Splits, Protocol and Findings: We use two splits:
Track 1 (N = 120; 67 Supported, 45 Refuted and 8
Overruled claims anchored in pre-1980 decisions, 1850 ≤
T ≤1980) and Track 2 (N = 50; 50 modern decisions
from 1982–2023, e.g., Citizens United v. FEC and United
States v. Windsor) as an out-of-corpus refusal control.
All 510 evaluations (360 Track 1, 150 Track 2 across
Direct LLM, Flat Vector RAG and Full TERRA) used
strict payload parity (T = 0.0, max tokens 4096, identical
LLaMA 3.3 70B generator) and two non-LLaMA judges
(qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct, gpt-oss-120b); the judges
agreed on 100.0% of classification labels parsed from each
completion’s header (not an independent legal judgement),
with zero LLaMA fallbacks.
3) Empirical Findings: Track 1 (Table VIII, Panel A)
is mixed. Full TERRA’s three-class Macro-F1 is 54.48%,
above Flat Vector RAG (48.26%; +6.22 pp) and Direct
LLM (46.51%; +7.97 pp), but the gain comes entirely from
the Overruled class (n = 8): over Supported and Refuted
alone the macro is 53.16% for TERRA against 55.72% for
Flat and 51.59% for Direct, TERRA’s Supported F1 is
the lowest of the three, and its Refuted F1 is below Flat’s.
Accuracy, counting out-of-corpus predictions as errors,
is lowest for TERRA (62/120 against 64/120 for each
baseline); the NLI gate converted 25 borderline in-corpus
queries into out-of-corpus refusals when retrieved context
was legally incomplete. As a diagnostic with exact Clopper-
Pearson 95% intervals, TERRA identifies 4/8(50.0%)
Overruled cases (95% CI: [15.70%, 84.30%]), double Flat
Vector RAG (2/8(25.0%), [3.19%, 65.09%]) and Direct
LLM (2/8(25.0%), [3.19%, 65.09%]); a post-hoc exact two-
sided McNemar test on the eight Overruled items gives
p = 0.625 against each baseline (3 TERRA-only versus 1
baseline-only correct classifications). This small diagnostic
does not establish equivalence. Of the 8 pairs, 2 (Wolf
→Mapp, Olmstead →Katz) have direct OVERRULES
edges in the EEG and 6 rest on in-graph citation paths.
None of the 4 non-recalled cases involved fabrication: 2
were intercepted by the gate (Plessy, Schenck), 1 was
a defensible label discordance (Cicenia v. Lagay), and 1
was a retrieval-depth limit (Chimel v. California, whose
overruling authority United States v. Robinson lay beyond
the 2-hop boundary; Section VII-C).
Track 2 (Panel B) shows full out-of-corpus discrimina-
tion. TERRA classifies 50/50(100.0%) of modern cases
as out-of-corpus, against Flat Vector RAG (46/50(92.0%))
and Direct LLM (40/50(80.0%)). Of the 50 queries,
46/50(92.0%) were short-circuited by the NLI gate and
4/50(8.0%) were disclosed pre-1980 analyses ending in
OUTSIDE PRECEDENT CORPUS. Examination of all
50 completions found 0/50(0.0%) hallucination and no
phantom citations, whereas Direct LLM hallucinated
substantive answers on 10/50(20.0%) and Flat RAG on
4/50(8.0%).
