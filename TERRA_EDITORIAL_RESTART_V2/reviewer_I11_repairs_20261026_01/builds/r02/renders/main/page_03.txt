TERRA combines temporal precedent management,
generic procedural scaffolds and LLM-prompted evidence
checking in one experimental pipeline. Its node-level scaf-
folds repeat a fixed five-step template, rather than provid-
ing case-specific legal reasoning. The evaluation tests the
combined system and its trade-offs; it does not isolate the
scaffolds’ effect or establish individual-component novelty.
A binary Traﬀic Cop router selects anchor retrieval or
citation-graph traversal without retraining the foundation
model.
III. System Architecture and Methodology
TERRA
is
designed
as
a
two-phase,
closed-loop
GraphRAG architecture for temporal legal reasoning on
historical common-law jurisprudence. The core engineer-
ing philosophy strictly separates offline corpus ingestion
and knowledge structure construction from online query
routing and inference. Citation extraction, procedural-
scaffold construction and indexing are performed offline;
retrieval, HARD-route graph traversal, entailment grad-
ing and generation are online operations. The evaluated
Category A and Category B queries have different latency
profiles (median 5.83 s on factual lookups vs. 17.94 s on
multi-hop citation chains; Figure 1).
A. Phase 1: Offline Ingestion and Knowledge Graph Con-
struction
1) Authentic Judicial Opinion Corpus (20,000 Cases):
TERRA operates on 20,000 full-text Supreme Court opin-
ions spanning 1850–1980 (Volumes 50–448), curated from
an initial harvest of 23,010 public-domain federal decisions
from CourtListener and the Harvard Caselaw repositories
(public domain under 17 U.S.C. § 105). To remove
non-precedential noise, opinions were filtered by length
(character count ≥4,000). Of the 131,315 decisions in
399 scanned U.S. Reports volumes, 102,235 (77.85%) are
shorter than 500 characters (procedural orders, summary
dispositions and docket records, subject to audit). The
length filter nevertheless induces bias: it excludes 8,324
intermediate decisions (500–3,999 characters, 6.34%), of
which 46 rank in the top 0.5% of citation authority
under Harvard CAP PageRank (e.g., Haines v. Kerner,
Dusky v. United States, Lucas v. Earl, Sipuel v. Board
of Regents, Gayle v. Browder). The corpus contains no
synthetic cases. A core set of 34 landmark civil rights
decisions, from Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857) through
Plessy v. Ferguson (1896), Missouri ex rel. Gaines v.
Canada (1938) and Brown v. Board of Education (1954)
to Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg (1971), anchors the
multi-hop benchmark.
2) Citation Parsing and Directed Edge Classification:
Citations are extracted with eyecite [22], a token-level
extraction and reporter-normalization utility that estab-
lishes citation existence (A
CITES
−−−−→B) without inferring
precedential treatment or approval; strings are normalized
via cite.corrected_citation() (e.g., “347 U.S. 483”) and
matched to opinion identifier hashes. The verified Event
Evolution Graph has 145,366 directed edges (145,310
CITES and 56 OVERRULES), with forward chronological
validity at calendar-year granularity and zero cross-year
violations (0.00%); 4,077 edges (2.81%) link same-year
opinions, including 13 intra-year reporting artifacts. An
earlier “99.91% chronological validity” claim has unre-
solved provenance (likely a decimal approximation of
full-date ordering, 145, 175/145, 310 = 99.91%). A repro-
ducible stratified audit across 14 decades (N = 200, seed
20260924) confirms verbatim citation presence in 100.0%
of sampled edges (95% CI: [98.1%, 100.0%]), with 94.5%
verified direct reliance (95% CI: [90.4%, 96.9%]) and 5.5%
citations inside third-party quotations.
The OVERRULES edges are typed relations taken from
a Wikipedia mirror of the Congressional Research Service
(CRS) report R45319 and resolved to corpus nodes by cita-
tion and name matching (A
OVERRULES
−−−−−−−−−→B: B overruled
in whole or in part by A). We audited all 56 tagged edges
against the overruling opinions: 3 (Edelman→Wyman-
Gordon, Griﬀin→Frisbie, Hensley→Norwood) were mis-
resolved by the first-party-name fallback when the in-
tended target was absent from the 20k corpus, and the
other 53 are consistent in case identity, although the
scope of each overruling (full or partial) was not legally
adjudicated. None of the 3 was serialised in any of the
32 deployed contexts that contained an OVERRULES
line, but indirect effects on retrieval or ranking cannot
be excluded: replaying the current traversal code on both
graphs gave identical outputs for 159 recorded traversals,
but the code version used in the runs was not pinned.
Historical results retain their recorded index attribution;
a corrected derivative without the 3 edges (145,363
edges; SHA256 14827bd6...) is retained as a separately
versioned local derivative and was not substituted into
the historical runs; the prospective comparison in Sec-
tion VI-C uses this corrected graph. Public release of the
corrected derivative remains pending. The 19 Category-D
queries (e.g., Brown invalidating Plessy) cover 9 distinct
OVERRULES edges, all among the 53 consistent ones
(Section V-A). The graph is serialized as a NetworkX
DiGraph (dataset_20k_cap/terra_eeg_index_20k.json).
