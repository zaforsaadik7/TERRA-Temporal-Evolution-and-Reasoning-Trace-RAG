To test whether the architecture generalizes beyond
researcher-crafted queries, we evaluate TERRA on a strat-
ified sample of CLERC (Findings of NAACL 2025) [16], a
legal retrieval and drafting benchmark built by Johns Hop-
kins University and collaborators. Our validation thus has
four layers: the internal controlled benchmark (N = 350;
Sections VI-D–VI-A), CLERC (this section), CaseFacts
(Section VI-J), and licensed-practitioner evaluation, which
is not done (Limitation 1, Section VII-C).
1) Benchmark Protocol and Provenance Audit: Track 1
(N = 120) is a stratified sample of 120 pre-1980 drafting
contexts from the 1,498 candidate instances in the CLERC
test split, drawn before observing any model output with a
fixed seed (SEED=42), balanced between 60 Direct Prece-
dent Citation and 60 Indirect Synthesis queries across
five eras (1850–1880: 11.7%; 1881–1910: 20.0%; 1911–1940:
26.7%; 1941–1960: 21.7%; 1961–1980: 20.0%). Track 2
(N = 50) is not a native CLERC task but a TERRA-
specific refusal stress test of 50 negative controls (39 post-
1980 Supreme Court decisions, 11 non-SCOTUS federal
circuit or statutory queries). An automated provenance
audit found 0/120 Track 1 targets among the 34 hand-
curated seed nodes (overlapping queries were purged), so
evaluation is not aided by the bespoke traces; it does not
preclude retrieval over ordinary opinions in the shared
20,000-opinion corpus. The broader holding-extraction
cascade (Tier 1 syllabi and Tier 2 headnotes, 51.1% of
opinions) was not separately screened. The harness also
asserts, before each batch, that the NLI grader short-
circuits three known-refusal probes. All systems used strict
payload parity (T
= 0.0, max tokens 4096, identical
LLaMA 3.3 70B generator) and the two non-LLaMA
judges (qwen3-next-80b-a3b-instruct, gpt-oss-120b); cita-
tion precision and recall come from deterministic eyecite
parsing.
2) Empirical Findings: Track 1 (Table IX, Panel A) is
mixed. In retrieval (Task 1A), Full TERRA has slightly
higher Recall@10 (10.8% vs. 10.0%) and nDCG@10 (0.069
vs. 0.066) than Flat Vector RAG, a one-query difference in
hits@10 (13/120 vs. 12/120; MRR@10 0.056 vs. 0.055), so
the margin is modest (+0.8 points). In drafting (Task 1B),
TERRA attains 6.50 ROUGE-L and 1.10 BLEU-4 and the
highest Citation Precision (CP = 5.5% vs. 2.9% for Flat
and 4.5% for Direct), with Citation Recall 2.6% (Flat 1.4%,
Direct 4.9%) and Citation F1 3.5%[5.5%/2.6%]. Direct
LLM has the highest Citation F1 (4.7%, against TERRA’s
3.5% and Flat’s 1.8%; micro-pooled over items; TERRA’s
pooled precision omits its 53 gate-refused items, which
carry no citations, while its recall still counts them), and
TERRA’s judged soundness is the lowest of the three (1.98
against 2.34 and 2.84).
We recomputed a citation audit from the raw Track 1
completions. Among case captions extracted by pattern
from judge-flagged completions, the share matching no
corpus title is 15.3% for TERRA (43/281), 15.0% for
Flat (88/585) and 41.1% for Direct LLM (147/358); the
remaining TERRA captions resolve to in-corpus decisions
(84.7%), and gate-intercepted TERRA completions carry
no citations (0/53). This is a corpus-membership check,
not an existence check: the unmatched captions include
real Supreme Court decisions outside the 20k subset as
well as extraction fragments, so we do not report a fabrica-
tion rate, and an earlier recorded value of 0.0% fabrication
for TERRA and Flat, which this recomputation does
not reproduce, is withdrawn. Dual judging nevertheless
flagged doctrinal misapplication in 50.0%/37.5% of all
120 completions (50.0% under OR, 37.5% under AND;
Flat 98.3%/86.7%, Direct 90.0%/64.2%, neither gated).
All of TERRA’s flags fall among the 67 completions gen-
erated on incomplete retrieval subgraphs (89.6%(60/67)
OR, 67.2%(45/67) AND), where incomplete retrieval is
a possible explanation rather than a demonstrated cause
of the generated misapplications: authentic evidence does
not equate to suﬀicient evidence, which motivates the path-
recovery analysis in Section VI-L.
