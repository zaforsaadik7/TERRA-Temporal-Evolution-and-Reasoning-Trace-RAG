14. Corpus Length-Filtering Bias and Concise Landmark Exclusion: Filtering by length (≥4,000 characters) purges noise: across 399 volumes (131,315 cases),
102,235 records are < 500 characters (77.85%, procedural orders/dockets). However, equating length with authority induces bias: it excludes 8,324 decisions (500–3,999
characters, 6.34%), with 46 in the top 0.5% of CAP PageRank authority. Deterministic audit of 63 landmarks reveals 21 (33.33%) were purged, including procedural
standards (Haines, 3,730 chars, 100.00th percentile; Diebold, 2,441 chars, 100.00th percentile), competency (Dusky, 1,532 chars, 99.99th percentile), tax milestones (Lucas
v. Earl, 3,654 chars), and desegregation orders (Sipuel, 2,012 chars; Gayle, 712 chars). Bolling v. Sharpe (5,137 chars) met the threshold but was omitted in subsampling.
Future graphs should replace static floors with citation retention.
15. Citation Extraction Precision, Same-Year Chronology, and Provenance Uncertainty: Automated citation extraction via eyecite establishes citation existence
rather than doctrinal endorsement. Our stratified audit (N = 200, seed 20260924) confirms 100.0% of sampled edges contain reporter strings; direct precedential reliance
is verified in 94.5% (95% CI: [90.4%, 96.9%]), with 5.5% in quotations. Furthermore, 4,077 edges (2.81%) connect same-year opinions, with 13 intra-year date reversals
from publication artifacts and zero cross-year forward violations.
The legacy claim of “99.91% chronological validity / 0.09% noise” in earlier working drafts was
untraceable to repository artifacts, mandating this audit. Citation trails provide candidate authorities with 94.5% precision rather than certified doctrinal paths.
16. Rule-Based Doctrinal Taxonomy, Lexical Polysemy, Truncation, and Residual Fallback Bias: The seven-domain classification is generated by a determin-
istic 69-phrase rule-based lexicon over truncated texts (≤4, 000 characters), inducing four limitations: (1) Lexical polysemy: Keyword matching without contextual
disambiguation risks misclassification when terms appear in procedural dicta or dissenting quotes. (2) Truncation artifacts: Truncating texts to 4,000 characters restricts
matching to introductory syllabi, occasionally missing dispositive holdings. (3) Residual fallback bias: The general_constitutional_statutory category acts as an
unkeyed fallback for 4,594 decisions (22.97%) lacking substantive keywords, functioning as a catch-all with 10.0% baseline precision despite 100.00% validity as a residual
class. (4) Absence of human expert annotation: Taxonomy validation relies on dual automated evaluators (IEA 44.29%, Cohen’s κ = 0.3395) and a blinded automated
adjudicator (34.15% weighted accuracy, 95% CI: [25.27%, 43.02%]), not licensed practitioner annotation; classifications are not certified human ground truth.
17. A Priori Hyperparameter Selection and Sensitivity Analysis Scope: Core retrieval and graph expansion parameters (k = 2, depth d = 2, hub cap 50, geometric
threshold τ = 1.30) were fixed prior to benchmark construction and evaluation based on topological network constraints and preliminary feasibility testing on an
independent 35-query development suite (August 2026). The development audit does not establish global optimality or exclude all contamination. Empirical sensitivity
sweeps across 22 configurations (Section VI-L, Table X Panel C) profile performance across horizons (k ∈{1, 2, 4}, d ∈{1, 2, 3}, C ∈{25, 50, 100}), describing the
older stored-context configuration, not exact reproduction of canonical-v2 (k = 2, d = 2, C = 50, 86.08% macro recall, 62.0% full path recall) while quantifying the
coverage-eﬀiciency trade-off under bounded operational caps (90.85% landmark retention, 97.0% context reduction). Canonical-v2 recovers 66/100 complete targets,
older stored contexts 62/100 and the separate bounded engine 61/100; these are distinct records and configurations.
18. Task-Corpus Domain Asymmetry and Private-Law Generalizability Scope: A core methodological boundary is the intentional asymmetry between corpus
composition and benchmark task scope. While the indexed repository reflects the complete 20,000-case docket spanning seven domains (51.5% private and general
statutory law), the evaluated multi-hop reasoning benchmark (Category B) evaluates constitutional and public-law precedent exclusively (100.0% across five public-
law domains; zero private-law chains). While the evaluation demonstrates TERRA’s measured performance on the evaluated constitutional citation chains across a
dense background network containing 5,707 private-law distractors, the system’s performance on complex multi-hop statutory or patent reasoning remains unvalidated.
Multi-hop evolutionary retrieval over complex commercial codes and specialized regulatory dockets constitutes an important open frontier for future domain-specific
evaluation.
